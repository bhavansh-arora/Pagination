Most photographers' blogs go quiet after three posts because "blogging" feels like a chore with no clear payoff. The fix is to treat every post as a small landing page aimed at a specific search or a specific doubt in a couple's mind.

Here are forty ideas, grouped by what they do for you.

2. A rainy-day wedding that still looked incredible
3. A small or micro wedding
4. An elopement
5. A winter wedding
6. A multi-day or cultural celebration
7. A wedding with an unusual timeline (brunch, sunset ceremony)
8. Your favourite wedding of the year, and why

## How to write each post

- **Title:** include the venue or the question, the way couples would type it
- **Intro:** two or three sentences that set the scene
- **Body:** 300 to 800 words. Google can't read photos; it needs text.
- **Images:** well compressed, descriptive file names and alt text ([image SEO](/blog/image-seo/))
- **Links:** to the venue, vendors and your related posts
- **Call to action:** "Planning a wedding at [venue]? Check your date."

More on structure in [how to write a blog post that ranks](/blog/how-to-write-a-blog-post-that-ranks/).

## A realistic schedule

| Month type | Post |
| --- | --- |
| Busy season | One real wedding |
| Quiet season | One venue guide and one planning article |

Twelve to eighteen good posts a year will outperform fifty rushed ones.

## Repurpose everything

Each post becomes a Pinterest board, three Instagram carousels, a reel and an email to past enquiries. Write once, use five times.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should a wedding blog post be?
At least 300 words alongside the images. Venue guides and planning articles do better at 800 to 1,200.

### Should I blog every wedding?
No. Blog the ones that show your style and target venues or locations you want more of.

### Do blogs still work when couples use Instagram?
Yes. Instagram is for discovery; Google is where couples research venues and vendors with intent to book.

=== photography-website-speed
title: How to Make Your Photography Website Load Fast (Without Losing Quality)
desc: Practical steps to speed up an image-heavy photography website: formats, sizing, lazy loading, galleries and video, with no visible loss in quality.
cat: photographers
kw: photography website speed, optimize images for web photographers, fast photography website, image compression photographers
takeaways: Export at the size the image is displayed, not full resolution | WebP or AVIF cut file sizes dramatically with no visible difference | Lazy-load everything below the first screen | Load video players only when someone clicks play
---
Photographers face a real tension: the work has to look incredible, and incredible images are heavy. A slow site loses couples before they've seen a single frame, and Google ranks slow pages lower. The good news is that you can have both speed and quality. It comes down to how images are prepared and delivered.

## Why speed matters more for photographers

- Most couples arrive on a phone, often on mobile data
- Portfolio pages carry dozens of images
- Visitors compare several photographers in one sitting; the slow tab gets closed
- Google's Core Web Vitals reward fast-loading pages ([explained here](/blog/core-web-vitals-explained/))

## Step 1: export at display size

A camera file might be 8,000 pixels wide. Your website shows it at 1,600 at most. Uploading the original forces every visitor to download four or five times more than they can see.

**Guidelines:**

| Use | Width to export |
| --- | --- |
| Full-width hero | 2,000 to 2,400 px |
| Gallery image | 1,400 to 1,800 px |
| Grid thumbnail | 600 to 800 px |

## Step 2: use modern formats

**WebP** is supported by every modern browser and is typically 25 to 35% smaller than JPEG at the same visual quality. **AVIF** is smaller still. Export JPEGs from Lightroom at around 80 quality, then convert to WebP at 75 to 82 with a tool like Squoosh, ShortPixel or your site's built-in optimiser.

At normal viewing sizes, nobody can tell the difference. You'll see it in your load times.

## Step 3: serve the right size to each device

A phone doesn't need the desktop version. Responsive images (`srcset`) let the browser choose the smallest file that still looks sharp on that screen. A good custom build or a quality platform handles this automatically; if yours doesn't, it's worth fixing.

## Step 4: lazy-load below the fold

Only the first image or two should load immediately. Everything else should load as the visitor scrolls towards it. This single change can turn a ten-second page into a two-second one.

## Step 5: reserve space for images

If images pop in and shove the text around, the page feels janky and Google counts it against you (it's called layout shift). Setting width and height on every image reserves the space before it loads.

## Step 6: tame your galleries

- **Paginate or "load more"** for galleries beyond 40 to 60 images
- **Avoid heavy slider plugins** that load every slide up front
- **Use a lightbox** that loads the large version only on click

## Step 7: handle video carefully

- Compress background loops hard and keep them short
- Always set a poster image
- For Vimeo and YouTube, show a thumbnail and load the player on click

More in [wedding videographer websites](/blog/wedding-videographer-website/).

[[audit]]

## Step 8: trim everything else

- **Fonts:** two families at most, self-hosted or preloaded
- **Plugins and widgets:** each one adds weight; remove those you don't need
- **Third-party scripts:** chat widgets, pixels and Instagram feeds are common culprits
- **Hosting:** a good host with a CDN delivers images from a server near the visitor

## How to test

1. Open [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/) and test your homepage and a gallery page on mobile.
2. Look at **Largest Contentful Paint**; aim for under 2.5 seconds.
3. Check the "properly size images" and "next-gen formats" suggestions.
4. Test on a real phone on mobile data, not only on office Wi-Fi.

## What we do on photography builds

On sites like the ones below, every image is exported in multiple sizes as WebP, lazy-loaded with reserved space, and the hero is prioritised so it appears first. That's how a gallery-rich homepage still loads quickly.

[[work:dan-reynolds-photography,capturedbykatherine]]

## Frequently asked questions

### Will compressing images make them look worse?
Not at sensible settings. At WebP quality 75 to 82 and display size, the difference is invisible to visitors.

### What file size should I aim for?
Under 300 KB for large images and under 100 KB for thumbnails is a good target.

### Is my platform the problem?
Sometimes. If you've optimised images and the site is still slow, the template or plugins may be the bottleneck. We're happy to take a look.

=== wedding-inquiry-form
title: The Perfect Wedding Inquiry Form: Questions That Qualify Couples
desc: What to ask on a wedding photography or videography inquiry form to get more, better enquiries, with a ready-to-use field list and follow-up tips.
cat: photographers
kw: wedding inquiry form, photography contact form, wedding photographer enquiry form, contact form questions photographers
takeaways: Ask for the date and venue first; they tell you most of what you need | Keep required fields to six or fewer | One open question lets couples show their personality | Reply within a few hours; speed wins bookings
---
Your inquiry form is the bridge between a couple loving your work and you getting the chance to book them. Ask too little and you're emailing back and forth for days. Ask too much and they abandon it halfway.

Here's the balance that works.

## The essential fields

1. **Names** (both partners)
2. **Email**
3. **Wedding date** (with a "we don't have one yet" option)
4. **Venue or location**
5. **What they're looking for** (photo, film or both)
6. **Tell me about your day** (open text)

That's enough to check availability, understand the scale and start a real conversation.

## Useful optional fields

- **Phone number.** Optional; some couples prefer a text.
- **Estimated guest count.** Helps you judge coverage.
- **How did you find me?** Tells you which marketing works.
- **Budget range.** Divisive. If you show starting prices on your site, you may not need it. If you don't, a range dropdown filters effectively.
- **Instagram handle.** A nice way to get to know them.

## Questions that start a conversation

Swap the dull "Message" box for a prompt:

- "Tell me about the two of you and what you're planning."
- "What matters most to you about your wedding photos?"
- "What's the part of the day you're most excited about?"

Couples who answer thoughtfully tend to be the ones who value photography. And you get something personal to respond to.

## Design details that lift completions

- **One column,** thumb-friendly on mobile
- **A date picker** rather than free text
- **Clear labels** above each field
- **A friendly button:** "Send my enquiry" or "Check my date" beats "Submit"
- **A note about response time:** "I reply within 24 hours"
- **A confirmation message** that tells them what happens next

More in [contact forms that convert](/blog/contact-form-best-practices/).

## Add an availability check

A simple "Check your date" field near the top of your site is a lovely hook. The couple gets an instant answer and you get the start of an enquiry. Captured by Katherine uses this to great effect.

[[work:capturedbykatherine,jackalbert]]

## Where the form should live

- A dedicated contact page
- A short version at the bottom of every portfolio and investment page
- A button in the header on every page

Don't make couples hunt for it after they've fallen for a gallery.

[[cta]]

## What happens after they submit

### Instantly

An automatic email confirming you received it, with a link to your pricing guide or a favourite gallery while they wait.

### Within a few hours

A personal reply. Mention something specific they wrote. Confirm availability. Suggest a call. Speed matters: couples often book the first photographer who replies warmly and promptly.

### If they go quiet

Follow up after three days and again after a week. Most photographers never follow up at all; those who do book more weddings.

## Tools

Your form can feed a studio management tool (HoneyBook, Dubsado, Studio Ninja, Sprout Studio) so enquiries, replies, contracts and invoices sit in one place. A good website integrates with whichever you use.

## Protect against spam

Use a hidden "honeypot" field or an invisible challenge. Avoid old-style puzzles that make humans prove themselves.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I ask for budget on the form?
If your site shows starting prices, it's optional. If it doesn't, a budget range saves time for you both.

### How long should my form be?
Six required fields at most. Everything else can be optional or asked later.

### Is it better to use a form or just list my email?
Offer both. Forms capture structured information; some couples simply prefer to email.

=== photographer-portfolio-curation
title: How Many Photos Should Be in Your Portfolio? A Curation Guide
desc: How wedding photographers should curate an online portfolio: how many images to show, how to choose and order them, and what to leave out.
cat: photographers
kw: photography portfolio curation, how many photos in portfolio, wedding photography portfolio, portfolio website tips
takeaways: Twenty to forty exceptional images beat hundreds of good ones | Show the work you want to be hired for | Include full wedding galleries so couples can see consistency | Open and close with your strongest frames
---
The hardest part of building a photography website isn't design. It's deciding what to leave out. Every image you love has a memory attached; couples only see whether it's great. A tight portfolio signals confidence. A sprawling one hides your best work among your average.

## How many images?

| Section | Recommended number |
| --- | --- |
| Homepage | 1 hero plus 10 to 20 highlights |
| Main portfolio | 30 to 50 |
| Each category (weddings, engagements) | 20 to 30 |
| Full real-wedding gallery | 40 to 80 |

If you're unsure about an image, the answer is no.

## Show what you want to shoot

Your portfolio is a filter. If you want elopements in the mountains, show those. If you fill it with ballroom receptions because that's what you've shot most, you'll book more ballrooms.

## A five-step curation process

1. **Gather** your 200 favourite images from the last two years.
2. **First cut:** remove anything technically weak or stylistically inconsistent. Aim for 100.
3. **Second cut:** remove duplicates of the same moment or pose. Aim for 60.
4. **Third cut:** ask "would I be thrilled to deliver this as the hero image?" Aim for 40.
5. **Get an outside eye.** Ask a photographer friend or a past client which ten they'd drop.

## Variety without chaos

A strong portfolio covers the range of a wedding day:

- Getting ready and details
- Emotional moments (the look, the tears, the laughter)
- Portraits of the couple
- Family and friends
- Ceremony and reception atmosphere
- The party

It should also show different light (bright sun, golden hour, indoor, night) and reflect the diversity of the couples you serve.

## Consistency

Editing style should be coherent across images shown together. If your look has changed, retire the old work, however much you love it.

## Order matters

- **Open with your best.** The first image decides whether anyone sees the second.
- **End strong.** People remember the last frame.
- **Alternate rhythm:** wide then close, quiet then energetic, colour then black and white.
- **Pair images** that complement each other side by side.

## Full galleries build trust

Highlights show your peak. Couples also want to know what an entire day looks like in your hands. Publish three to five full weddings (as blog posts or gallery pages) so they can see consistency from the first frame to the last. These posts double as SEO assets; see [venue pages](/blog/venue-pages-for-photographers/).

[[cta]]

## What to leave out

- Styled shoots presented as real weddings (label them honestly)
- Work that's more than three or four years old, unless it's iconic
- Images where the couple's faces are hidden in every frame
- Anything you're including only because the venue was impressive

## Keep it fast

Even the best curation fails if the page takes ten seconds to load. Export at display size, use WebP and lazy-load. See [photography website speed](/blog/photography-website-speed/).

## Refresh regularly

Review your portfolio at the end of each season. Swap in new favourites and remove the weakest five. A portfolio is never finished.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I have separate portfolios for weddings and engagements?
Yes, if you want to book both. Keep each one tight.

### Should I include black and white images?
If it's part of your style, yes. Mix them with intention rather than at random.

### How do I choose a hero image?
Pick the frame that best represents your style and makes people stop. Test two options and see which gets more enquiries.
