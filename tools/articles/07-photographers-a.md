---
A wedding photographer's website has one of the hardest jobs on the internet. It has to be beautiful enough to make a couple fall for your work, fast enough that they don't leave while it loads, and clear enough that they know how to hire you. Most manage one of the three.

We've built sites for wedding photographers and filmmakers in Chicago, Hawaii, Brisbane, Denver, Ottawa and Christchurch. Here's what consistently works.

## The first screen

When a couple lands on your homepage, they should see, without scrolling:

1. **One stunning image or short film clip.** Your single best frame, not a slideshow of twelve.
2. **Who you are and where you work.** "Chicago & Peoria wedding photographer."
3. **A line that captures your style.** "Moments you'll see again."
4. **A way to enquire.** "Check your date" or "Inquire".

That's it. Everything else comes after the scroll.

## The pages you need

### Portfolio

Organise by what couples want to see: weddings, engagements, and perhaps by style or season. Show full galleries from a few real weddings as well as highlights; couples want to know what a whole day looks like, not only the hero shots. See [how many photos should be in your portfolio](/blog/photographer-portfolio-curation/).

### About

Couples will spend their whole wedding day with you. They want to like you. Show your face, share your approach and let your personality come through. More in [writing an about page couples connect with](/blog/wedding-photographer-about-page/).

### Investment

Whether to show prices is the eternal debate. Our view: show at least a starting price. We explain why in [should you show prices on your wedding photography website?](/blog/wedding-photography-pricing-page/)

### Real weddings / journal

Individual blog posts for weddings you've shot. They show range, help couples picture their own day and are the backbone of your SEO, especially when you name the venue.

### Contact

A form that asks the right questions without feeling like homework. See [the perfect wedding inquiry form](/blog/wedding-inquiry-form/).

## Speed: the invisible dealbreaker

Photography sites are image-heavy by nature, and that's where most go wrong. A homepage with 40 full-resolution JPEGs can take ten seconds to load on mobile data. Couples won't wait.

The fix isn't fewer images; it's smarter delivery: modern formats (WebP or AVIF), images sized for the screen they're shown on, and lazy loading so pictures only download as they scroll into view. Done properly, a gallery-rich page can still load in under two seconds. Details in [how to make your photography website load fast](/blog/photography-website-speed/).

## Get found locally

Couples search for "[city] wedding photographer" and "[venue name] wedding photos". To appear:

- Put your location in your homepage title and headline
- Create a page for each area you serve
- Blog real weddings with the venue name in the title
- Set up and maintain a Google Business Profile
- Collect Google reviews

Our full guide: [SEO for wedding photographers](/blog/wedding-photographer-seo/).

## Proof

- **Reviews** from Google, The Knot or WeddingWire, shown on the site
- **Awards and features** ("ranked in the top 10 in Illinois")
- **Numbers** that reassure: years in business, weddings photographed
- **Kind words** next to matching images from that couple's day

## Make enquiring easy

Put an enquiry button in the header on every page. On mobile, make it sticky. An availability checker ("Check your date in seconds") is a lovely touch: it gives the couple instant gratification and starts the conversation.

[[cta]]

## Design principles for photography sites

- **Let the work breathe.** Neutral backgrounds, generous spacing.
- **Type with character.** An elegant serif or refined sans sets the mood without competing.
- **Consistent editing** across images shown together.
- **Subtle motion.** Gentle fades and reveals; nothing that fights the photos.
- **Mobile first.** The majority of couples will see your site on a phone, often from an Instagram link.

## Template or custom?

Squarespace, Showit and Pixieset make attractive sites and are fine when you're starting. Custom becomes worth it when you want to stand apart from the hundreds of photographers on the same templates, load faster, and rank better. We compare them in [Squarespace vs custom websites for photographers](/blog/squarespace-vs-custom-for-photographers/).

## Frequently asked questions

### How many images should be on my homepage?
Fewer than you think: one hero image and 10 to 20 carefully chosen frames further down is plenty.

### Should I include video on my homepage?
A short, silent, well-compressed clip can be powerful, especially if you also offer films. Make sure it doesn't slow the page.

### How often should I update my portfolio?
At least once a season. Replace older work with your latest best so the site reflects how you shoot today.

---
Instagram shows your work to people who already follow you. Google shows it to couples who are actively looking to book. For many wedding photographers, search is the single biggest source of enquiries, and it's work that keeps paying for years.

## How couples search

- **"[City] wedding photographer"** — high intent, high competition
- **"[Venue] wedding photographer"** or **"[venue] wedding photos"** — high intent, low competition
- **"[Style] wedding photographer [region]"** — documentary, elopement, Catholic, destination
- **Planning questions** — "best time for wedding photos", "how many hours of photography do I need"

A good SEO plan covers all four.

## Step 1: optimise your homepage for your main location

- **Title tag:** "Chicago & Peoria Wedding Photographer | Dan Reynolds Photography"
- **H1 heading:** include location and "wedding photographer" naturally
- **First paragraph:** say where you're based and where you travel
- **Footer:** location and contact details

See [title tags and meta descriptions](/blog/title-tags-meta-descriptions/) for how to write them.

## Step 2: location pages

If you serve several areas, give each a page: "Milwaukee Wedding Photographer", "Wisconsin Wedding Photographer". Each needs unique content: weddings you've shot there, favourite venues, local tips. No copy-and-paste. Our guide to [local landing pages](/blog/local-landing-pages/) shows how.

## Step 3: venue pages and real-wedding posts

This is the most underused strategy in wedding photography. When a couple books a venue, they immediately search for photos taken there. If your blog post or venue guide appears, you're talking to a couple with a confirmed date and location.

For each wedding you blog:

- Title: "[Venue Name] Wedding | [Couple's names]"
- 300+ words telling the story of the day and mentioning the venue, town and vendors
- 30 to 60 well-optimised images
- Links to the venue and other vendors
- A call to action for couples marrying there

We go deeper in [venue pages for wedding photographers](/blog/venue-pages-for-photographers/).

## Step 4: image SEO

Your images are your content, so make them readable to Google:

- **File names:** `kualoa-ranch-wedding-ceremony.webp`, not `IMG_4821.jpg`
- **Alt text:** a plain description: "Bride and groom walking through the gardens at Kualoa Ranch"
- **Compression:** WebP or AVIF, sized appropriately
- **Lazy loading** for everything below the first screen

More in our [image SEO guide](/blog/image-seo/).

## Step 5: Google Business Profile

Your profile decides whether you appear in the map results. Fill in every field, choose "Wedding photographer" as the primary category, add your service areas, upload fresh photos monthly and reply to every review. Full walkthrough: [Google Business Profile for wedding photographers](/blog/google-business-profile-photographers/).

## Step 6: reviews

Ask every couple for a Google review when you deliver their gallery; that's when they're happiest. Make it easy with a direct link. Reviews influence both rankings and bookings. See [how wedding vendors can get more reviews](/blog/reviews-for-wedding-vendors/).

[[audit]]

## Step 7: links from the wedding world

- Venue "preferred vendor" lists
- Features on wedding blogs and magazines
- Vendor friends linking to your blog posts about shared weddings
- Local directories and associations

When you publish a wedding, send the link to every vendor involved. Many will share and link to it.

## Step 8: technical health

A beautiful site that's slow or broken on mobile won't rank. Check:

- Mobile PageSpeed score
- HTTPS
- One H1 per page
- An XML sitemap submitted in Search Console
- No broken links

Our [technical SEO checklist](/blog/technical-seo-checklist/) lists everything.

## Helpful content couples search for

- "How many hours of wedding photography do I need?"
- "Wedding day timeline for the best photos"
- "Best wedding venues in [region]"
- "What to wear for engagement photos"

More in [40 blog post ideas for wedding photographers](/blog/wedding-photographer-blog-ideas/).

## How long does it take?

Venue posts can rank in weeks because competition is low. City-level terms usually take six to twelve months of consistent work. Start now; the photographers ranking today began years ago.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does Instagram help my Google ranking?
Not directly. It builds awareness, which leads to people searching your name, which helps.

### Should I blog every wedding?
Blog the ones at venues you'd love to shoot again and the ones that best show your style.

### Is Squarespace or Showit bad for SEO?
They cover the basics. Custom sites offer more control over speed and structure, which matters in competitive cities.

title: Wedding Videographer Website: Showcasing Films Without Slowing Down
desc: How to build a wedding videography website that puts your films front and centre, loads quickly, and converts couples into enquiries.
cat: photographers
kw: wedding videographer website, wedding film website, videography website design, wedding filmmaker website
takeaways: Lead with a short, silent, looping clip and let couples choose to press play | Host films on Vimeo or YouTube and load players only when clicked | Each film deserves a page with the story, the venue and stills | Explain your packages and deliverables clearly because couples don't know film jargon
---
Film is the most emotional medium a wedding vendor has, and the hardest to present online. Video files are heavy, autoplay is unreliable and a single badly embedded player can add seconds to your load time. Get it right, though, and nothing sells better.

## The hero: motion without the wait

The strongest opening for a filmmaker's site is a short, silent background loop: 8 to 15 seconds of your best moments, compressed hard, with a still poster image that shows instantly while it loads.

Rules:

- **Keep it under 3 to 5 MB.** 720p is plenty for a background.
- **Muted and looping.** Browsers block autoplay with sound.
- **Poster image first** so the page never looks empty.
- **A fallback image on slow connections.**

Then give couples a clear "Watch the film" button for the full piece.

[[work:aristafilms,jackalbert]]

## Embedding films the fast way

A page with six embedded Vimeo players loads six players' worth of scripts, even if nobody presses play. Instead:

1. Show a thumbnail with a play button.
2. Load the actual player only when clicked.

This "facade" technique can cut seconds off load time and is one of the biggest wins on film sites. See [website animations and performance](/blog/website-animations-performance/) for more on keeping rich sites fast.

**Where to host:** Vimeo gives clean, ad-free playback and privacy controls. YouTube adds discoverability. Many filmmakers use Vimeo on the site and YouTube for reach.

## Give each film a home

Don't just list embeds on one page. Give your best films their own pages with:

- The film
- A few paragraphs on the couple and the day
- The venue, location and vendors
- A handful of stills (frame grabs work beautifully)
- A call to action

These pages rank for venue searches the same way photographers' blog posts do. See [venue pages](/blog/venue-pages-for-photographers/).

## Explain what couples are buying

Couples know what a photo album is. They often don't know the difference between a highlight film, a feature film, a teaser and documentary edits. Spell it out:

| Deliverable | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Teaser | 60 to 90 seconds for social, delivered quickly |
| Highlight film | 4 to 8 minutes, the story of the day |
| Feature film | 15 to 30 minutes with full speeches and vows |
| Documentary edit | Ceremony and speeches in full |

Mention anything distinctive: 4K, drone, Super 8, same-day edits, licensed music.

## Pricing

Film packages confuse couples more than photography. A clear investment page with starting prices and what's included will save you hours of email. Alpha Visuals, for instance, states that highlight films start from a set price, which immediately qualifies enquiries.

## Stills matter too

Not everyone will press play, especially at work or on mobile data. Sprinkle high-quality frame grabs throughout so the site still sells with the sound off.

[[cta]]

## Sound and music

Never autoplay audio. Let couples choose. On film pages, mention that the film is best watched with sound on; it's a small nudge that changes how they experience it.

## SEO for filmmakers

- Homepage targeting "[city] wedding videographer"
- Film pages with venue names
- Text on every page: Google can't watch your film
- Video schema markup so films can appear in video results
- A Google Business Profile with reviews

The principles in [SEO for wedding photographers](/blog/wedding-photographer-seo/) apply almost exactly.

## Photo and film together

If you offer both, or partner with a photographer, say so prominently. Couples love booking one team. Dan Reynolds Photography presents photo and film side by side for exactly this reason.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should my film autoplay on the homepage?
A muted background loop, yes. A full film with sound, no; let visitors choose.

### How many films should I show?
Five to eight of your best, covering different venues and styles.

### Can a video-heavy site still be fast?
Yes, with compressed loops, poster images and click-to-load players. Our film sites load quickly on mobile for this reason.

=== wedding-photography-pricing-page
title: Should You Show Prices on Your Wedding Photography Website?
desc: The case for and against showing wedding photography prices online, what to include on an investment page, and wording that filters without scaring couples off.
cat: photographers
kw: wedding photography pricing page, show prices on photography website, wedding photographer investment page, photography pricing guide
takeaways: Showing a starting price saves time for you and the couple | You don't have to publish full packages; "collections begin at" works well | Present price alongside what's included and why it's worth it | Follow pricing with proof and an easy way to enquire
---
Few questions divide wedding photographers like this one. One camp says publishing prices scares couples away before they've fallen for your work. The other says hiding them wastes everyone's time. After building sites for photographers and filmmakers across six countries, here's where we land.

## The case for showing prices

- **Couples expect it.** Most are comparing several photographers and will skip those who make them ask.
- **It filters enquiries.** You stop spending evenings replying to couples whose budget is a third of your rate.
- **It builds trust.** Openness reads as confidence.
- **It anchors value.** Seeing a number next to what's included frames the conversation.

## The case against

- **Bespoke quoting.** If every wedding is priced individually, a single number may mislead.
- **Competitors can see it.** True, though they can find out anyway.
- **Sticker shock without context.** A price with no explanation of value can put people off.

## The middle path most photographers should take

Show a **starting price** and explain what shapes the final figure.

> "Wedding collections begin at $3,800. Most couples invest between $4,500 and $6,000 for full-day coverage with a second photographer."

This filters effectively without boxing you in, and invites the enquiry rather than replacing it.

## What to include on an investment page

1. **A short intro** about your approach and what the experience includes
2. **Starting price** or two to three collections
3. **What's included:** hours, second shooter, engagement session, gallery, albums, turnaround time
4. **Add-ons:** extra hours, albums, film, rehearsal dinner
5. **How booking works:** retainer, contract, payment schedule
6. **Travel:** where you're based and how travel fees work
7. **Testimonials** from couples who felt it was worth every penny
8. **FAQs**
9. **A call to action:** "Check your date"

## Presenting collections

If you list full packages, three is the magic number. Name them, lead with the experience and put the most popular in the middle.

| | Essential | Signature | Complete |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Coverage | 6 hours | 8 hours | 10 hours |
| Second photographer | — | Included | Included |
| Engagement session | — | Included | Included |
| Album | — | — | Included |

[[cta]]

## Wording that reassures

- Call it "Investment" or "Pricing"; both are fine. Couples search for "pricing".
- Explain the why: "You're not only booking a day of photography. You're booking 14 months of planning support, 40 hours of editing and images your grandchildren will see."
- Offer payment plans if you have them.
- Invite conversation: "Every wedding is different. Tell me about yours and I'll put together a custom quote."

## If you truly can't show prices

Say so and give a reason, plus a response time: "Because every celebration is unique, I create a custom proposal for each couple. Send me your date and I'll reply within 24 hours." Silence is the real conversion killer.

## A pricing guide PDF

Some photographers offer a detailed guide in exchange for an email address. It works if the site still shows a starting price. If the guide is the only way to see any number, many couples won't bother.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will I get fewer enquiries if I show prices?
Possibly fewer, but better. Photographers usually find the quality and booking rate improve.

### Should I show prices on Instagram too?
A "starting at" line in your highlights or link page is helpful and saves repetitive DMs.

### How often should I raise my prices?
Review them at least once a year, or whenever you're booking more than about 70% of enquiries.

=== venue-pages-for-photographers
title: Venue Pages: The Most Underrated SEO Strategy for Wedding Photographers
desc: How wedding photographers and videographers can use venue pages and real-wedding posts to rank on Google and reach couples who have already booked a venue.
cat: photographers
kw: wedding venue pages seo, venue guides for photographers, wedding photographer venue seo, real wedding blog posts
takeaways: Couples search "[venue] wedding photos" the moment they book | Venue pages face far less competition than city keywords | Combine practical venue advice with your best images from that location | Share each page with the venue and vendors to earn links and referrals
---
A couple books their venue. That night, they type "Kualoa Ranch wedding photos" into Google to see what their day might look like. Whoever appears is talking to a couple with a date, a location and a budget, who haven't yet chosen a photographer.

That's why venue pages are the best-kept secret in wedding SEO.

## Why they work

- **High intent.** These couples are booking vendors now.
- **Low competition.** Hundreds of photographers fight for "Chicago wedding photographer". A handful compete for each venue.
- **Relevance.** Seeing their actual venue through your lens is the most persuasive portfolio possible.
- **Relationships.** Venues love and share good content about themselves.

## Two formats

### 1. Real-wedding blog posts

One wedding at one venue. Tell the story of the day with 30 to 60 images.

**Title:** "A Summer Wedding at Lanikuhonua | Maya & Chris"

### 2. Venue guides

An evergreen page about the venue itself, drawing on several weddings you've shot there.

**Title:** "Four Seasons Oahu Wedding Photography: A Photographer's Guide"

Guides take more effort and rank for longer. Aim for a guide for each of your top five to ten venues, and real-wedding posts for everything else.

## What to include in a venue guide

1. **An introduction** to the venue and why you love shooting there
2. **Your best images** from that location, across seasons and lighting
3. **Best photo spots** on the property
4. **Timing advice:** when the light is best, sunset times, where to do portraits
5. **Rain plan:** indoor options that still look great
6. **Practical details:** capacity, ceremony locations, getting-ready rooms
7. **Recommended vendors** you've worked with there
8. **Links** to full galleries from weddings at the venue
9. **A call to action:** "Getting married here? Check your date."

Aim for 600 to 1,200 words of genuinely useful advice. This is what separates a page that ranks from a gallery that doesn't.

## On-page SEO checklist

- Venue name and "wedding" in the page title and H1
- Venue name and town in the first paragraph
- Descriptive image file names and alt text ([image SEO guide](/blog/image-seo/))
- A link to the venue's own website
- Internal links to and from related posts
- Fast loading despite many images ([photography website speed](/blog/photography-website-speed/))

[[cta]]

## Getting links and referrals from the page

When you publish:

1. **Email the venue coordinator** with the link and a few images they can use, credited.
2. **Tag and message every vendor** involved.
3. **Ask to be considered for the preferred vendor list.**
4. **Share on Pinterest** with the venue name in the pin title.

Venues regularly link to good guides, and those links lift your whole site.

## Which venues to prioritise

- Venues you've shot at and want to return to
- Popular venues with high search volume
- New venues with little content yet
- Venues that suit your style and price point

Check demand by typing the venue name plus "wedding" into Google and seeing what autocomplete suggests.

## What if you haven't shot there yet?

Be honest. You can still write a useful guide based on a site visit or styled shoot and say so. Never imply you've photographed weddings there if you haven't.

### How many venue pages do I need?
Start with your top five. Each one is a long-term asset.
