Selling online has never been more accessible, and the choices have never been more confusing. Shopify, WooCommerce, Squarespace, custom builds. The right answer depends on what you sell, how many products you have and how much control you need.

## Step 1: choose a platform

| | Shopify | WooCommerce | Custom build |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Setup | Fast | Moderate | Slower |
| Monthly cost | $29 to $299+ plus apps | Hosting plus plugins | Hosting only |
| Transaction fees | Yes, unless using Shopify Payments | Processor fees | Processor fees |
| Flexibility | High within the ecosystem | Very high | Unlimited |
| Maintenance | Handled for you | Your responsibility | Minimal to moderate |
| Best for | Most small to mid-size stores | WordPress users wanting control | Unique flows, small catalogs, brand-led stores |

### Choose Shopify if

- You have many products or variants
- You want inventory, shipping and tax handled out of the box
- Non-technical staff will run the store

### Choose custom if

- You sell a small number of products or services
- You need an unusual purchase flow (configurators, bookings with payment, subscriptions with custom logic)
- Speed and distinctive design are a competitive advantage
- You want to avoid monthly platform and app fees

A custom storefront with Stripe Checkout can be remarkably simple and fast for a focused catalog.

## Step 2: essential pages

- **Homepage** with bestsellers and clear categories
- **Category pages** with filters
- **Product pages**
- **Cart and checkout**
- **About** and brand story
- **Shipping and returns**
- **Contact and FAQ**
- **Privacy policy and terms**

## Step 3: product pages that sell

The product page is your salesperson.

- **Photos:** several angles, in use, with zoom. Consistent style.
- **Title:** clear and descriptive
- **Price** and any savings
- **Description:** benefits first, then specifications
- **Variants:** size, colour, clearly selectable
- **Stock and delivery estimate**
- **Reviews** with photos
- **Trust:** returns policy, secure payment, guarantees
- **A prominent "Add to cart" button**
- **Related products**

Write unique descriptions. Copying manufacturer text hurts SEO.

## Step 4: a frictionless checkout

- Guest checkout, no forced account
- As few steps and fields as possible
- Express payment: Apple Pay, Google Pay, Shop Pay, PayPal
- Clear shipping costs early; surprise fees are the top cause of abandonment
- Trust badges and security reassurance
- Order summary always visible

See [accepting payments on your website](/blog/stripe-payments-website/).

## Step 5: shipping, tax and returns

Decide before launch:

- Flat rate, free over a threshold, or calculated shipping
- Which regions you ship to
- How sales tax or VAT is handled
- Your returns policy and who pays for returns

Publish all of it clearly.

## Step 6: SEO for stores

- Keyword-led category and product titles
- Unique descriptions
- Clean URLs
- Product schema with price, availability and reviews ([guide](/blog/schema-markup-guide/))
- Fast pages and optimised images ([image SEO](/blog/image-seo/))
- Helpful content: buying guides, comparisons

## Step 7: recover abandoned carts

- Automated emails an hour and a day after abandonment
- A small incentive on the second or third email
- Clear, direct link back to the cart

## Step 8: build trust

- Reviews and ratings
- Real contact details
- Clear policies
- Secure checkout
- Professional design

See [20 website trust signals](/blog/website-trust-signals/).

## After launch

- Track conversion rate, average order value and abandonment
- Collect reviews with post-purchase emails
- Build an email list from day one
- Test product pages and checkout continuously

## Selling services or bookings

Not all e-commerce is physical products. Courses, programmes, deposits and appointments can all be sold online with the right flow. See [online booking for small businesses](/blog/online-booking-for-small-business/).

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does an e-commerce website cost?
A Shopify store with a customised theme might cost $2,000 to $8,000 to set up. Custom stores typically start around $5,000 and rise with complexity.

### Can I start small and grow?
Yes. Launch with your best products and expand. Choose a platform that can scale with you.

### Do I need a blog on my store?
Helpful content brings organic traffic and builds trust. Buying guides are especially effective.

If customers can pay you online, you get paid faster, take deposits that reduce no-shows and sell while you sleep. Setting it up is easier than most business owners expect.

## What you need

1. A **payment provider** to process cards and send you the money
2. A **way to take the payment** on your site: a button, checkout page or embedded form
3. A **bank account** for payouts

## Choosing a provider

| Provider | Best for |
| --- | --- |
| Stripe | Websites, subscriptions, custom flows; developer-friendly |
| PayPal | Customers who prefer PayPal; simple buttons |
| Square | Businesses also taking in-person payments |
| Razorpay, Paystack and others | Regional strengths in India, Africa and elsewhere |
| Shopify Payments | Shopify stores |

Many businesses use Stripe as the primary processor and add PayPal as an option.

### Fees

Expect roughly 2.5 to 3% plus a small fixed fee per transaction for standard online card payments, with variations by country, card type and method. There's usually no monthly fee. Check current pricing for your region.

## Ways to take payment

### Payment links

The simplest option. Create a link in your provider's dashboard and share it by email, text or a button on your site. No coding.

### Hosted checkout

The customer clicks "Pay" on your site and completes payment on a secure page hosted by the provider (such as Stripe Checkout), then returns. Fast to set up, highly secure and optimised for conversion.

### Embedded payment form

Card fields appear on your own page, styled to match, while the sensitive data goes directly to the provider. More seamless, slightly more work.

### Invoices

Send an invoice with a "Pay now" button. Good for services.

### Through your platform

Booking tools, course platforms and e-commerce systems integrate payments for you.

## What you can sell

- **One-off payments:** products, services, tickets
- **Deposits:** secure bookings and reduce no-shows
- **Subscriptions:** memberships, retainers, software
- **Payment plans:** instalments for higher-priced services
- **Donations**

## Payment methods to offer

- Credit and debit cards
- Apple Pay and Google Pay (one-tap on mobile; they lift conversion)
- PayPal
- Bank transfers and local methods (ACH, SEPA, iDEAL, UPI) depending on your market
- Buy now, pay later for larger purchases

Offer what your customers already use.

## Security and compliance

- **Never store card numbers yourself.** Use hosted fields or checkout so sensitive data never touches your server.
- **HTTPS** across your site
- **Strong Customer Authentication / 3D Secure** where required; providers handle this
- **PCI compliance** is greatly simplified when you use hosted solutions
- **Fraud tools** are built into major providers

## A smooth payment experience

- Show the total, including tax and shipping, before the final step
- Keep forms short
- Allow guest checkout
- Confirm immediately on screen and by email
- Provide a receipt or invoice
- Make refunds easy

## Taxes and invoices

Check your obligations for sales tax, VAT or GST. Tools like Stripe Tax can calculate automatically. Make sure invoices include required details for your country.

## Connecting payments to everything else

- Add customers to your CRM or email list on purchase
- Trigger onboarding emails
- Update booking calendars
- Record sales in your accounting software

See [15 automation ideas for small businesses](/blog/business-automation-ideas/).

Payments built into different kinds of sites: enrol-and-pay for school programmes and a one-click checkout for a digital product.

## Common mistakes

- Surprise fees at the last step
- Forcing account creation
- No mobile wallets
- A checkout that looks different from the rest of the site without explanation
- No confirmation email
- Unclear refund policy

## Frequently asked questions

### How quickly do I get paid?
Typically within two to seven days of the transaction, depending on provider and country.

### Can I take payments without a full online store?
Yes. Payment links and hosted checkout work on any website.

### What about chargebacks?
Keep clear records, policies and proof of delivery. Providers offer dispute tools, and good communication prevents most disputes.

title: Buying a Ready-Made Website vs Building From Scratch
desc: The pros and cons of buying a ready-made or pre-built website compared with a custom build, what to check before buying and who each option suits.

---
Not every business needs a website designed from a blank page. If your needs match a common pattern (a salon with booking, a clinic with services and appointments, a solar installer with a quote form) a ready-made site that's already designed and built can get you live in a fraction of the time.

Here's how to decide between buying and building.

## What "ready-made" means

There's a spectrum:

| Type | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Theme or template | A design sold to many buyers; you add content |
| Pre-built custom site | A complete, professionally built site sold to one owner |
| Existing business website | A live site with traffic or revenue, bought as an asset |

This article focuses on the first two.

## Advantages of buying

- **Speed.** Live in days.
- **Cost.** Lower than a custom design.
- **Certainty.** You see exactly what you're getting.
- **Proven structure.** Built around what works for that industry.

## Disadvantages

- **Fit.** You adapt to the design, not the other way round.
- **Uniqueness.** Mass-market themes are used by thousands.
- **Quality varies.** Many templates are bloated and slow.
- **Customisation limits** unless you have the source code.

## Advantages of building custom

- **Designed around your business** and customers
- **Completely unique**
- **Any feature** you need
- **Optimised** for your specific goals

## Disadvantages

- **Higher cost**
- **Longer timeline**
- **More decisions** required from you

## What to check before buying a ready-made site

1. **Is it exclusive?** A design sold once is very different from one sold a thousand times.
2. **Do you get the full source code?**
3. **Who owns it after purchase?**
4. **How easily can it be rebranded?** Colours, fonts, logo, copy, images.
5. **Is it fast?** Test the demo on PageSpeed Insights.
6. **Is it mobile-friendly?**
7. **Is SEO built in?** Titles, schema, clean structure.
8. **What's included?** Booking, payments, forms.
9. **What does hosting cost?**
10. **Is support or customisation available?**

## Mass-market templates vs one-owner sites

A $60 theme from a marketplace is cheap for a reason: it's built to suit everyone, so it's stuffed with options, and thousands of other sites look the same.

A pre-built custom site sold to a single owner is different: designed like a client project, coded cleanly and yours alone. At Code Bunny we build a small number of these for specific industries. Each sells once.

[[work:tpl-beauty,tpl-event]]

## Who should buy

- New businesses that need to launch quickly
- Owners with a clear match to an existing design
- Businesses with modest budgets who still want quality
- Anyone who'd rather react to something finished than start from nothing

## Who should build

- Businesses with unusual services or processes
- Brands where distinctiveness is central
- Sites needing custom features or integrations
- Companies in very competitive markets

## A hybrid approach

Buy a strong pre-built site, then customise: your branding, copy, photos, plus any extra pages or features. You get most of the benefit of custom at a lower cost and faster.

## Buying an existing website business

A different proposition: you're buying traffic, revenue and reputation. Do proper due diligence on analytics, income, backlinks and any penalties, ideally with professional help.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a ready-made site rank on Google?
Yes, if it's well built and you add unique content about your business and location.

### Will it look like everyone else's?
Mass-market themes might. A one-owner design, rebranded with your identity and content, won't.

### Can I upgrade to custom later?
Yes. Many businesses start with a ready-made site and invest in a custom design once revenue grows.

takeaways: A blog is worth it if customers search for answers before they buy | It brings long-term traffic, builds trust and feeds social, email and AI search | Quality and consistency matter more than volume | If you can't commit, a small set of evergreen guides is a better choice than an abandoned blog
---
"You should start a blog" is advice every business owner has heard. It's also why the internet is full of company blogs with three posts from 2021. A blog is a tool. It's powerful for some businesses and a distraction for others.

Here's how to tell which you are.

## What a blog can do

### Bring search traffic

Each article can rank for questions your customers ask. A solar installer's guide to local grants. A photographer's guide to a wedding venue. A coach's article on changing careers at 40. People find the article, then find you.

### Build trust before the sale

Helpful content shows you know your subject. By the time someone enquires, they already believe you can help.

### Feed your other marketing

One article becomes several social posts, a newsletter and answers you can send to prospects.

### Support AI visibility

AI assistants draw on clear, authoritative content when answering questions. Useful articles increase your chances of being cited. See [how to get mentioned in AI search](/blog/ai-search-optimization/).

### Strengthen your whole site

More relevant pages, more internal links, more reasons for other sites to link to you.

## When a blog is worth it

- Customers research before buying
- There are questions you answer repeatedly
- Your service is expensive or complex enough to need explaining
- You compete locally and want to stand out
- You (or someone on your team) can commit to publishing consistently

## When it probably isn't

- Customers buy on impulse with no research
- All your work comes from a few long-term contracts
- You have no time and no budget to delegate
- You'd be writing only because someone said you should

An abandoned blog looks worse than no blog.

## The alternative: evergreen guides

If regular publishing isn't realistic, create five to ten substantial guides that answer your customers' biggest questions, and update them once or twice a year. Call the section "Guides" or "Resources". You get most of the SEO benefit without the treadmill.

## What to write about

- **Questions customers ask:** every FAQ is a potential article
- **Costs and pricing:** "How much does X cost?"
- **Comparisons:** "X vs Y"
- **How-to guides**
- **Local topics:** venues, regulations, area guides
- **Case studies:** real projects and results
- **Mistakes to avoid**

Skip company news nobody outside the business cares about.

Use [keyword research](/blog/keyword-research-small-business/) to confirm people search for a topic before you write.

## How often

Consistency beats frequency.

| Capacity | Realistic plan |
| --- | --- |
| Very limited | One solid article a quarter, plus updates |
| Moderate | One a month |
| Committed | Two to four a month |

One genuinely useful article a month for a year gives you twelve assets that can bring traffic for years.

## How to write posts that work

- Answer one clear question
- Put the answer early
- Use headings, lists and examples
- Show your own experience
- Link to your services and related posts
- End with a next step

Full method in [how to write a blog post that ranks](/blog/how-to-write-a-blog-post-that-ranks/).

## Turning readers into leads

Traffic alone doesn't pay the bills.

- A relevant call to action in every post
- A lead magnet for readers who aren't ready
- Clear links to service pages
- A simple form at the end

Every article on this site ends with an invitation to get a free website preview, because that's the next logical step for our readers.

[[cta]]

## Who writes it?

- **You:** the most authentic, if you have time
- **A team member**
- **A freelance writer** briefed with your expertise
- **Your web partner**
- **AI-assisted:** fine for drafts, but add real experience and check every fact

## Technical must-haves

- Fast pages
- Clean URLs
- Article and FAQ schema ([guide](/blog/schema-markup-guide/))
- Categories that make sense
- An editor you can actually use

When we launch client sites, we often include a blog and a simple editor so owners can publish themselves. Dan Reynolds Photography's site shipped with both.

## Measure it

- Organic traffic to articles
- Search queries they rank for ([Search Console guide](/blog/google-search-console-guide/))
- Enquiries that started on a blog post
- Time on page

Give it six to twelve months. Content compounds.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long before a blog brings in leads?
Typically three to six months for early results, growing over time.

### How long should posts be?
As long as needed to answer the question well; often 800 to 1,500 words.

### Should I delete old posts?
Update the good ones, merge overlapping ones and remove those with no value. A smaller, stronger library performs better.
