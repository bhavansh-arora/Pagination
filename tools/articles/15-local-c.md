title: Websites for Trades and Home Services: Get More Calls and Quotes

---
When a boiler breaks or a roof leaks, people search on their phone and call one of the first few businesses that look trustworthy. A trade website doesn't need to be fancy. It needs to be fast, clear and convincing in about ten seconds.

## What customers are looking for

1. Do you do the job I need?
2. Do you cover my area?
3. Can I trust you?
4. How do I get hold of you right now?

Design every page around those four questions.

## Homepage must-haves

- **A plain headline:** "Plumbing and heating in Leeds. Same-day callouts."
- **A tappable phone number** at the top
- **"Get a free quote" button**
- **Trust strip:** years trading, review rating, accreditations, insurance
- **Services** with icons or photos
- **Areas covered**
- **Recent work** photos
- **Reviews**
- **A short form** at the bottom

## Service pages

One page per main service, because customers search specifically: "boiler repair", "consumer unit replacement", "flat roof repair", "kitchen fitter".

Each page: what's included, typical problems you solve, rough pricing or how you quote, photos, FAQs, and a call to action.

## Area pages

A page for each key town or district you cover, with local jobs and reviews. See [how to create local landing pages](/blog/local-landing-pages/).

## Proof

- **Before-and-after photos** of real jobs
- **Google reviews,** shown with stars and count
- **Accreditations:** trade bodies, licences, Gas Safe, NICEIC, manufacturer approvals
- **Insurance** and guarantees
- **Photos of you and your van.** People like seeing who'll turn up.

## Make contact effortless

- Phone number in the header, sticky on mobile
- WhatsApp button for photos of the problem ([how to add it](/blog/whatsapp-for-business-website/))
- A quote form with three or four fields plus photo upload
- Emergency callout information
- Opening hours

[[cta]]

## Pricing

You don't need a full price list. Even a guide ("boiler service from £80", "callout fee £60") builds trust and filters time-wasters.

## Local SEO

For trades, Google Maps is the shop window.

- Google Business Profile with service areas, photos of jobs and weekly updates ([checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization/))
- Ask for a review after every job
- Consistent name, address and phone everywhere
- LocalBusiness schema

Full plan in our [local SEO guide](/blog/local-seo-guide/).

## Keep it fast and simple

- Loads in under two seconds on mobile
- Large text and buttons
- No pop-ups blocking the phone number
- Works well in poor signal

## Extras that help

- A short video introducing yourself
- Finance options for bigger jobs
- A maintenance plan or service contract page
- A blog answering common questions: "why is my boiler losing pressure?"

## Commercial work

If you also do commercial contracts, add a separate page with relevant accreditations, insurance levels and case studies. Business buyers look for different proof.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a website if I get work from Facebook and word of mouth?
A website makes you findable on Google, gives new customers confidence and works when referrals slow down.

### How much should a trades website cost?
A solid lead-generating site typically costs $1,500 to $5,000. See [how much a website costs](/blog/how-much-does-a-website-cost-small-business/).

### What's the most important feature?
A phone number people can tap, followed closely by reviews.

=== online-booking-for-small-business
title: Online Booking for Small Businesses: How to Add It to Your Website

A large share of appointments are booked outside working hours, when nobody's answering the phone. Online booking means those customers can commit there and then, while you're with a client, at dinner or asleep.

## Who benefits

- Salons, barbers and spas
- Clinics, dentists and therapists
- Coaches and consultants
- Fitness studios and personal trainers
- Photographers
- Trades offering fixed-price services or assessments
- Restaurants and venues

If customers book time with you, it applies.

## Choosing a booking tool

| Need | Popular options |
| --- | --- |
| One-to-one calls and consultations | Calendly, TidyCal, Cal.com, Google Calendar booking |
| Salons and spas | Fresha, Booksy, Vagaro, Timely |
| Classes and studios | Mindbody, Momence, Acuity |
| Clinics | Cliniko, Jane, practice-specific systems |
| Restaurants | OpenTable, Resy, ResDiary |
| General services | Square Appointments, Acuity, SimplyBook |

### What to look for

- Calendar sync, so you're never double-booked
- Embeds cleanly in your website
- Email and SMS reminders
- Deposits or card capture
- Staff and service management
- Fair pricing and no punitive commission
- You keep ownership of your customer data

## Embedding it properly

- **Keep customers on your site** with an embedded widget where possible
- **A "Book now" button** in the header on every page
- **Link each service** directly to its booking flow
- **Match the look:** colours and fonts close to your brand
- **Test on mobile.** Most bookings happen there.

## Keep the flow short

Ideal path: choose service, choose time, enter details, confirm. Every extra step loses people. Ask only for what you need at booking; collect the rest later.

## Reduce no-shows

- **Deposits or card on file** for longer appointments
- **Automated reminders** 24 to 48 hours before, and again on the day
- **Easy rescheduling** via a link
- **A clear cancellation policy** shown before they confirm

## Custom booking flows

Off-the-shelf tools suit most businesses. A custom flow makes sense when you need something specific: multi-step quotes, packages, group bookings, or a booking experience that's part of your brand. We've built custom booking for an escape-room venue and enrol-and-pay flows for school dance programmes.

## After the booking

- Instant confirmation with a calendar invite
- Preparation instructions
- Directions or video link
- A follow-up afterwards asking for a review

## Common mistakes

- Sending customers to a separate site that looks nothing like yours
- Too many required fields
- No mobile testing
- Hidden booking link
- No reminders
- Not showing prices or durations

## Measure it

Track how many visitors start a booking and how many complete it. If many abandon, shorten the flow or reconsider the deposit amount.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will customers still phone to book?
Some will. Offer both. Over time, most businesses see a majority of bookings move online.

### Is online booking expensive?
Many tools have free or low-cost plans for solo businesses. Costs rise with staff numbers and features.

### Can booking connect to payments?
Yes. Most tools integrate with Stripe, Square or PayPal for deposits and full payments. See [accepting payments on your website](/blog/stripe-payments-website/).

=== whatsapp-for-business-website
title: Adding WhatsApp to Your Website: A Practical Guide for Small Businesses
desc: How and why to add a WhatsApp chat button to your business website, with setup steps, message templates, etiquette and tips for turning chats into customers.
cat: local
kw: whatsapp button website, whatsapp for business, click to chat whatsapp, whatsapp business website integration
takeaways: Many customers would rather message than call or fill in a form | A click-to-chat link takes minutes to set up and costs nothing | Pre-filled messages make starting a conversation effortless | Set expectations for reply times and keep conversations professional
---
For a lot of customers, sending a quick WhatsApp is easier than making a phone call or completing a form. In many countries it's the default way people talk to businesses. Adding it to your website is one of the simplest changes you can make to get more enquiries.

## Why it works

- **Low effort.** One tap opens a chat.
- **Familiar.** People already use it daily.
- **Asynchronous.** They can ask now and you can reply between jobs.
- **Rich.** Customers can send photos, voice notes and locations.
- **Personal.** A conversation builds trust quickly.

We use it ourselves. One of our favourite pieces of client feedback arrived as a WhatsApp from a wedding photographer: "I got a lot of compliments on it."

## Step 1: set up WhatsApp Business

Download the free WhatsApp Business app and register your business number. Then:

- Add your business name, logo, description, address and hours
- Set a **greeting message** for new chats
- Set an **away message** for out-of-hours
- Create **quick replies** for common questions
- Add a **catalogue** if you sell products or packages

## Step 2: create a click-to-chat link

The format is:

`https://wa.me/<number>?text=<message>`

Use your full number with country code and no plus sign, spaces or dashes. The optional `text` adds a pre-filled message, for example: "Hi, I found you online and would like a quote."

Pre-filling the first message removes the awkward blank box and tells you where the enquiry came from.

## Step 3: add it to your website

- **A floating button** in the corner of every page
- **In the header or contact section** next to your phone number
- **On service pages** with a tailored pre-filled message: "Hi, I'm interested in boiler servicing"
- **On the thank-you page** after a form submission

Keep the button visible without covering content or other buttons on mobile.

## Step 4: reply well

- **Respond quickly** in business hours. Speed is the whole advantage.
- **Use their name** and answer the actual question.
- **Keep messages short.**
- **Move to a call or booking** when it makes sense: "Would a quick call be easier? Here's my calendar."
- **Stay professional.** It's still business communication.

[[cta]]

## Etiquette and compliance

- Only message people who contacted you or opted in
- Don't add customers to groups or broadcast lists without consent
- Respect privacy laws in your country
- Avoid sharing sensitive information over chat
- Mention WhatsApp in your privacy policy

## When to upgrade

The free app suits most small businesses. If several staff need to answer from one number, or you want automation and CRM integration, look at the WhatsApp Business Platform through an approved provider.

## Combine with other channels

WhatsApp works best alongside, not instead of, a phone number and a form. Different customers prefer different routes. See [contact forms that convert](/blog/contact-form-best-practices/).

## Track it

Add tracking to your WhatsApp link clicks in analytics so you know how many enquiries it generates and from which pages.

## Add automation

A chatbot can answer common questions, collect details and hand over to you when needed. See [AI chatbots for small business](/blog/ai-chatbot-for-small-business/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a separate phone number?
It's wise to use a business number so personal and work chats stay separate.

### Is WhatsApp suitable for every business?
It suits most service businesses. For regulated sectors such as healthcare and finance, check what you're allowed to discuss over chat.

### Will it slow my website down?
A simple link button adds no weight. Avoid heavy third-party chat widgets if a plain link does the job.

---
A barbershop is a brand as much as a service. The best shops have a look, a sound and a feel, and their website should carry it. It also has to do the practical job: let someone book a fade at 11pm on a Sunday for Tuesday afternoon.

## What every barbershop site needs

### Online booking

- "Book now" in the header and hero
- Choose barber, service and time
- Reminders by text
- Walk-in information if you take them

Booksy, Fresha, Squire and Square are common choices. More in [online booking for small businesses](/blog/online-booking-for-small-business/).

### A price list

Cuts, fades, beard trims, hot towel shaves, kids' cuts, packages. Clear prices and durations.

### Location and hours

Address, map, parking and opening times, visible on every page.

### The barbers

Photo, name, specialities, Instagram handle and a personal booking link for each. Regulars follow barbers.

### A gallery

Real cuts from your chairs. Fresh fades, beard work, the shop itself. Update it often.

## Give it attitude

Barbershops have some of the best branding of any local business. Use it.

- **Typography:** bold serifs, vintage scripts or sharp modern sans
- **Colour:** dark and moody, classic red-white-blue or clean monochrome
- **Photography:** atmospheric shots of the shop and the craft
- **Voice:** confident and short. "Sharp cuts. Sharper confidence."

- **Products** for sale: pomades, oils, tools
- **Gift cards**
- **Memberships** or loyalty schemes
- **Groom and wedding party packages**
- **Hiring page** for new barbers
- **Events and collaborations**

## Local SEO

- Consistent details across Instagram, Facebook and directories

## Mobile first

Nearly every visitor is on a phone, often coming from Instagram. Big buttons, fast loading, booking in two taps.

## Instagram and your website

Instagram shows the work. The website turns interest into bookings and shows up on Google. Link them: booking link in your bio, feed highlights on your site.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do barbershops need a website if they use a booking app?
Yes. Your own site ranks on Google, shows your brand properly and isn't dependent on one platform.

### Should I list every barber?
Yes, with photos. Clients choose people.
