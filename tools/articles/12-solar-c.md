For many installers, batteries and EV chargers have moved from optional extras to a major share of revenue. Yet on most solar websites they're a line in a list. Giving each a proper page captures searches you're currently missing and lifts the value of every solar quote.

## Why separate pages

- People search for them directly: "home battery installation [town]", "EV charger installer near me"
- Each has its own questions and objections
- Existing solar owners want to add them later
- EV owners may not have solar yet and are a new audience

## The battery storage page

### Lead with the benefit

- "Use your solar power after dark"
- "Keep the lights on in a power cut"
- "Cut your reliance on the grid"

### Explain how it works, simply

A short diagram: panels charge the battery by day; the battery powers the home in the evening; the grid tops up only when needed.

### Who it's for

- Homes out during the day
- Areas with frequent outages
- Customers on time-of-use tariffs
- Anyone wanting more energy independence

### Options and sizing

Brands you install, typical capacities and what each can run. A simple table helps.

### Cost and payback

Be honest. Batteries often have a longer payback than panels. Give typical ranges and explain non-financial benefits such as backup power.

### Retrofit

Make it clear you can add a battery to an existing system, including ones you didn't install.

### FAQs

Lifespan, warranty, safety, where it's installed, whether it works in a power cut.

## The EV charger page

### Lead with convenience and savings

- "Charge at home for a fraction of public charging costs"
- "Charge your car from your roof"

### Cover the basics

- Charger types and speeds
- Smart charging and solar-aware charging
- Tethered vs untethered
- Typical install time and what's involved

### Grants and requirements

Any available home or workplace charger grants, and electrical requirements such as fuse board upgrades.

### Who you serve

Homes, workplaces, apartment blocks, fleets. Commercial charging can be its own page.

### FAQs

Do I need planning permission? Can I install without solar? Which cars are compatible?

[[cta]]

## Bundle them

Offer clear packages: solar only, solar plus battery, solar plus battery plus EV charger. Show the combined benefit. Customers often choose the fuller package when it's presented as a complete system.

## Cross-link everywhere

- From the solar page: "Add a battery" and "Add an EV charger"
- From the calculator results: "With a battery you'd use X% more of your own power"
- From case studies featuring combined systems

Good internal linking helps SEO too; see our [internal linking guide](/blog/internal-linking-guide/).

## Market to your past customers

Your existing solar customers already trust you. Email them when:

- Battery prices fall or new grants appear
- You start installing a new battery brand
- They've had their system long enough to see how much they export

A dedicated landing page for "add a battery to your existing solar" gives those emails somewhere to send people.

## SEO essentials

- Titles like "Home Battery Storage Installation in [Region]" and "EV Charger Installers [Town]"
- FAQ and Service schema
- Area mentions and links to your [service area pages](/blog/solar-service-area-pages/)
- Real installation photos with descriptive alt text

## See it done

Lithium Solar Energy and Eco Horizon both present solar, battery and EV charging as distinct services within one coherent offer.

[[work:lisolarenergy,ecohorizon]]

## Frequently asked questions

### Should batteries be mentioned in every solar quote?
Yes, as an option with its own price and benefit, so the customer can choose.

### Is there enough search demand for separate pages?
Yes. Battery and EV charger searches have grown quickly and competition is often lower than for solar itself.

### What if we only install one brand?
Explain why you chose it. A confident recommendation is more reassuring than a long list.

Selling solar to a business or a farm is a different sale from selling to a homeowner. There are more decision-makers, bigger numbers and a longer timeline. The website content that wins residential leads (lifestyle images, monthly bill savings) won't convince a finance director or a dairy farmer.

## What commercial buyers want to know

1. **What's the return?** Payback period, IRR, lifetime savings
2. **How is it financed?** Capital purchase, lease, power purchase agreement
3. **What's the tax position?** Allowances and depreciation
4. **Will it disrupt operations?**
5. **Who else have you done this for?**
6. **What happens after installation?** Monitoring, maintenance, performance guarantees

Your commercial page should answer all six.

## Structure of a commercial solar page

### Headline

Lead with the business outcome: "Cut your energy costs by up to 40% and fix your price for 25 years."

### Proof strip

Total kW or MW installed, number of commercial clients, sectors served, accreditations.

### Who it's for

Manufacturing, warehousing, retail, hospitality, offices, schools. Short sections or tabs for each, with relevant examples.

### The financial case

- Typical system sizes and costs per kW
- Example payback calculations
- Financing options explained
- Tax allowances and grants (with advice to confirm with their accountant)

### Process

Feasibility study, design, grid application, installation, commissioning, monitoring, with realistic timelines.

### Case studies

The centrepiece. For each:

- Client and sector
- Challenge
- System size
- Annual generation and savings
- Payback
- A quote from the decision-maker
- Photos

### Call to action

"Request a free feasibility assessment". Business buyers respond to a tangible deliverable more than "get a quote".

## Agricultural solar

Farms combine high daytime energy use, large roofs and, in many regions, dedicated grants. Give agriculture its own page.

### Speak the language

Milking parlours, grain drying, cold storage, poultry sheds. Show you understand how farms use energy.

### Cover the grant in detail

Where farm-specific schemes exist (such as TAMS in Ireland), explain eligibility, rates and the application process, and offer to handle it.

### Show farm projects

Photos of panels on sheds, with herd size or operation type, system size and savings.

### Address practical concerns

Roof condition, asbestos sheeting, livestock safety, three-phase supply, export limits.

[[cta]]

## Lead capture for B2B

Ask slightly different questions from the residential form:

- Company name and sector
- Site location
- Approximate annual electricity spend
- Roof or land availability
- Decision timeline
- Contact name and role

Offer to review energy bills confidentially. Make it easy to upload them.

## Content that supports the sale

- **A downloadable guide:** "The business case for commercial solar"
- **An ROI calculator** for commercial systems
- **Sector pages** for your strongest industries
- **Articles** on financing, tax treatment and grid connection

Business buyers forward links to colleagues. Give them something worth forwarding.

## Utility-scale and land

If you develop solar farms or work with landowners, that's a third audience with its own page: land requirements, lease terms, the development process. Watthaus Strategy, for example, speaks separately to homeowners and to utility-scale and land projects.

[[work:watthaus,ecohorizon]]

## SEO for commercial solar

Target "commercial solar installers [region]", "solar for farms [country]" and sector terms like "solar for warehouses". Volume is lower than residential, but each lead is worth far more. See [SEO for solar companies](/blog/solar-seo/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Should commercial and residential be on the same website?
Usually yes, with clearly separate sections so each audience sees relevant content.

### How long is the commercial sales cycle?
Often three to twelve months. Nurture with case studies and follow-up, not pressure.

### Do case studies need exact figures?
Real numbers are far more persuasive. If a client prefers privacy, use ranges and anonymise the name.

=== solar-faq-content
title: 25 Solar FAQs Your Website Should Answer

cat: solar

takeaways: Every FAQ is a search query and an objection you can remove | Answer in two to four plain sentences, then link to detail | Group questions by topic so visitors find theirs quickly | FAQ schema helps answers appear in search results and AI summaries
---
Before they request a quote, homeowners have questions, usually the same ones. If your website answers them clearly, you build trust, shorten sales calls and pick up search traffic for each question. If it doesn't, they'll find the answers on a competitor's site.

Here are the 25 to cover, grouped by theme.

## Cost and savings

1. **How much do solar panels cost?** Give typical ranges for common system sizes in your area.
2. **How much will I save?** Explain what affects savings and link to your calculator.
3. **How long is the payback period?** A typical range with a worked example.
4. **Are there grants or incentives?** Summarise and link to your [incentives page](/blog/solar-incentives-content/).
5. **Is finance available?** Outline the options.
6. **Will solar increase my home's value?** Share what's known honestly.

## Suitability

7. **Is my roof suitable?** Orientation, pitch, shading and condition.
8. **Does solar work in cloudy weather or winter?** Yes, at reduced output; explain.
9. **How many panels do I need?** Based on usage and roof space.
10. **Do I need planning permission?** State local rules and exceptions.
11. **Can I install solar on a flat roof or the ground?** Explain options.

## How it works

12. **How do solar panels work?** Two or three sentences and a diagram.
13. **What happens to electricity I don't use?** Export payments or net metering in your region.
14. **Will solar power my home in a power cut?** Only with a battery and the right inverter; be clear.
15. **Do I need a battery?** Pros, cons and who benefits most.
16. **Can I charge an electric car with solar?** Yes; link to your [EV charger page](/blog/battery-ev-charger-pages/).

## Installation

17. **How long does installation take?** Typical days on site and overall timeline.
18. **What's involved in the process?** Survey, design, install, commissioning.
19. **Will there be disruption?** Scaffolding, brief power-down, access needed.
20. **Who handles the paperwork?** Grid applications, grants, certificates.

## After installation

21. **How long do panels last?** Typical lifespan and degradation.
22. **What warranties do I get?** Product, performance and workmanship.
23. **Do panels need maintenance or cleaning?** Minimal; explain.
24. **How do I monitor my system?** App and what it shows.
25. **What if something goes wrong?** Your aftercare and response times.

## How to write good answers

- **Answer first.** Put the direct answer in the first sentence.
- **Keep it short.** Two to four sentences, then link to a detailed page.
- **Use local specifics.** Your region's rules, rates and schemes.
- **Be honest.** Admit limitations; it builds credibility.
- **Update regularly.** Especially costs and incentives.

[[cta]]

## Where FAQs should live

- A dedicated FAQ page, grouped by topic
- The most relevant three to five on each service page
- A few on the homepage
- In area pages, for local questions

## Mark them up with schema

FAQ schema tells search engines exactly which text is the question and which is the answer. That helps your content appear in rich results and makes it easier for AI assistants to quote you. See our [schema markup guide](/blog/schema-markup-guide/) and [how to get mentioned in AI search](/blog/ai-search-optimization/).

## Turn the big ones into articles

Questions like "how much do solar panels cost in [region]?" and "is solar worth it?" deserve full guides of their own. Link to them from the short FAQ answer. This is the backbone of [SEO for solar companies](/blog/solar-seo/).

## Use them in sales

Send links to relevant answers after a call. It reinforces what you said and shows you're thorough.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many FAQs should a solar site have?
Start with these 25. Add more as your sales team hears new questions.

### Should answers include prices?
Typical ranges, yes. They're among the most searched questions and avoiding them pushes visitors elsewhere.

### Do FAQs help with SEO?
Yes. Each is a chance to match a specific search, and structured answers are favoured by both search engines and AI tools.
