title: SEO for a New Website: What to Do in the First 90 Days

takeaways: Get the technical foundations and tracking right in week one | Submit your sitemap and make sure Google can index every key page | Publish a small amount of genuinely useful content rather than a lot of thin pages | Early reviews, listings and a few good links build trust fastest
---
A new website starts with no history, no links and no trust. Google doesn't know it exists. The first three months are about fixing that: getting found, getting understood and earning the first signs of credibility.

Here's a realistic plan.

## Week 1: foundations

- **Set up Google Search Console** and verify your domain
- **Set up Bing Webmaster Tools** (it also feeds several AI assistants)
- **Install analytics** (GA4 or a privacy-friendly alternative)
- **Submit your XML sitemap** in both
- **Check indexing:** use URL Inspection on your homepage and request indexing
- **Confirm HTTPS** and that all versions of your domain redirect to one
- **Run PageSpeed Insights** and fix anything in the red

Use our [technical SEO checklist](/blog/technical-seo-checklist/) to cover everything.

## Week 2: on-page basics

- **Unique title tag and meta description** on every page ([how to write them](/blog/title-tags-meta-descriptions/))
- **One clear H1** per page
- **Service pages** for each thing you sell
- **Contact details** in the footer
- **Schema markup:** Organization or LocalBusiness, Service, FAQ ([guide](/blog/schema-markup-guide/))
- **Internal links** between related pages

## Week 3: local presence

- **Create or claim your Google Business Profile** and complete every field ([checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization/))
- **Claim Bing Places and Apple Business Connect**
- **Add your business to key directories** for your industry and region
- **Make sure name, address and phone match** everywhere

## Week 4: first reviews

Ask five to ten recent customers for a Google review. A new site with a handful of genuine reviews looks far more established than one with none.

## Weeks 5 to 8: content

Publish four to six genuinely useful pieces:

- Answers to your customers' most common questions
- A pricing or cost guide
- A "how it works" explainer
- A comparison or buying guide
- A case study or two

Quality matters more than quantity. See [how to write a blog post that ranks](/blog/how-to-write-a-blog-post-that-ranks/) and [keyword research for small businesses](/blog/keyword-research-small-business/).

## Weeks 9 to 12: early links and refinement

### Earn your first links

- Suppliers and partners
- Industry associations and accreditation bodies
- Local chamber of commerce
- Clients who list their suppliers
- A local news story or guest article

A few relevant links from real organisations are worth more than hundreds from low-quality directories.

### Review the data

In Search Console, look at:

- Which pages are indexed
- Which queries you're starting to appear for
- Pages with impressions but low clicks (improve titles)
- Any crawl errors

Our [Search Console guide](/blog/google-search-console-guide/) shows where.

[[audit]]

## What results to expect

| Timeframe | Typical progress |
| --- | --- |
| Weeks 1 to 2 | Site indexed, ranks for your brand name |
| Month 1 to 2 | Appears for long-tail and local searches |
| Month 3 | First organic enquiries, growing impressions |
| Month 6+ | Competing for main service keywords |

New sites take time to build trust. Steady effort wins. See [how long does SEO take?](/blog/how-long-does-seo-take/)

## What not to do

- **Buy links** or use link schemes
- **Publish dozens of thin pages** to "look bigger"
- **Copy content** from other sites
- **Change URLs repeatedly**
- **Expect page-one rankings in week two**

## Replacing an old site?

If your new site replaces an existing one, the priority is redirects. Without them you'll lose rankings the old site earned. See [how to redesign without losing SEO](/blog/seo-migration-redesign/).

## Don't forget AI search

State clearly who you are, what you do and where; add FAQs; allow AI crawlers. See [how to get mentioned in AI search](/blog/ai-search-optimization/).

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does it take Google to index a new site?
Usually a few days to a couple of weeks after you submit a sitemap.

### Should I run ads while SEO builds?
Often, yes. Ads bring traffic immediately while organic visibility grows. See [SEO vs Google Ads](/blog/seo-vs-google-ads/).

### Do I need a blog from day one?
You need useful content. A handful of strong guides is enough to start.

=== keyword-research-small-business
title: Keyword Research for Small Businesses: A Simple, Free Method
desc: A simple keyword research method for small businesses using free tools: find what customers search, judge intent and competition, and map keywords to pages.
cat: seo
kw: keyword research small business, how to do keyword research, free keyword research tools, local keyword research
takeaways: Start with how your customers describe their problem, not industry jargon | Intent matters more than volume; "near me" and "cost" searches are close to buying | Free tools like Google autocomplete and Search Console are enough to begin | Assign one main keyword to each page
---
Keyword research is simply finding out what your customers type into Google, so you can build pages that answer them. You don't need expensive software. An hour with free tools will give you a list that guides your whole website.

## Step 1: brainstorm seed terms

Write down:

- Each service or product you sell
- The problems customers come to you with
- Words customers actually use (not your internal jargon)
- Your location and areas served

A plumber's list might include: boiler repair, blocked drain, leaking tap, emergency plumber, bathroom installation, Leeds, Headingley.

## Step 2: expand with free tools

### Google autocomplete

Type a seed term and note the suggestions. Add a letter after it ("boiler repair a", "boiler repair b") to see more.

### People also ask

The question boxes in search results show what people want to know. Each is a potential FAQ or article.

### Related searches

Scroll to the bottom of the results page for more ideas.

### Google Search Console

If your site is live, the Performance report shows queries you already appear for. Look for ones ranking between positions 8 and 20; they're your quickest wins.

### Google Keyword Planner

Free with a Google Ads account. Gives rough volume ranges and related terms.

### Other free options

AnswerThePublic, AlsoAsked, Ubersuggest's free tier and Bing Webmaster Tools' keyword research.

## Step 3: understand intent

Not all searches are equal. Sort each keyword by what the searcher wants:

| Intent | Example | Best page type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Buy / hire | "emergency plumber Leeds" | Service or location page |
| Compare | "best boiler brands" | Comparison guide |
| Learn | "why is my boiler losing pressure" | Blog article |
| Find you | "Harbour Plumbing reviews" | Homepage, reviews page |

Buying-intent keywords bring customers. Informational ones build awareness and trust. You need both, but start with the first.

## Step 4: judge competition

Search each important keyword and look at page one.

- Are the results big national brands or local businesses like you?
- Are the pages specifically about that topic or only loosely related?
- Could you create something clearly more useful?

If page one is all household names, target a more specific version: add a location, a niche or a detail. "Wedding photographer" is brutal. "Catholic wedding photographer Denver" is winnable.

## Step 5: go long-tail

Long, specific phrases have lower volume but higher intent and less competition:

- "career coach for tech professionals Vancouver"
- "solar panel installers for farms Cork"
- "balayage specialist Vaudreuil"

A handful of these often bring more customers than one broad term.

## Step 6: map keywords to pages

Give each page one primary keyword and a few closely related ones.

| Page | Primary keyword | Related |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Homepage | plumber Leeds | plumbing and heating Leeds |
| Service | boiler repair Leeds | boiler breakdown, gas engineer |
| Location | plumber Headingley | emergency plumber Headingley |
| Article | boiler losing pressure | how to repressurise a boiler |

Never target the same keyword with two pages; they'll compete with each other.

[[audit]]

## Step 7: use them naturally

Put the primary keyword in:

- The title tag
- The H1
- The first paragraph
- One or two subheadings
- The URL
- Image alt text where relevant

Then write for humans. See [title tags and meta descriptions](/blog/title-tags-meta-descriptions/).

## Local modifiers

For local businesses, combine every service with:

- Your town or city
- Neighbourhoods and nearby towns
- "near me" (you don't write this on the page; you earn it through local signals)

See our [local SEO guide](/blog/local-seo-guide/).

## Revisit every six months

Search behaviour shifts. Check Search Console for new queries, and add pages for topics you're starting to appear for.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need paid tools?
Not to begin. Paid tools such as Ahrefs or Semrush become useful once you're investing seriously in content.

### What search volume is worth targeting?
For local services, even 20 to 50 searches a month can be valuable if the intent is to buy.

### How many keywords should one page target?
One main keyword and a few close variations. Google understands synonyms.

=== how-to-write-a-blog-post-that-ranks
title: How to Write a Blog Post That Ranks on Google
desc: A step-by-step method for writing blog posts that rank and bring in leads: choosing topics, structure, headings, on-page SEO, internal links and updates.
cat: seo
kw: how to write a blog post that ranks, seo blog writing, blog post seo, write seo content
takeaways: Start from a question your customers actually search | Answer it early, then go deeper with clear headings | Demonstrate real experience with examples, numbers and specifics | End with a next step so readers can become leads
---
Most business blog posts get no traffic. Not because blogging doesn't work, but because the posts are written for the business ("Our new office!") and not for the searcher. A post that ranks does one thing: it answers a specific question better than what's already on page one.

Here's the method we use, including for the article you're reading.

## 1. Choose a topic people search for

Pick one question or problem.

- Ask your sales team or check your inbox: what do customers ask?
- Use Google autocomplete and "People also ask"
- Check Search Console for queries you almost rank for

See [keyword research for small businesses](/blog/keyword-research-small-business/).

## 2. Check what's ranking

Search the phrase. Look at the top five results.

- What format are they? Guides, lists, comparisons?
- What do they cover?
- What's missing, outdated or vague?

Your goal is to be more useful, not longer.

## 3. Match the intent

If the top results are step-by-step guides, write a guide. If they're lists of examples, write a list. Google is showing you what searchers want.

## 4. Write a clear title

Include the phrase people search, near the front. Make a promise.

- "How Much Does a Website Cost for a Small Business in 2026?"
- "15 Must-Haves for a Coaching Website"

Under 60 characters where possible. See [title tags and meta descriptions](/blog/title-tags-meta-descriptions/).

## 5. Answer early

Open with a short introduction that confirms the reader is in the right place, then give the direct answer within the first few paragraphs. People, search engines and AI assistants all reward this.

## 6. Structure with headings

- One H1 (the title)
- H2s for main sections, written as the questions or steps readers have
- H3s for sub-points

A reader should grasp the whole article by skimming the headings.

## 7. Make it easy to read

- Short paragraphs
- Bullet points and numbered steps
- Tables for comparisons
- Bold for key phrases
- Plain words

## 8. Show real experience

This is what separates content that ranks from generic filler.

- Use your own examples and client stories
- Include real numbers and ranges
- Add your opinion and say why
- Show photos or screenshots of your work

Google's quality guidelines emphasise experience, expertise, authoritativeness and trust. First-hand detail demonstrates all four.

## 9. Add a key takeaways box and FAQs

A short summary at the top helps skimmers and gives AI tools a clean extract. Three to five FAQs at the end capture related searches. Mark them up with FAQ schema ([guide](/blog/schema-markup-guide/)).

## 10. Link internally

Link to related articles and to the service page the topic relates to. It helps readers and spreads authority around your site. See our [internal linking guide](/blog/internal-linking-guide/).

## 11. Optimise images

Compress them, give them descriptive file names and write alt text. See [image SEO](/blog/image-seo/).

## 12. End with a next step

Every post should lead somewhere: a consultation, a quote, a download, a related guide. Traffic that doesn't convert is just a number.

[[cta]]

## 13. Publish, then promote

- Share on your social channels and in your newsletter
- Send to anyone mentioned in it
- Add links to it from older related posts

## 14. Update it

Check your top posts every six to twelve months. Refresh facts, add new examples and update the date. Updated content often jumps in rankings.

## How long should a post be?

As long as it takes to answer the question fully and no longer. For most small business topics that's 800 to 1,500 words.

## Should you use AI to write?

AI is useful for outlines and first drafts. Published as-is, it tends to be generic and identical to everyone else's. Add your experience, examples and voice, and check every fact. Google's guidance is about helpfulness, not how the text was produced; unhelpful content at scale is what gets penalised.

## A simple template

1. Title with the search phrase
2. Two or three sentence intro
3. Key takeaways
4. Direct answer
5. Detailed sections with H2s
6. Examples
7. Call to action
8. FAQs

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should I publish?
Consistency beats frequency. One strong post a month is enough for most small businesses.

### How long until a post ranks?
Typically a few weeks to a few months, depending on competition and your site's authority.

### Should I write about topics outside my services?
Stay close to what you sell. Relevance builds authority in your niche.

=== internal-linking-guide
title: Internal Linking: The Simple SEO Tactic Most Small Sites Ignore
desc: What internal links are, why they matter for SEO and conversions, and a practical method for adding them across a small business website.
cat: seo
kw: internal linking, internal links seo, website linking strategy, anchor text
takeaways: Internal links help search engines discover pages and understand which matter most | Descriptive anchor text tells Google what the linked page is about | Link from high-traffic pages to the pages you want to rank | Every important page should be linked from several others
---
Internal links are links from one page on your site to another. They're free, entirely in your control and take minutes to add. They're also one of the most neglected parts of small business SEO.

## Why they matter

### Discovery

Search engines find pages by following links. A page with no internal links pointing to it (an orphan) may never be crawled.

### Understanding

The words you link with (anchor text) tell Google what the destination page is about.

### Authority

Links pass value. Your homepage usually has the most authority; linking from it to key pages shares that strength.

### Visitors

Good links guide people to the next relevant thing, keeping them on your site and moving them towards an enquiry.

## Types of internal links

- **Navigation:** main menu and footer
- **Contextual:** links within the body text
- **Breadcrumbs:** Home > Services > Boiler Repair
- **Related content:** "You might also like"
- **Calls to action:** buttons to contact or booking pages

Contextual links inside your content carry the most weight.

## How to do it well

### 1. Use descriptive anchor text

Bad: "Click here to learn more."

Good: "See our guide to [local SEO for small businesses](/blog/local-seo-guide/)."

The reader and Google both know what's behind the link.

### 2. Link to your most important pages most often

Decide which pages matter most (usually service pages and your contact or booking page) and make sure they receive links from many other pages.

### 3. Link from strong pages

In Search Console or analytics, find your most visited pages. Add links from them to pages you want to boost.

### 4. Connect related content

Group pages into topics. A solar installer might have a main "solar panels" page linked to and from pages on batteries, grants, costs and FAQs. This "hub and spoke" structure shows depth on a topic.

### 5. Add links when you publish

Each time you add a page or post:

- Link out from it to three to five relevant existing pages
- Go back to two or three older pages and add links to the new one

### 6. Keep it natural

Link where it genuinely helps the reader. A few useful links per section is plenty.

## A 30-minute internal linking audit

1. List your five most important pages
2. For each, search your site (`site:yourdomain.com "keyword"`) to find pages that mention the topic
3. Add a link from those mentions to the important page
4. Check that every page in your sitemap is linked from at least one other
5. Fix any broken internal links

[[audit]]

## Common mistakes

- **Orphan pages** nobody links to
- **Generic anchors:** "here", "read more"
- **Same anchor text for different pages,** which confuses search engines
- **Too many links** in one paragraph
- **Links only in the menu,** none in content
- **Broken links** after URL changes

## Internal links and conversions

Don't only link for SEO. Each article should point to the next step: the relevant service, a case study, a booking page. That's how readers become leads. See [how to get more leads from your website](/blog/website-conversion-rate-optimization/).

## Site structure tips

- Keep important pages within three clicks of the homepage
- Use clear categories
- Add breadcrumbs, with breadcrumb schema ([guide](/blog/schema-markup-guide/))
- Put your key pages in the footer

## Frequently asked questions

### How many internal links should a page have?
There's no fixed number. Include as many as are genuinely useful; most articles have five to fifteen.

### Should internal links open in a new tab?
Generally no. Keep visitors in the same tab for internal links.

### Does anchor text need to be the exact keyword?
No. Use natural, descriptive variations.
