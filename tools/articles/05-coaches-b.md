Your about page is usually the second most-visited page on your site. People go there with one question: *can I trust this person with something that matters to me?* Most about pages answer a different question: *what has this person done since university?*

Here's a structure that answers the right one.

## The five-part structure

### 1. Start with them

Open with a line or two that shows you understand where your reader is.

> "You've done everything right. Good job, good salary, and a quiet feeling on Sunday evenings that this can't be it."

Now they're listening.

### 2. Introduce yourself as the guide

One or two sentences: who you are and who you help. "I'm Maria, a career transition coach. I help tech professionals move into work that feels like flow."

### 3. Tell the relevant part of your story

Not your biography; the part that explains why you do this work. Usually that's a turning point:

- A moment you were in your client's shoes
- What you tried that didn't work
- What changed
- Why you now help others through the same thing

Keep it honest and specific. A real detail ("I cried in a car park before a board meeting") is worth ten abstract sentences.

### 4. Show your credibility

Now bring in the evidence: certifications, training, years of experience, number of clients, notable organisations, media features. A short list or a row of logos works well.

### 5. Invite the next step

End with what to do now: "If this sounds familiar, let's talk. Book a free 30-minute call." Add the button.

## Voice: write like you speak

Write in first person, in the tone you'd use on a discovery call. Read it aloud. If you wouldn't say a sentence to a client's face, rewrite it.

Avoid:

- "Passionate about empowering individuals to unlock their potential"
- Long lists of every course you've taken
- Third-person bios ("Jane is a certified...") unless it's a separate speaker bio

## Photos

Include at least two: a professional portrait and something more relaxed that shows your personality. Clients are choosing a person; let them see one.

## Add a few human details

A short "outside of coaching" section makes you relatable: where you live, what you love, a quirk or two. Three lines is enough.

## A fill-in-the-blanks template

> **[Line that names your client's situation.]** // I'm [name], a [type] coach. I help [who] to [outcome]. // [Two or three short paragraphs: your turning point and what you learned.] // Today I've worked with [number/type of clients] and trained with [credentials]. // [One line on your approach or method.] // Away from work you'll find me [human detail]. // **Ready to talk? [Book a free call.]**

## Common mistakes

- **Starting with "I".** Start with "you".
- **The full CV.** Nobody needs your 2009 job title.
- **No photo.**
- **No call to action.** An about page with no next step is a dead end.
- **Hiding it.** Link to it from your homepage and main menu.

## A separate speaker bio

If you speak or get quoted in the press, add a short third-person bio at the bottom of the page (50 and 150-word versions) with a downloadable headshot. Event organisers will thank you.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should a coaching about page be?
Roughly 300 to 600 words. Long enough to tell a real story, short enough to read on a phone.

### Should I share personal struggles?
Share what's relevant and what you're comfortable having public. The aim is to show you understand, not to overshare.

### Do I need professional photos?
They're one of the best investments you can make for a coaching site. A two-hour shoot gives you images for the whole website and a year of social posts.

Every coaching website is, underneath, a funnel with one output: a booked call with a good-fit prospect. Each unnecessary step between "this person gets me" and "I'm in your calendar" loses a share of people. Here's how to build the path so fewer drop out.

## The five stages

1. **Arrive.** From Instagram, LinkedIn, Google or a referral.
2. **Recognise.** "This is for someone like me."
3. **Trust.** Proof that you're credible and get results.
4. **Decide.** A clear, low-risk invitation.
5. **Book.** A time in the diary within a minute.

Your website's job is to move people through each stage without friction.

## Stage 1 and 2: the first screen

The top of your homepage needs a client-focused headline, one supporting sentence and a booking button. If visitors have to scroll to find out what you do, many won't. See [coaching website copywriting](/blog/coach-website-copywriting/).

## Stage 3: trust

Place proof before you ask for the booking: testimonials, credentials, a short version of your story. Then ask again.

## Stage 4: frame the call

People hesitate because they fear a sales pitch. Remove the fear by saying exactly what happens:

- **Name it.** "First Step call", "Clarity call".
- **Say how long.** 20 or 30 minutes.
- **Say what they'll get.** "We'll talk through where you are, where you'd like to be, and whether coaching is the right fit."
- **Say what it isn't.** "No pressure, no obligation."

## Stage 5: the booking page

### Embed the calendar

Use Calendly, TidyCal, Acuity, Cal.com or Google Calendar appointment schedules, embedded on your own page. Sending people to "email me your availability" can lose half of them.

### Ask the right questions

Two or three is the sweet spot:

1. What would you most like help with?
2. What have you tried so far?
3. How did you hear about me?

These prepare you for the call and screen out poor fits without feeling like an application.

### Keep the page clean

Remove the main navigation distractions. Show a short reminder of what the call is, a testimonial, and the calendar.

[[cta]]

## After they book

This is where many coaches lose the sale they just earned.

- **Instant confirmation** with a calendar invite and video link
- **A welcome email** with a short note from you and, ideally, one thing to think about before the call
- **Reminders** 24 hours and 1 hour before, by email and SMS if your tool supports it
- **An easy reschedule link**

Automated reminders are the single biggest lever for reducing no-shows.

## For visitors who aren't ready

Most people won't book on the first visit. Give them a smaller step:

- A [lead magnet](/blog/lead-magnets-for-coaches/) in exchange for an email
- A short email series that shares your thinking
- A gentle invitation to book at the end of each email

## Measure it

Track three numbers each month:

| Metric | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| Visitors to booking page | Is the site sending people there? |
| Bookings ÷ booking page visitors | Is the booking page convincing? |
| Clients ÷ calls held | Is the call converting? |

If lots of people reach the booking page but few book, simplify the page. If few reach it, your homepage and calls to action need work.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should the discovery call be free?
For most coaches, yes. It lowers the barrier and lets both sides check fit. Some established coaches charge a small fee to filter for commitment.

### How long should it be?
Twenty to thirty minutes is enough to understand the situation and explain how you work.

### Which booking tool is best?
Any of the mainstream ones work. Choose the one that syncs with your calendar, sends reminders and embeds cleanly on your site.

title: 20 Lead Magnet Ideas for Coaches (That People Actually Want)

Most people who visit your coaching website aren't ready to book a call today. A lead magnet gives them a useful first step and gives you permission to keep in touch until they are.

The best ones share three traits: they're specific, they're quick to consume, and they give a real result.

## Quick-win formats

1. **Checklist.** "The 10-point Sunday reset for a calmer week."
2. **One-page worksheet.** "Find your top five values in ten minutes."
3. **Script or template.** "Three ways to say no without feeling guilty."
4. **Cheat sheet.** "20 questions to ask yourself before changing careers."
5. **Journal prompts.** "Seven prompts for the week after a breakup."

## Interactive formats

6. **Quiz.** "What's your leadership style?" Quizzes convert well because people love learning about themselves.
7. **Self-assessment scorecard.** "Rate your work-life balance across eight areas."
8. **Wheel of life.** A classic coaching tool, made interactive or printable.
9. **Calculator.** "What is your burnout costing you?"

## Deeper formats

10. **Short email course.** Five days, one small action a day.
11. **Mini video training.** 15 minutes on one problem.
12. **Guided audio.** A meditation, visualisation or pep talk.
13. **Workbook.** A longer guided exercise around your method.
14. **Webinar or masterclass replay.**
15. **First chapter of your book.**

## Niche-specific ideas

16. **Career coaches:** a CV or LinkedIn headline template, or a "should I stay or go?" decision guide.
17. **Executive coaches:** a 90-day plan for new leaders, or a one-to-one meeting template.
18. **Wellness coaches:** a seven-day energy tracker or simple meal planner.
19. **Relationship coaches:** conversation starters for couples.
20. **Business coaches:** a pricing calculator or weekly planning template.

## How to choose

Ask: what's the first small problem my ideal client wants solved, the one that sits just before the bigger problem I'm paid to solve? Your lead magnet should fix that small thing and reveal the bigger one.

A career coach's checklist on "signs it's time to leave your job" naturally leads to a call about what to do next.

## Where to put it on your website

- **In the hero** as a secondary button under "Book a call"
- **A dedicated section** halfway down the homepage
- **At the end of every blog post**
- **In the footer**
- **A dedicated landing page** you can link to from social

Keep the form to first name and email. Every extra field lowers sign-ups.

[[cta]]

## What happens after they sign up

The download is the start, not the goal. Set up a short welcome sequence:

1. **Immediately:** deliver the resource and say who you are.
2. **Day 2:** a quick tip that builds on it.
3. **Day 4:** a client story.
4. **Day 6:** common mistakes people make with this problem.
5. **Day 8:** an invitation to book a discovery call.

Then add them to your regular newsletter. For the full journey, see [the discovery call funnel](/blog/discovery-call-booking-funnel/).

## Make it look good

A scruffy PDF undermines a premium service. Use your brand fonts and colours, keep layouts clean and add your photo and booking link on the last page.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Too long.** A 40-page ebook rarely gets read.
- **Too broad.** "A guide to a better life."
- **No follow-up.**
- **Asking for a phone number** on the form.
- **Hiding it** on one page.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do lead magnets still work?
Yes, when they're genuinely useful. People are more selective about giving their email, so quality matters more than ever.

### What tool do I need?
An email platform such as MailerLite, Kit (ConvertKit), Flodesk or Mailchimp. They provide the form, delivery and automated emails.

### How many lead magnets should I have?
Start with one strong one. Add more later for different audiences or blog topics.

Most coaches rely on referrals and social media. Both are valuable, and both stop when you do. Search is different: a page that ranks keeps bringing in prospects month after month while you're busy coaching.

You don't need to become an SEO expert. You need to do a handful of things well.

## How people search for coaches

Searches fall into three groups:

- **Service + location:** "life coach Denver", "executive coach London"
- **Service + niche:** "career coach for tech workers", "coach for women in leadership", "divorce coach online"
- **Problem-based:** "how to change careers at 40", "feeling stuck in life what to do"

The first two are people ready to hire. The third are people earlier in the journey who may hire later.

## Step 1: one page per service

If you offer life coaching, career coaching and corporate workshops, each needs its own page. One page can't rank for three different things.

On each page include:

- The service and audience in the page title and main heading
- Who it's for and the problems it solves
- How it works
- Testimonials specific to that service
- FAQs
- A booking call to action

Read [title tags and meta descriptions](/blog/title-tags-meta-descriptions/) to get the basics right.

## Step 2: claim your location

Even if you work on Zoom, many clients prefer someone "local". Mention your city and region on your homepage, contact page and footer, and set up a Google Business Profile. You can hide your home address and list a service area. Our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization/) walks through it.

Then ask happy clients for Google reviews. They influence both rankings and trust.

## Step 3: write what your clients are Googling

List the ten questions you hear most on discovery calls. Each is an article:

- "How do I know if I need a career coach?"
- "What happens in a life coaching session?"
- "How much does executive coaching cost?"
- "Life coach vs therapist: what's the difference?"

Answer each clearly in 800 to 1,500 words, with a call to action at the end. One good article a month is enough. See [how to write a blog post that ranks](/blog/how-to-write-a-blog-post-that-ranks/).

## Step 4: get the technical basics right

- **Speed:** fast on mobile
- **Mobile-friendly:** obviously
- **HTTPS:** the padlock
- **Clear structure:** one H1 per page, logical headings
- **Schema markup:** tells Google you're a professional service with reviews and FAQs

Our [technical SEO checklist](/blog/technical-seo-checklist/) covers these in plain English.

## Step 5: earn a few good links

Links from other sites tell Google you're trusted. Good sources for coaches:

- Your certifying body's directory (ICF, EMCC)
- Podcast guest appearances
- Guest articles for industry publications
- Local business associations
- Collaborations with therapists, HR consultants and other coaches

## Getting found in AI search

People now ask ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's AI Overviews for recommendations: "who's a good career coach for engineers in Vancouver?" These tools pull from clear, specific, well-structured web pages.

To be included: state plainly who you help, where and how; use question-style headings with direct answers; keep your details consistent across your site, LinkedIn and directories. We cover this in depth in [how to get mentioned in AI search](/blog/ai-search-optimization/).

## How long will it take?

Expect brand searches (your name) to work within weeks, niche and local terms within three to six months, and competitive terms longer. SEO compounds: the work you do this quarter keeps paying next year. More in [how long does SEO take?](/blog/how-long-does-seo-take/)

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I rank if I only coach online?
Yes. Target niche terms ("online career coach for nurses") and still claim your home city for local searches.

### Is SEO worth it for a solo coach?
If one new client is worth $1,500 or more, even one extra enquiry a month from Google is a strong return.

### Do I need to blog every week?
No. One genuinely helpful article a month beats four thin ones.

Coaching is invisible. There's no product photo, no before-and-after picture. Testimonials are how prospects see the result before they buy, which makes them the most persuasive content on your site.

## What makes a testimonial persuasive

Compare:

> "Sarah is an amazing coach. I highly recommend her!"

> "I'd been in the same role for nine years and couldn't see a way out. Within three months of working with Sarah I'd landed a director role at a company I love, and I negotiated a 25% raise."

The second has the three ingredients that matter:

1. **Before:** the situation and how it felt
2. **After:** the specific result
3. **Bridge:** what the coaching did

## How to ask

Don't ask for "a testimonial". Ask questions that produce the story:

1. What was going on for you before we started working together?
2. What made you hesitate about coaching?
3. What changed as a result of our work?
4. What specifically was most helpful?
5. Who would you recommend this to?

Send them by email, or ask on your final session and offer to write it up for the client to approve. Most people are happy to help and relieved not to face a blank page.

**When to ask:** right after a win, at the end of a programme, or when a client says something lovely unprompted ("Would you mind if I quoted that?").

## Permission and confidentiality

Coaching is private. Always:

- Get written permission before publishing
- Offer options: full name and photo, first name and role, or anonymous ("Marketing Director, tech company")
- Let clients approve the final wording
- Remove a testimonial promptly if asked

Named testimonials with photos are most powerful, but an honest anonymous one beats none.

## Formats

- **Short quotes** for scattering across pages
- **Mini case studies** (150 to 300 words) for programme pages
- **Video testimonials:** highly convincing and reusable on social
- **Screenshots** of messages, with permission
- **Google reviews** for extra credibility and SEO

Real messages can be especially convincing because nobody wrote them for marketing. One of our own favourite pieces of feedback is a WhatsApp from a client that simply says: "I got a lot of compliments on it."

## Where to put them

Not all on one page that few people visit.

- **Homepage:** two or three of your best, near the top
- **Each programme page:** testimonials from people who took that programme
- **Next to every booking button**
- **About page:** one that speaks to what you're like to work with
- **Booking page:** to reassure at the moment of decision
- **A dedicated page** for those who want more

[[cta]]

## Match testimonials to objections

List the reasons people hesitate: "it's expensive", "I've tried coaching before", "I don't have time". Find a testimonial that answers each and place it where that doubt arises, such as a "worth every penny" quote next to your pricing.

## Add specifics

Wherever possible include name, role, location, programme and a photo. Details make a testimonial believable.

## Keep them fresh

Add new testimonials every quarter. Dates aren't essential, but a wall of quotes that hasn't changed in four years looks stale.

## Mark them up for Google

Review schema can help search engines understand your testimonials. Use it honestly and only for genuine reviews. See our [schema markup guide](/blog/schema-markup-guide/).

## Frequently asked questions

### What if I'm a new coach with no testimonials?
Offer a few pro bono or discounted sessions in exchange for honest feedback. Character references from colleagues can also help while you build.

### Can I edit a testimonial?
You can tidy grammar and shorten it, but never change the meaning, and always get the client's approval of the final version.

### How many should I show?
Three to five strong ones on the homepage is plenty. Quality over quantity.
