---
An interior designer's website is judged twice: once for the work it shows and once for how the site itself looks and feels. If the typography is clumsy or the layout crowded, visitors assume your interiors are too. The site is part of the portfolio.

## Lead with your best room

One beautiful image, a line about your style and where you work, and a clear way to enquire. "Rooms that feel like you, only better."

## Project pages, not just a gallery

A grid of images shows taste. Project pages show thinking. For each project include:

- Project type, location and scope
- The brief and the client's goals
- Your concept and key decisions
- 10 to 20 photographs, including details
- Before and afters where you have them
- Floor plans, mood boards or sketches
- Credits for trades and suppliers
- A client quote

Five to ten strong projects beat thirty thin ones.

## Explain your services

Clients often don't know what an interior designer actually does or how fees work.

- Full-service design
- Design consultation
- E-design or remote packages
- Kitchen and bathroom design
- Commercial and hospitality
- Styling and staging

For each: what's included, who it suits and how it's priced (fixed fee, percentage, hourly).

## Show your process

Four or five steps from first conversation to installation: consultation, concept, design development, procurement, installation. It reassures clients that there's a plan.

## Pricing signals

You may not publish fees, but indicate scale: "Full-service projects typically begin at..." or "We work on projects with budgets from...". It filters enquiries and saves awkward conversations.

## A qualifying enquiry form

Ask:

- Project type and location
- Scope (rooms or square footage)
- Timeline
- Approximate budget range
- How they found you
- A few lines about what they want

Budget dropdowns feel less intrusive than an open box. See [contact forms that convert](/blog/contact-form-best-practices/).

## About you

Your background, philosophy and a good portrait. Clients invite you into their homes; they want to know who you are.

## Press and recognition

Publications, awards and features, shown as logos.

## Design principles for a designer's site

- **Generous white space**
- **Refined typography**
- **Large, high-quality images,** optimised for speed ([image SEO](/blog/image-seo/))
- **Restrained colour** that lets the work lead
- **Subtle motion**

## SEO for interior designers

- "Interior designer [city]" in titles and headings
- Project pages naming the neighbourhood and property type
- A Google Business Profile with project photos
- Articles answering client questions: "how much does an interior designer cost?", "how long does a renovation take?"
- Pinterest, which drives significant traffic in this field

See the [local SEO guide](/blog/local-seo-guide/).

If you sell furniture or homewares, a catalog with filters and checkout can sit alongside the portfolio. Our Velmora furniture design shows how a storefront can still feel editorial.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many projects should I show?
Five to ten of your best, each with its own page.

### Should I include prices?
A starting point or typical range helps attract clients with realistic budgets.

### Do I need professional photography?
Yes. Interior photography is a specialism, and it's the single best investment for your portfolio.

=== school-education-website
title: Website Design for Schools, Academies and Learning Centres

---
An education website serves several audiences at once: parents comparing options, students checking what they'll learn, schools or employers looking for a provider, and existing families looking for dates and forms. The best ones give each group a short, obvious path.

## Start with a clear promise

Who do you teach and what do they gain?

- "A skills studio where students build" (hands-on robotics and AI for Grades 6 to 12)
- "Get your whole school moving" (curriculum-aligned dance programmes)

One line that a parent understands in three seconds.

## Programmes

Each programme or course needs its own page:

- Who it's for (age, grade, level)
- What students learn and make
- Schedule, duration and location
- Teachers
- Outcomes: skills, certificates, progression
- Fees
- How to enrol

A comparison table helps families choose between options.

[[work:vinc-lab,dancecart]]

## Enrolment and payment

Make signing up as easy as buying a ticket.

- Online enrolment forms
- Secure payment with Stripe or your local provider
- Automatic confirmation and receipts
- Free trial or demo class booking
- Waitlists for full classes

Dance Cart, for example, lets schools browse programmes, enrol and pay online. See [accepting payments on your website](/blog/stripe-payments-website/).

## Trust

- **Teachers:** photos, qualifications, registration
- **Safeguarding** and child protection policies
- **Accreditation** and curriculum alignment
- **Results and testimonials** from parents, students and schools
- **Photos and video** of real classes
- **Partners** and affiliations

## Paths for different audiences

Use clear top-level navigation such as:

- For parents
- For schools
- For students
- For undergraduates / professionals

Each leads to relevant programmes and a tailored enquiry form. STEAM-IE and VINC both organise content this way.

## Practical information

- Term dates and timetables
- Location, transport and parking
- FAQs: what to bring, class sizes, refunds, catch-up classes
- Contact details and office hours

[[cta]]

## Content that builds authority

- Student projects and showcases
- Event recaps
- Articles for parents: "how to get kids interested in coding", "why dance matters in primary school"
- Free resources for teachers

These bring search traffic and give parents confidence. See [how to write a blog post that ranks](/blog/how-to-write-a-blog-post-that-ranks/).

## Local SEO

- "Robotics classes [city]", "tutoring [suburb]" in titles
- Google Business Profile with photos and reviews
- EducationalOrganization and Course schema ([guide](/blog/schema-markup-guide/))

## Design

Friendly and energetic for younger audiences; calm and credible for professional training. Always accessible, readable and fast on phones. See [web accessibility basics](/blog/website-accessibility-basics/).

## Portals and platforms

Larger providers may need student logins, learning management or credentialing. That's custom software territory; see our [software development service](/services/software-development/).

[[work:steamie,vinc-lab]]

## Frequently asked questions

### Can parents pay fees online?
Yes, through integrated payment at enrolment or via invoices with a pay link.

### How do we handle multiple campuses?
Give each its own page with address, timetable and staff, and let families filter programmes by location.

### What's the most important page?
The programme pages. They're where families decide.

=== manufacturer-website-b2b
title: Websites for Manufacturers: Turning Technical Buyers into B2B Leads
desc: How manufacturers and industrial suppliers can use their website to generate quote requests: capabilities, product catalogs, proof of quality and RFQ forms.
cat: local
kw: manufacturer website, industrial website design, b2b manufacturing lead generation, product catalog website
takeaways: Engineers and buyers want specifications, capabilities and proof of quality | An easy request-for-quote flow with file upload is the key conversion | Certifications, tolerances and case studies build confidence | Product and capability pages bring in specific, high-value searches
---
Industrial buyers do most of their research online before speaking to a supplier. They're looking for evidence: can you make this part, to this tolerance, in this material, at this volume, by this date? A manufacturing website that answers those questions clearly wins the request for quote.

## Who's visiting

- **Engineers** checking capabilities and tolerances
- **Procurement** comparing suppliers, certifications and lead times
- **Business owners** looking for a product or partner

Give each what they need without making them dig.

## Capabilities

Create a page for each process or capability: CNC machining, sheet metal, injection moulding, fabrication, assembly, finishing.

Include:

- Equipment list
- Materials handled
- Tolerances and size ranges
- Volumes: prototype to production
- Lead times
- Industries served
- Photos and video of the shop floor

## Product catalog

If you sell products, a structured catalog helps buyers and search engines.

- Categories and filters
- Specification tables
- Downloadable datasheets and CAD files
- Clear photos from several angles
- "Request a quote" or "Enquire" on every product

IB Refrigeration presents display counters, food carts and commercial kitchen equipment this way, with reviews and years in business up front.

[[work:ibrefrigeration,tpl-manufacturing]]

## The RFQ form

This is your primary conversion.

- Name, company, email, phone
- Part or product description
- Quantity and timeline
- **File upload** for drawings and CAD (STEP, DXF, PDF)
- NDA option

Promise a response time ("quotes within 24 hours") and keep it.

## Proof of quality

- **Certifications:** ISO 9001, AS9100, ISO 13485 and others relevant to your sector
- **Inspection capability:** CMM, reports, traceability
- **Statistics:** on-time delivery, parts shipped, years operating
- **Case studies:** problem, solution, result
- **Client logos,** with permission

## Industries

Pages for the sectors you serve (aerospace, medical, automotive, food service, energy) with relevant experience and standards.

[[cta]]

## Content for technical buyers

- Design-for-manufacturing guides
- Material selection guides
- Tolerance and finish charts
- Process comparisons

Useful technical content ranks for specific searches and demonstrates expertise.

## SEO for manufacturers

B2B searches are specific and valuable: "5-axis CNC machining titanium", "commercial display counter manufacturer [city]".

- A page per capability and product category
- Specifications in text, not only in PDFs
- Location pages if you serve regional markets
- Product schema for catalog items ([guide](/blog/schema-markup-guide/))

## Design

Industrial doesn't have to mean dated. A precise, modern design signals a precise, modern operation. Use real photography from your facility, not stock images of gears.

## Useful features

- Live chat or WhatsApp for quick questions
- Order or production status tracking for customers
- Distributor or dealer locator
- Multilingual content for export markets

## A ready-made option

Torvane Manufacturing, our CNC and manufacturing site with a CAD-upload quote flow, is available on the [templates page](/templates/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Should manufacturers show prices?
For standard products, guide prices help. For custom work, a fast, easy quote process matters more.

### How important is mobile for B2B?
More than you'd think. Buyers research on phones and tablets, and Google indexes the mobile version of your site.

### What converts best?
Clear capabilities, visible certifications and a simple RFQ form with file upload.

=== fitness-personal-trainer-website
title: Personal Trainer and Fitness Coach Website Guide
desc: How personal trainers and fitness coaches can build a website that wins clients: clear offers, transformations, booking, online programmes and local SEO.
cat: local
kw: personal trainer website, fitness coach website, fitness website design, online coaching website
takeaways: Lead with the result your clients want, not your certifications | Transformations and testimonials are your strongest proof | Offer one clear first step, such as a free consultation or trial session | Online programmes and memberships can be sold directly from your site
---
Personal training is sold on belief: the client has to believe change is possible for them and that you're the person to get them there. Your website builds that belief with proof, personality and a simple way to start.

## Lead with the outcome

"Certified personal trainer with ten years' experience" is about you. "Build a strong body at any age" is about them. Open with the result, then say who you help: busy professionals, new mums, over-50s, athletes.

## Show transformations

- Before-and-after photos (with consent)
- Client stories: where they started, what changed, how they feel
- Numbers where meaningful: strength gained, events completed
- Video testimonials

Be honest about typical results and timeframes.

## Your offers

Present clearly:

- One-to-one training
- Small group sessions
- Online coaching
- Nutrition coaching
- Programmes or challenges
- Memberships

For each: who it's for, what's included, how often, price and how to start. See [how to write a coaching packages page](/blog/coaching-packages-page/) for structure.

## One clear first step

Offer a free consultation, movement assessment or trial session, with an embedded calendar. Make it the main button across the site.

## About you

Your story, why you train people, your qualifications and a few photos that show your personality. Fitness is personal; people buy the trainer.

A strong personal brand can carry an entire site. Lauren Powers, a ten-time bodybuilding champion, TV host and author, leads with a bold identity and message.

## Selling online

If you coach remotely, your site can:

- Take payment for programmes and subscriptions
- Deliver plans through an app or member area
- Collect check-ins
- Run challenges with leaderboards

See [accepting payments on your website](/blog/stripe-payments-website/).

## Lead magnets

- A free seven-day workout plan
- A home workout guide
- A meal prep template
- A mobility routine video

Capture emails and follow up with useful tips and an invitation to book.

[[cta]]

## Local SEO

For in-person trainers:

- "Personal trainer [town]" in titles and headings
- Google Business Profile with photos and reviews ([checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization/))
- Pages for each service and location
- Reviews from clients

See the [local SEO guide](/blog/local-seo-guide/).

## Social proof and content

- Instagram highlights embedded sparingly
- Short articles answering common questions
- Press, podcast or competition features

## Design

Energetic and confident, with strong photography. High contrast, bold type and plenty of movement suit the category, but keep it fast on mobile.

## Legal bits

- Health disclaimer
- Terms for online programmes
- Privacy policy
- Consent for before-and-after images

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I show prices?
Yes, or at least starting prices. It qualifies enquiries and builds trust.

### Do I need a website if I train at a gym?
A website helps you build your own client base, which you keep if you move gyms.

### Can I sell programmes directly from my site?
Yes, with online payment and automated delivery.
