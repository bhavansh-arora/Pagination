People don't hire a life coach the way they buy a product. They hire someone they trust with the parts of their life that aren't working. Your website has to earn that trust quickly, then make the next step (a free call) feel safe and easy.

We've built coaching sites for life, executive, wellness, career and relationship coaches in five countries. This is what the ones that book calls have in common.

## Start with one goal

Almost every successful coaching site is built around a single action: **book a free discovery call.** Not "learn more", not "follow me on Instagram". One call to action, repeated consistently.

Before you design anything, decide what that call is named (First Step call, clarity call, chemistry session), how long it is, and how people book it.

## The homepage, section by section

### 1. A headline about them

Weak: "Certified Life Coach and NLP Practitioner."
Strong: "Feeling stuck? Find your footing and choose your path."

Your visitor is thinking about their own problem. Meet them there. Follow the headline with one sentence on who you help and how, and a booking button.

### 2. Your face

Coaching is personal. A warm, professional photo of you in the hero section does more for trust than any stock image of a sunrise.

### 3. The problem, in their words

Three or four lines that describe how your ideal client feels right now. When someone reads this and thinks "that's me", they keep scrolling.

### 4. The change you offer

What will be different after working with you? Be concrete: "set boundaries without guilt", "make the career move you've been postponing for two years".

### 5. How it works

Three simple steps: book a free call, choose a programme, start. Removing uncertainty about the process removes a major barrier.

### 6. Proof

Testimonials with full names and photos where clients allow it. Results are more persuasive than praise: "I left the job I'd hated for six years" beats "She's a wonderful coach." More in [how to use testimonials on a coaching website](/blog/coaching-testimonials/).

### 7. About you, briefly

A short version of your story with a link to the full [about page](/blog/coach-about-page/). Credentials belong here, as supporting evidence.

### 8. The call to action, again

End with a clear invitation and a booking button.

## The other pages you need

- **About:** your story, told in a way that shows you understand the client's.
- **Work with me:** your programmes and packages. See [how to write a coaching packages page](/blog/coaching-packages-page/).
- **Testimonials:** a fuller collection of client stories.
- **Book a call:** an embedded calendar, not a form that leads to email tennis.
- **Blog or resources:** optional, but powerful for SEO and trust.

## Make booking effortless

Embed a scheduler (Calendly, TidyCal, Acuity or Google Calendar's booking pages) directly on your site. Every extra step between "I'm interested" and "I'm booked" loses people. Ask only what you need: name, email and one question about what they'd like help with.

We map the whole journey in [the discovery call funnel](/blog/discovery-call-booking-funnel/).

## Mistakes we see on coaching websites

- **Leading with qualifications.** Clients care about their outcome first, your training second.
- **Trying to help everyone.** "I help people live their best life" speaks to nobody. See [positioning your coaching niche](/blog/coaching-niche-positioning/).
- **No prices or even a hint.** Mystery reads as expensive or evasive.
- **Walls of text.** Short paragraphs, clear headings, generous space.
- **A hidden booking link.** If it takes more than one click to find, it's hidden.
- **Slow, template-heavy pages.** They look like every other coach's site.

## Don't forget Google

Clients search for coaches: "career coach Vancouver", "life coach for women", "executive coach Auckland". A site with clear service pages, your location and helpful articles can bring in enquiries every month without ads. Start with [SEO for coaches](/blog/seo-for-coaches/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a website if I get clients from Instagram?
Yes. Social platforms are rented space, and serious prospects will look you up. A website is where they decide whether to book.

### Should I put prices on my coaching website?
At least a starting price or range. It filters out poor fits and makes the discovery call more productive.

### How many pages does a coaching website need?
Five is plenty to start: home, about, work with me, testimonials and booking. Add a blog when you're ready to invest in SEO.

Use this as a checklist against your current site. Score yourself honestly: most coaching websites we review are missing at least five.

## 1. A clear niche statement

Within five seconds a visitor should know who you help and with what. "Career transition coaching for tech professionals" tells the right person they're in the right place.

## 2. A client-focused headline

Speak to the feeling or goal your client has today. Save your title and credentials for the about section. For formulas and examples, see [coaching website copywriting](/blog/coach-website-copywriting/).

## 3. A professional photo of you

Not a selfie, not a stock image. A warm, well-lit portrait where you're looking at the camera.

## 4. One primary call to action

"Book a free call" in the header, the hero and the footer. Same wording each time.

## 5. Embedded online booking

A live calendar on your site. No "email me to arrange a time".

## 6. A signature method or framework

Naming your process (a four-step method, a framework with an acronym) makes your coaching tangible and memorable. Lasting Change Wellness, for example, builds its whole site around the S.H.I.N.E. method.

## 7. Clear programmes or packages

What's included, how long it lasts, who it's for. See [how to write a coaching packages page](/blog/coaching-packages-page/).

## 8. Testimonials with names and results

Specific outcomes from real people. Video is even better.

## 9. Credentials and affiliations

ICF, EMCC, certifications, relevant career history. Shown as logos or a short list, supporting your story rather than leading it.

## 10. An about page that connects

Your story, framed around why you understand your clients. See [how to write a coach about page](/blog/coach-about-page/).

## 11. A lead magnet

Most visitors aren't ready to book today. A useful free resource in exchange for an email keeps the conversation going. Ideas in [20 lead magnets for coaches](/blog/lead-magnets-for-coaches/).

## 12. Mobile-first design

Most coaching traffic comes from Instagram and LinkedIn links opened on a phone. Design for the small screen first.

## 13. Fast loading

A slow site loses people before they've read your headline. Aim for under two seconds on mobile.

## 14. SEO foundations

A page per service, your location if you coach locally, proper title tags and a few genuinely helpful articles. Start with [SEO for coaches](/blog/seo-for-coaches/).

## 15. A privacy policy and clear contact details

You're collecting names and emails. A privacy policy is a legal requirement in most places, and visible contact details signal you're a real business.

Executive coaching is sold differently from life coaching. Your buyer is busy, sceptical and often spending the company's money. They may be a leader looking for themselves or an HR director choosing for someone else. Either way, they'll judge you on your website in under a minute.

## Know your two audiences

**The leader** wants to know: do you understand my world, and have you helped people like me?

**The sponsor** (HR, L&D or a board member) wants to know: is this credible, measurable and safe to recommend?

A strong executive coaching site answers both, often with separate pages: "For leaders" and "For organisations".

## Positioning: be specific

"I help leaders reach their potential" is forgettable. Specific positioning wins:

- "Coaching for first-time CEOs in scale-ups"
- "Inclusive leadership for global teams"
- "AI leadership programmes for executive teams"

Specificity is itself a credibility signal. It tells a senior reader you've done this before. More in [positioning your coaching niche](/blog/coaching-niche-positioning/).

## Proof that works at this level

### Client logos

If you've worked with recognisable organisations and have permission, show them near the top.

### Outcome-based case studies

Senior buyers want business impact: retention, engagement scores, promotion readiness, a successful transition. Structure each case as situation, approach, result. Anonymise where needed ("CFO, FTSE 250 retailer").

### Credentials

ICF PCC or MCC, EMCC, an executive education background, board or operating experience. At this level your own leadership history is often your strongest credential.

### Thought leadership

Articles, a TEDx talk, podcast appearances, a book. These show you have a point of view worth paying for.

## Pages an executive coach site needs

- **Home:** positioning, proof and a path for each audience
- **Executive coaching:** how one-to-one engagements work, duration and what's measured
- **Team and organisational programmes**
- **Speaking** with topics, a showreel and a booking enquiry
- **Case studies**
- **About** with career history
- **Insights** (articles)
- **Contact:** a short enquiry form and a calendar link

## Design: premium, restrained, fast

- **Typography carries the brand.** A confident serif or a clean grotesque, used consistently.
- **Photography is editorial**, not corporate stock.
- **White space signals confidence.**
- **Nothing is broken.** A typo or a slow page undermines a premium fee instantly.

## The enquiry path

Executives won't fill in a ten-field form. Offer two routes:

1. **Book a confidential conversation:** an embedded calendar for a 20 to 30 minute call.
2. **Organisation enquiry:** a short form for HR with fields for company, role and what they're looking to solve.

Mention confidentiality explicitly. It matters at this level.

## Should you show pricing?

Most executive coaches don't publish exact fees, because engagements are scoped individually. A line such as "Engagements typically run six months and start from £X" still helps qualify enquiries. For structuring your offers, see [how to write a coaching packages page](/blog/coaching-packages-page/).

## SEO for executive coaches

Senior buyers do search: "executive coach London", "leadership coaching for CTOs", "CEO coach". Service pages, location signals, and articles that answer real leadership questions build visibility over time. See [SEO for coaches](/blog/seo-for-coaches/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Do executive coaches need a blog?
It's one of the best ways to demonstrate thinking. Even one strong article a month gives prospects something to forward to their boss.

### Should I use a personal name or a company brand?
Either works. A personal brand builds trust quickly; a company name is easier to grow beyond yourself. Many coaches use both.

### How important is LinkedIn compared with my website?
LinkedIn starts conversations. Your website closes them. Senior buyers nearly always check both.

title: How to Write a Coaching Packages Page That Sells

Your packages page (often called "Work with me") is where interest turns into intent. Done well, it means people arrive at the discovery call already knowing what they want. Done badly, it's a list of session counts that leaves them more confused than before.

## Sell the outcome, then the logistics

Nobody wants "six 60-minute sessions". They want to stop dreading Monday mornings, lead their team with confidence, or finally make the move.

For each package, lead with:

1. **Who it's for.** "For professionals who know they want out but don't know what's next."
2. **The outcome.** What will be true at the end.
3. **How it works.** Duration, session rhythm, support between sessions.
4. **What's included.** Sessions, assessments, worksheets, message access.
5. **The investment.**
6. **The next step.** Book a free call.

## How many packages?

Two or three. Any more and people freeze. A classic structure:

| Package | For | Typical format |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starter / Clarity | Someone with one specific challenge | 4 sessions over 6 weeks |
| Signature | Your ideal client, your full method | 3 months, fortnightly sessions plus support |
| Premium / VIP | Those who want more access | 6 months, weekly sessions, priority messaging |

Make the middle option the one you'd most like to sell and mark it "most popular". Many people choose the middle.

## Naming your packages

Names should describe a result or a stage of the journey, not a metal. "Find Your Footing", "The Transition", "Lead with Confidence" tell a story that "Bronze, Silver, Gold" doesn't.

## Should you show prices?

There's no universal rule, but here's what we've seen across dozens of coaching sites:

**Show prices if** you want fewer, better-qualified enquiries, and your pricing is fixed.

**Show a "from" price if** engagements vary but you want to filter out people for whom it's far out of reach.

**Hide prices if** you sell mainly to organisations with bespoke scopes. Even then, say so explicitly.

Whatever you choose, never make visitors feel foolish for asking. "Investment is discussed on your free call, with options from $X" is clear and respectful.

## Elements that lift conversions

- **A testimonial under each package** from someone who took that programme
- **An FAQ** covering refunds, rescheduling, online vs in person, and how quickly they can start
- **A "not sure which?" line** pointing to the discovery call
- **Payment options:** pay in full or in instalments
- **A guarantee or clear cancellation policy**

## Example layout

1. Short intro: who you work with and the change you help create
2. Two or three package cards side by side
3. A "how it works" strip: call, choose, start
4. Testimonials
5. FAQ
6. Final call to action

## Common mistakes

- **Describing only the format.** Sessions and minutes, with no mention of results.
- **Too many options.** Hourly, packages, group, VIP days, retreats, all on one page.
- **No call to action** under each package.
- **Jargon.** If a client wouldn't say it, don't write it.
- **Out-of-date prices.** Review the page every quarter.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I offer single sessions?
Usually not as your main offer. Transformation takes time, and single sessions attract people who aren't committed. A paid one-off "intensive" can work as an entry point.

### How do I price coaching packages?
Start from the value of the outcome to your client and what comparable coaches in your niche charge, then test. Raising prices as demand grows is normal.

### Where should the packages page sit in my menu?
In the main navigation, labelled plainly: "Work with me", "Coaching" or "Programmes".
