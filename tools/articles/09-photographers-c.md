takeaways: Your profile is what appears in Google's map results; your website alone won't get you there | Choose "Wedding photographer" as the primary category | Add fresh photos monthly and reply to every review | You can hide your home address and list a service area
---
Search "wedding photographer near me" and the first thing you see is a map with three businesses. Those results come from Google Business Profiles, not websites. If you haven't set yours up, or set it up once and forgot it, you're invisible in the most valuable spot on the page.

It's free and takes about an hour to do properly.

## Step 1: create or claim your profile

Go to [google.com/business](https://www.google.com/business/). Search for your business name first; a listing may already exist. Create or claim it, then verify (usually by video or postcard).

## Step 2: get the basics exactly right

- **Business name:** your real trading name. Don't stuff keywords in; Google can suspend profiles for that.
- **Primary category:** "Wedding photographer". Add secondary categories such as "Photographer", "Portrait studio" or "Video production service" if they apply.
- **Service area:** if you work from home, hide your address and add the cities and regions you cover.
- **Phone and website:** match exactly what's on your site.
- **Hours:** when you answer enquiries.

Consistency of name, address and phone across the web matters for local rankings. More in our [local SEO guide](/blog/local-seo-guide/).

## Step 3: write a strong description

You have 750 characters. Lead with what you do and where:

> "Dan Reynolds Photography is a Chicago and Central Illinois wedding photography and film team. We document weddings across Illinois and Wisconsin in a warm, documentary style..."

Mention your style, areas, and what makes you different. Write for couples, not for an algorithm.

## Step 4: add services

List each service with a short description: wedding photography, wedding videography, engagement sessions, elopements. Add starting prices if you're comfortable.

## Step 5: photos, lots of them

Profiles with more photos get more clicks. Upload:

- 20 to 30 of your best wedding images to start
- A logo and a cover image
- A photo of you
- Short video clips

Then add a few new images every month. Fresh activity signals a live business.

## Step 6: reviews

Reviews are the biggest factor you control.

- Ask every couple when you deliver their gallery
- Send your direct review link (find it under "Ask for reviews")
- Reply to every review, warmly and specifically
- Never offer incentives; it's against Google's rules

Tactics in [how wedding vendors can get more reviews](/blog/reviews-for-wedding-vendors/).

[[audit]]

## Step 7: posts and updates

Google lets you publish short posts. Share a recent wedding, a blog post or an available date once or twice a month. They take five minutes and show activity.

## Step 8: questions and messages

Seed the Q&A with common questions ("Do you travel?", "How far in advance should we book?") and answer them. Turn on messaging only if you'll reply promptly.

## Step 9: link it to a strong website

Your profile and website reinforce each other. Make sure your site:

- States your location in the title and headline
- Has matching contact details in the footer
- Includes local business schema ([schema guide](/blog/schema-markup-guide/))
- Has pages for the areas you serve

## What moves you up the map

Google says local ranking depends on relevance, distance and prominence. In practice:

| Factor | What you can do |
| --- | --- |
| Relevance | Right categories, complete services, good description |
| Distance | Accurate service areas |
| Prominence | Reviews, photos, website quality, links |

## Common mistakes

- Keyword-stuffed business names
- A profile with five photos from 2021
- Ignoring reviews
- Different phone numbers on the profile and website
- Duplicate listings

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a physical studio?
No. Service-area businesses are fully supported. Hide your address and list where you work.

### How many reviews do I need?
More than your nearest competitors, with a steady flow of new ones. Recency matters as much as total count.

### Can I have profiles in several cities?
Only where you have a genuine, staffed location. Use service areas and location pages on your site for the rest.

=== wedding-photographer-about-page
title: Writing a Wedding Photographer About Page Couples Connect With
desc: How to write an about page for a wedding photography or videography business that shows your personality, builds trust and leads couples to enquire.
cat: photographers
kw: wedding photographer about page, photographer bio, about page photography website, how to write photographer about me
takeaways: Couples are choosing a person to spend their wedding day with, so show them one | Lead with how you work and what it feels like, then your story | Use real photos of you, ideally at work | End with an invitation to get in touch
---
A couple can find a hundred photographers whose work they like. They book the one they'd be happy to have beside them for twelve hours on the most emotional day of their lives. Your about page is where that choice gets made.

## What couples want to know

- What are you like to be around?
- How do you work on the day: directing or blending in?
- Do you understand what matters to us?
- Can we trust you to handle it?

Notice what isn't on the list: your first camera, your gear, or the year you "fell in love with capturing moments".

## A structure that works

### 1. Open with them

> "You're planning a day full of people you love, and you'd rather be with them than posing for hours."

### 2. How you work

Describe your approach in plain terms. "I'll give gentle direction for portraits, then step back and let the day unfold. Most couples tell me they forgot I was there."

### 3. Who you are

Now a little about you: where you're from, how long you've been doing this, why weddings. Keep it warm and brief. If you're a duo, introduce both of you and how you work together.

### 4. Proof you're good at this

Years of experience, number of weddings, awards or features, and a short quote from a couple about what you were like on the day.

### 5. A few human details

Coffee order, favourite film, your dog's name. Three or four lines that give couples something to connect over.

### 6. Invite them in

"I'd love to hear what you're planning. Tell me about your day." Then the button.

## Photos of you

This is non-negotiable. Include at least one clear, friendly portrait and one of you working at a wedding. Couples who can picture you in the room are far more likely to enquire.

## Voice

Write the way you'd talk to a couple over coffee. First person, relaxed, specific. Skip the phrases every photographer uses:

- "I'm passionate about capturing authentic moments"
- "More than just a photographer"
- "Telling your unique love story"

Say something only you could say.

[[cta]]

## For duos and teams

Husband-and-wife teams and studios should explain who does what and who'll be there on the day. Couples worry about booking a brand and getting a stranger. Dan Reynolds Photography, for example, presents photo and film as the work of one close team.

[[work:dan-reynolds-photography,alphavisuals]]

## Add a short "what to expect"

Three steps from enquiry to gallery remove uncertainty:

1. **Say hello.** Tell me your date and plans.
2. **Let's chat.** A relaxed call to see if we're a fit.
3. **Book and relax.** I'll help with timelines and be there from prep to the last dance.

## Common mistakes

- No photo of yourself
- A life story in chronological order
- Gear lists
- Third-person bios for a one-person business
- No call to action

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should a photographer's about page be?
Around 250 to 500 words. Enough to feel like you, short enough to read on a phone.

### Should I mention my family or faith?
If it's part of how you connect with your ideal couples, yes. Share what feels natural.

### Should I include a video?
A 60-second hello filmed in good light can be more persuasive than any amount of text.

=== squarespace-vs-custom-for-photographers
title: Squarespace vs Custom Websites for Photographers

---
Most wedding photographers start on Squarespace, Showit, Pixieset or a similar builder, and for good reason: they're affordable, attractive and quick to set up. The question isn't whether they're good. It's whether they're still the right tool once your business depends on standing out.

## The quick comparison

| | Template platform | Custom website |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Upfront cost | $0 to $500 (plus template) | $2,500 to $8,000 |
| Monthly cost | $16 to $40 | $5 to $30 hosting |
| Look | Shared with thousands of others | Unique to you |
| Speed | Average, depends on template | Fast when built well |
| SEO control | Basics covered | Full control |
| Editing | Easy drag and drop | Easy if an editor is built in |
| Ownership | Rented | Yours |

## Where templates shine

- **Speed to launch.** You can be live in a weekend.
- **Low cost.** Ideal in year one.
- **Easy editing.** Swap images whenever you like.
- **Integrations.** Galleries, booking and email tools plug in.

If you're in your first couple of seasons, use a template and spend your money on education and gear.

## Where they hold you back

### Sameness

Popular photography templates are used by thousands of studios. When a couple has five tabs open and three share a layout, your work has to do all the differentiating.

### Speed

Builders load a lot of platform code on every page. Add 40 images and the mobile experience suffers. Custom sites ship only what the page needs. See [photography website speed](/blog/photography-website-speed/).

### SEO depth

You can rank on a template; many do. In a competitive city, the extra control of a custom build (clean code, precise schema, fast load times, flexible venue pages) is an edge. See [SEO for wedding photographers](/blog/wedding-photographer-seo/).

### Design limits

Want a film-strip hero, an availability checker, or a layout the template doesn't support? You're fighting the tool.

## What custom gives you

- A design built around *your* style, not adapted from someone else's
- Performance engineered for image-heavy pages
- Features made for how you work
- Full ownership of code and content

These are all custom builds, and none could be mistaken for a template:

## "But I need to update it myself"

A fair concern, and a solvable one. A custom site can include a simple editor and blog. Dan Reynolds Photography launched with exactly that: the team adds their own posts and updates content without touching code.

[[cta]]

## When to make the switch

Consider going custom when:

- You're booking consistently and want to raise prices
- You're in a crowded market and look like your competitors
- Your site is slow and you've already optimised images
- You want to invest seriously in SEO
- You're embarrassed by how "template" it feels

## Switching without losing rankings

Moving platforms is safe when done properly:

1. List every existing URL
2. Redirect each to its new equivalent
3. Keep page titles and content that already rank
4. Resubmit your sitemap in Search Console

Full process in [how to redesign without losing SEO](/blog/seo-migration-redesign/).

## A middle option

A focused custom homepage and core pages, connected to your existing gallery delivery tool (Pixieset, Pic-Time) for client galleries. You get a distinctive front door without rebuilding everything.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Showit better than Squarespace for photographers?
Showit offers more design freedom; Squarespace is simpler. Both are solid template platforms with similar limitations.

### Will a custom site get me more bookings?
A site that's faster, more distinctive and better optimised usually converts more visitors. The work still has to be good.

=== reviews-for-wedding-vendors
title: How Wedding Vendors Can Get More Reviews (Without Feeling Awkward)
desc: A simple system for wedding photographers, videographers and vendors to collect more Google and platform reviews, with timing tips and message templates.
cat: photographers
kw: wedding vendor reviews, how to get more google reviews, wedding photographer reviews, ask clients for reviews
takeaways: Ask at the moment of peak happiness, usually gallery or film delivery | Make it one click with a direct review link | A short personal message outperforms an automated request | Reply to every review and reuse the best ones on your website
---
Couples read reviews before they read anything else. A photographer with 80 recent five-star reviews will out-book an equally talented one with six, almost every time. Yet most vendors collect reviews by hoping.

Here's a system that takes ten minutes to set up and runs every time you deliver.

## Why reviews matter so much

- **Trust.** Couples can't try before they buy.
- **Local rankings.** Review count, rating and recency influence Google's map results.
- **Conversion.** Reviews on your website lift enquiries.
- **Words to reuse.** Couples describe your value better than you can.

## Where to collect them

1. **Google** first. It affects search visibility most.
2. **The Knot / WeddingWire / Hitched / Easy Weddings**, whichever couples use in your country.
3. **Facebook**, if your audience is active there.

Focus on Google until you have a healthy base, then spread out.

## When to ask

Timing is everything. Ask when the couple is happiest:

- The day you deliver the gallery or film
- When they message you saying they've cried over it
- After an album arrives

Not six months later when the glow has faded.

## How to ask

### 1. Get your direct link

In your Google Business Profile, choose "Ask for reviews" and copy the short link. It opens the review box directly.

### 2. Send a personal message

> "Hi Maya and Chris! I'm so glad you love the photos. It would mean a lot if you could share a few words about your experience on Google. It only takes a minute and really helps other couples find me: [link]. Thank you both, it was a joy to be part of your day."

### 3. Make it easy to write

Some couples freeze at a blank box. Offer prompts:

- What was it like working with me on the day?
- How did you feel when you saw your photos?
- Would you recommend me to a friend?

### 4. Follow up once

A gentle reminder a week or two later doubles response rates. Then let it go.

## Build it into your workflow

Add "request review" to your delivery checklist or automate it in your studio management tool, triggered a few days after delivery. The best system is the one you don't have to remember.

[[cta]]

## Reply to every review

A thoughtful reply shows future couples you care, and signals to Google that the profile is active. Mention something specific from their day.

## Handling a negative review

It happens to everyone eventually.

1. Take a breath; don't reply angry.
2. Respond publicly, briefly and professionally.
3. Offer to resolve it privately.
4. Let your many positive reviews speak for themselves.

A calm reply to a critical review often builds more trust than a perfect record.

## Put reviews to work

- Feature the best on your homepage and investment page
- Pair each with an image from that couple's wedding
- Screenshot heartfelt messages (with permission)
- Share on Instagram stories

Sometimes the most convincing review is a casual one. After we launched Dan Reynolds Photography's site, his WhatsApp said simply: "I got a lot of compliments on it." We put it on our homepage.

## What not to do

- **Don't pay or incentivise** reviews. It breaches Google's policies.
- **Don't write your own.**
- **Don't ask only your happiest clients** in a way that screens others out; platforms prohibit "review gating".
- **Don't copy a review** from one platform to another under the couple's name without permission.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many reviews should I aim for?
More than the competitors who appear next to you in the map results, with new ones arriving regularly.

### Can I ask past couples from years ago?
Yes. A friendly anniversary message is a natural moment to ask.

### Should reviews go on my website?
Definitely. Embed or quote them near your calls to action, and consider review schema so search engines understand them.
