When someone nearby searches for what you do, Google shows a map with three businesses, then a list of websites. Local SEO is the work of getting into both. For most small businesses it's the highest-return marketing there is: the people searching are close, ready and looking for exactly what you sell.

## How Google decides local rankings

Google has said local results depend mainly on three things:

- **Relevance:** how well your business matches the search
- **Distance:** how close you are to the searcher or the place named
- **Prominence:** how well known and trusted you are, online and off

You can't move your premises, but you can improve relevance and prominence a great deal.

## 1. Google Business Profile

This is the foundation. A complete, active profile is the biggest single factor for map visibility.

- Claim and verify it
- Choose the most specific primary category
- Fill in every field: hours, services, description, attributes
- Add photos every week or two
- Post updates monthly
- Answer questions

Step-by-step in our [Google Business Profile optimisation checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization/).

## 2. Reviews

Reviews influence rankings and whether people choose you once they see you.

- Ask every happy customer, at the moment they're happiest
- Send a direct link by text or email
- Reply to every review within a few days
- Aim for a steady flow; recency matters

## 3. Your website

Google reads your site to understand what you do and where.

### Essentials

- **Name, address and phone (NAP)** in the footer of every page, identical to your profile
- **Location in title tags and headings:** "Emergency Plumber in Leeds"
- **A page for each service**
- **A page for each key area you serve** ([how to build them](/blog/local-landing-pages/))
- **An embedded map** on the contact page
- **Local business schema** ([schema guide](/blog/schema-markup-guide/))
- **Fast, mobile-friendly pages** ([Core Web Vitals](/blog/core-web-vitals-explained/))

### Content

Write for local customers: local projects, area guides, answers to questions people in your town ask.

## 4. Citations

Citations are listings of your business details on other sites: Yelp, Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, Facebook, industry directories, local chambers. They matter less than they once did, but consistency still counts.

- Make sure your NAP is identical everywhere
- Claim the major platforms
- Fix old addresses or phone numbers

## 5. Local links

Links from other local websites are a strong prominence signal.

- Sponsor a local team or event
- Join the chamber of commerce
- Partner with complementary businesses
- Get featured in local news
- Supply a testimonial to a local supplier

[[audit]]

## 6. Behaviour signals

Google notices when people click your listing, ask for directions and call. Good photos, a strong rating and accurate hours all increase those actions.

## 7. Apple, Bing and AI

Local search isn't only Google.

- **Apple Business Connect** powers Apple Maps and Siri
- **Bing Places** powers Bing and Copilot, and feeds some AI assistants
- **AI search** (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews) draws on your website, listings and reviews

Clear facts and consistent details across all of them help. See [how to get mentioned in AI search](/blog/ai-search-optimization/).

## A monthly local SEO routine (about an hour)

| Task | Time |
| --- | --- |
| Add 3 to 5 new photos to your profile | 10 min |
| Publish one Google post | 10 min |
| Reply to all new reviews | 10 min |
| Ask recent customers for reviews | 10 min |
| Check Search Console for issues | 10 min |
| Update one page or add one piece of local content | 10+ min |

## Common mistakes

- Keyword stuffing your business name
- Different phone numbers on different sites
- One generic "services" page
- Ignoring negative reviews
- Dozens of thin, duplicate location pages
- A slow, outdated website

## How long does it take?

Profile improvements can show within weeks. Building a lead over established competitors usually takes three to six months of consistent effort. See [how long does SEO take?](/blog/how-long-does-seo-take/)

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a physical address to rank locally?
No. Service-area businesses can hide their address and list the areas they cover.

### How many reviews do I need?
Enough to beat the competitors shown beside you, with new ones arriving regularly.

### Is local SEO different from regular SEO?
It's a branch of it. The same fundamentals apply, with extra weight on your business profile, reviews and location signals.

=== google-business-profile-optimization
title: Google Business Profile Optimization Checklist (2026)
desc: A step-by-step checklist to optimise your Google Business Profile: categories, description, services, photos, reviews, posts and the mistakes to avoid.
cat: local
kw: google business profile optimization, google my business checklist, optimize google business profile, google maps ranking
takeaways: Complete every field; incomplete profiles are outranked by complete ones | The primary category is the most important single choice | Fresh photos, posts and review replies signal an active business | Never stuff keywords into your business name
---
Your Google Business Profile is often the first thing a customer sees: before your website, before your shopfront. It's free, it takes an hour to do well, and most of your competitors have done it badly. Work through this checklist and you'll be ahead of most of them.

## Setup

- [ ] **Claim or create** your profile at google.com/business
- [ ] **Verify** it (video, phone or postcard)
- [ ] **Add a second owner** so you're never locked out

## Core information

- [ ] **Business name:** exactly as on your signage. No added keywords or locations.
- [ ] **Primary category:** the most specific match ("Hair salon", not "Beauty")
- [ ] **Secondary categories:** up to nine, only if they truly apply
- [ ] **Address or service area:** hide your address if customers don't visit
- [ ] **Phone number:** a local number that's answered
- [ ] **Website:** your homepage, or the most relevant page for that location
- [ ] **Hours:** including holidays and special hours
- [ ] **Appointment or booking link**

## Description

- [ ] 750 characters; put the essentials in the first 250
- [ ] What you do, who for and where
- [ ] What makes you different
- [ ] Natural language; no keyword lists

## Services and products

- [ ] Every service listed with a short description
- [ ] Prices or "from" prices where possible
- [ ] Products with photos if you sell them
- [ ] Menu link for restaurants

## Attributes

- [ ] Accessibility, payment options, ownership attributes, amenities
- [ ] Anything customers filter by: "wheelchair accessible", "online appointments"

## Photos and video

- [ ] Logo and cover photo
- [ ] Exterior shots so people can find you
- [ ] Interior shots
- [ ] Team photos
- [ ] Work examples: before and afters, finished jobs, dishes
- [ ] Short videos
- [ ] **New photos added at least monthly**

Businesses with plenty of recent, real photos get more clicks, calls and direction requests.

## Reviews

- [ ] Create your short review link
- [ ] Ask every satisfied customer
- [ ] Reply to every review, good or bad
- [ ] Never offer rewards for reviews
- [ ] Report fake reviews through the profile

[[audit]]

## Posts

- [ ] One post every week or two: offers, news, events, new work
- [ ] A photo and a call-to-action button on each

## Questions and answers

- [ ] Add your own common questions and answer them
- [ ] Monitor for new questions

## Messaging

- [ ] Turn on only if you can reply quickly
- [ ] Set a welcome message

## Connect it to your website

- [ ] Name, address and phone on your site match the profile exactly
- [ ] Local business schema on your site ([how](/blog/schema-markup-guide/))
- [ ] Embed the Google map on your contact page
- [ ] Link to your profile from your site and ask for reviews there

## Track performance

- [ ] Check the Performance tab monthly: searches, views, calls, direction requests
- [ ] Add tracking parameters to your website link so visits show in analytics

## What to avoid

| Mistake | Why it hurts |
| --- | --- |
| Keywords in business name | Risk of suspension |
| Virtual office addresses | Against guidelines |
| Duplicate profiles | Splits reviews and confuses Google |
| Ignoring bad reviews | Looks careless to customers |
| Wrong hours | Frustrated customers, poor reviews |

## Keep it alive

A profile isn't a set-and-forget job. Ten minutes a week keeps it fresh. For the wider picture, see our [local SEO guide](/blog/local-seo-guide/).

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does it take to see results?
Changes can show within days, but meaningful ranking gains usually take a few weeks of consistent activity.

### Can I list more than one location?
Yes, one profile per genuine, staffed location.

### What should I do if my profile is suspended?
Review Google's guidelines, fix any violations (often the business name or address) and submit a reinstatement request with evidence.

takeaways: Each page must offer something unique and useful about that location | Combine service and location in the title, heading and opening paragraph | Local proof, such as projects, reviews and photos, is what separates ranking pages from doorway pages | Start small with your most valuable areas
---
If you serve more than one town, a single homepage can't rank everywhere. Local landing pages give each area its own page to match searches like "roof repair Peoria" or "life coach Vancouver". The catch: Google has seen millions of lazy versions and filters them out. Yours need to be genuinely useful.

## When you need them

- You serve multiple towns, cities or neighbourhoods
- You have more than one location
- You want to rank beyond your immediate address

## The anatomy of a page that ranks

### Title tag and H1

"[Service] in [Location]" plus your brand.

- Title: "Mortgage Broker in Basingstoke | MDJ Mortgages"
- H1: "Mortgage advice Basingstoke trusts"

### Opening paragraph

Confirm the service and the place in the first two sentences, naturally.

### Local proof

This is what makes the page unique:

- Projects or clients in that area
- Reviews from local customers
- Photos taken there
- How long you've served the area

### Local detail

- Neighbourhoods or postcodes covered
- Landmarks and travel information
- Regulations, conditions or needs specific to the area
- Local pricing notes, if they vary

### Services available there

Summaries with links to full service pages.

### FAQs

Questions real customers in that area ask.

### Contact and map

Address or service area, phone, hours, an embedded map and a form.

### A clear call to action

"Book a free consultation in Basingstoke."

## How long should they be?

Typically 500 to 1,000 words. Length isn't the goal; uniqueness is. If you can swap the town name and the page still makes sense, it isn't local enough.

## Avoiding duplicate content

- Write each page from scratch around its local proof
- Use different photos, reviews and examples
- Vary structure where it makes sense
- Don't publish a page until you have something specific to say

[[cta]]

## Site structure

- An index page: `/areas/` or `/locations/`
- One page per place: `/areas/basingstoke/`
- Links from the homepage footer and relevant service pages
- Links between nearby areas

See our [internal linking guide](/blog/internal-linking-guide/).

## Schema

Use LocalBusiness schema with the correct address or `areaServed`, and FAQ schema for the questions. Details in the [schema markup guide](/blog/schema-markup-guide/).

## Service + location combinations

Larger businesses sometimes build pages for every service in every location. That multiplies quickly (10 services × 20 towns = 200 pages) and is hard to keep unique. Start with:

1. A page for each service
2. A page for each priority area
3. Combination pages only for your most valuable pairs

## Multi-location businesses

If you have several physical branches, each needs:

- Its own page with unique content, team and photos
- Its own Google Business Profile linking to that page
- Its own reviews

## Measure

In Search Console, filter by page to see which searches each landing page appears for. Improve pages that rank on page two; they're closest to paying off. See our [Search Console guide](/blog/google-search-console-guide/).

## Frequently asked questions

### How many location pages should I create?
Start with five to ten for the areas that matter most. Add more as you gather local proof.

### Can I rank in a town where I have no office?
Yes, with a strong page and real evidence you serve it. Map results are harder without a nearby address; organic results are achievable.

### Should each page have its own phone number?
Only if calls are genuinely answered locally. Otherwise use your main number consistently.

For a salon or spa, a website has a simple job: get people booked. Clients decide fast, usually on their phone, often late in the evening when you're closed. If booking takes more than a couple of taps, they'll book somewhere else.

## Online booking first

- **A "Book now" button** in the header on every page, sticky on mobile
- **Real-time availability** through your booking system (Fresha, Booksy, Vagaro, Square, Timely, GlossGenius)
- **Embedded or seamlessly linked**, so it feels part of your site
- **Deposits or card capture** to reduce no-shows
- **Automatic reminders** by SMS and email

More in [online booking for small businesses](/blog/online-booking-for-small-business/).

## A clear service menu

Clients want to know what you offer and what it costs.

- Group by category: cuts, colour, treatments, nails, massage, facials
- Show prices or "from" prices
- Add durations
- Describe treatments in a sentence, especially spa services
- Link each to booking

Hiding prices leads to phone calls and lost bookings.

## Show the work

- **A gallery** of real results: colour transformations, styles, nails, before and afters
- **Your space:** people choose a salon partly on atmosphere
- **Instagram feed,** used sparingly so it doesn't slow the page

Compress images properly; see [image SEO](/blog/image-seo/).

## Introduce the team

A photo, name, specialities and a booking link for each stylist or therapist. Clients often choose a person, not just a salon.

## Homepage essentials

1. A striking image or short video of the salon or your work
2. A headline that captures your vibe: "Sharp cuts. Sharper confidence."
3. Book button
4. Services overview
5. Reviews
6. Location, hours and a map
7. Gift cards or offers

## Local SEO for salons

Most new clients search "hair salon near me" or "[treatment] [town]".

- Complete your Google Business Profile and add your booking link ([checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization/))
- Ask every happy client for a review
- Put your suburb or town in page titles
- Create pages for your key services: "Balayage in [Town]", "Bridal hair [Town]"

Full strategy in our [local SEO guide](/blog/local-seo-guide/).

## Design notes

- **Mood matters.** Luxury spa, edgy barbershop and friendly family salon need different palettes and type.
- **Mobile first.** Most bookings happen on phones.
- **Fast.** Heavy sliders and auto-playing video slow sites down.
- **Accessible.** Good contrast and readable text sizes.

## Bilingual and multi-location salons

If you serve more than one language community, offer both. If you have several branches, give each its own page with address, hours, team and booking.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which booking system should I use?
Choose one that suits your size and budget and integrates cleanly with your website. Fresha, Booksy, Square and Vagaro are common choices.

### Should I list prices?
Yes. "From" prices are fine where services vary.

### Do I need a website if I have Instagram and a booking app?
A website gives you a home that you control, ranks on Google, and brings everything (services, team, booking, reviews) together.
