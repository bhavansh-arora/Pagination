Buying a Ready-Made Website vs Building From Scratch

The pros and cons of buying a ready-made or pre-built website compared with a custom build, what to check before buying and who each option suits.

Key takeaways
  • A ready-made site gets you online in days at a lower cost
  • Check design quality, code ownership, exclusivity and how easily it can be rebranded
  • Custom builds fit unusual needs and give the strongest differentiation
  • A professionally designed pre-built site can be customised to feel entirely yours

Not every business needs a website designed from a blank page. If your needs match a common pattern (a salon with booking, a clinic with services and appointments, a solar installer with a quote form) a ready-made site that's already designed and built can get you live in a fraction of the time.

Here's how to decide between buying and building.

What "ready-made" means

There's a spectrum:

TypeWhat it is
Theme or templateA design sold to many buyers; you add content
Pre-built custom siteA complete, professionally built site sold to one owner
Existing business websiteA live site with traffic or revenue, bought as an asset

This article focuses on the first two.

Advantages of buying

  • Speed. Live in days.
  • Cost. Lower than a custom design.
  • Certainty. You see exactly what you're getting.
  • Proven structure. Built around what works for that industry.

Disadvantages

  • Fit. You adapt to the design, not the other way round.
  • Uniqueness. Mass-market themes are used by thousands.
  • Quality varies. Many templates are bloated and slow.
  • Customisation limits unless you have the source code.

Advantages of building custom

  • Designed around your business and customers
  • Completely unique
  • Any feature you need
  • Optimised for your specific goals

Disadvantages

  • Higher cost
  • Longer timeline
  • More decisions required from you

What to check before buying a ready-made site

  1. Is it exclusive? A design sold once is very different from one sold a thousand times.
  2. Do you get the full source code?
  3. Who owns it after purchase?
  4. How easily can it be rebranded? Colours, fonts, logo, copy, images.
  5. Is it fast? Test the demo on PageSpeed Insights.
  6. Is it mobile-friendly?
  7. Is SEO built in? Titles, schema, clean structure.
  8. What's included? Booking, payments, forms.
  9. What does hosting cost?
  10. Is support or customisation available?

Mass-market templates vs one-owner sites

A $60 theme from a marketplace is cheap for a reason: it's built to suit everyone, so it's stuffed with options, and thousands of other sites look the same.

Who should buy

  • New businesses that need to launch quickly
  • Owners with a clear match to an existing design
  • Businesses with modest budgets who still want quality
  • Anyone who'd rather react to something finished than start from nothing

Who should build

  • Businesses with unusual services or processes
  • Brands where distinctiveness is central
  • Sites needing custom features or integrations
  • Companies in very competitive markets

A hybrid approach

Buy a strong pre-built site, then customise: your branding, copy, photos, plus any extra pages or features. You get most of the benefit of custom at a lower cost and faster.

Buying an existing website business

A different proposition: you're buying traffic, revenue and reputation. Do proper due diligence on analytics, income, backlinks and any penalties, ideally with professional help.

Frequently asked questions

Can a ready-made site rank on Google?

Yes, if it's well built and you add unique content about your business and location.

Will it look like everyone else's?

Mass-market themes might. A one-owner design, rebranded with your identity and content, won't.

Can I upgrade to custom later?

Yes. Many businesses start with a ready-made site and invest in a custom design once revenue grows.
