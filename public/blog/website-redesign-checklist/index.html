The Website Redesign Checklist: 30 Steps to Relaunch Without Losing Traffic

A 30-step website redesign checklist covering planning, content, SEO redirects, testing and launch, so your new site keeps its rankings and wins more leads.

Key takeaways
  • Benchmark your current traffic and rankings before changing anything
  • Map every old URL to a new one with 301 redirects
  • Test forms, speed and mobile on real devices before launch
  • Watch Search Console daily for the first two weeks after relaunch

A redesign is the highest-leverage marketing project most small businesses ever run. It's also the easiest way to accidentally erase years of Google rankings in an afternoon. This checklist keeps the upside and removes the risk.

Work through it in order. Each phase builds on the one before.

Phase 1: Before you touch anything

  1. Write down the goal. "Look more modern" isn't a goal. "Increase enquiries from 6 to 15 a month" is.
  2. Benchmark current performance. Record monthly visitors, enquiries, top landing pages and conversion rate from your analytics.
  3. Export your rankings. In Google Search Console, export the queries and pages report for the last 12 months. These are the pages you must protect.
  4. Crawl the existing site. Use a free tool like Screaming Frog (up to 500 URLs) to list every URL, title and meta description.
  5. List what works. Pages that rank, copy that converts, photos customers mention. Keep them.
  6. List what doesn't. Confusing navigation, dead pages, outdated services.
  7. Back everything up. Download a full copy of the current site and database.

Phase 2: Plan the new site

  1. Decide the sitemap. Which pages stay, merge, split or go. See what pages a small business website should have.
  2. Build the redirect map. A spreadsheet with two columns: old URL, new URL. Every old page needs a destination. This single document protects your SEO.
  3. Keep URLs where you can. If /services/boiler-repair/ ranks well, don't rename it for the sake of tidiness.
  4. Write a brief. One page covering audience, goals, pages, features and examples. Use our website brief template.
  5. Choose the platform deliberately. If the old platform was the problem, don't rebuild on it.

Phase 3: Content and design

  1. Rewrite the homepage headline. It should say what you do, for whom and where, in one line.
  2. Give every page one job. One primary call to action per page.
  3. Update proof. Fresh testimonials, current review counts, recent work.
  4. Keep ranking content. If a page ranks, improve its copy rather than replacing it wholesale.
  5. Optimise images. Export in WebP at the size they'll be displayed. Our image SEO guide covers this.
  6. Write unique titles and meta descriptions for every page. See title tags and meta descriptions.

Phase 4: Build and test

  1. Build on a staging link that search engines can't index.
  2. Test every form. Submit each one and confirm the email arrives.
  3. Test on real phones. At least one iPhone and one Android.
  4. Check speed. Run PageSpeed Insights on the homepage and a service page. Aim for green on mobile.
  5. Add schema markup. Organization, LocalBusiness, Service and FAQ where relevant. See our schema markup guide.
  6. Check accessibility basics. Colour contrast, alt text, keyboard navigation.

Phase 5: Launch

  1. Implement the 301 redirects from your redirect map and test a sample of old URLs.
  2. Remove the noindex from staging settings. Forgetting this is the classic launch-day mistake.
  3. Reconnect analytics and Search Console and submit the new XML sitemap.
  4. Update your Google Business Profile and social links if any URLs changed.

Phase 6: After launch

  1. Monitor Search Console daily for two weeks. Watch for 404 errors and coverage issues and fix them as they appear.
  2. Compare against your benchmark after 30, 60 and 90 days. A small dip in the first two weeks is normal. A sustained drop means redirects or content need attention.

The three mistakes that cause most redesign disasters

No redirects. Old URLs return 404 errors, and Google drops them. Rankings that took years vanish in weeks.

Deleting content that ranks. A designer trims a 900-word service page to three sentences because it "looks cleaner". The page stops ranking.

Launching with noindex still on. The whole site disappears from search until someone notices.

All three are avoidable with the checklist above. We go deeper in how to redesign without losing SEO.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a redesign take?

Most small business redesigns take two to six weeks. See realistic website timelines.

Should I redesign in stages or all at once?

For most small sites, a single well-planned launch is simpler and safer. Staged rollouts make sense for large sites with hundreds of pages.

Can you redesign my site without changing the platform?

Often, yes. But if the platform is the reason the site is slow or hard to edit, a rebuild on something better usually costs less over time.
