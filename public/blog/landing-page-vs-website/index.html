Landing Page vs Website: What's the Difference and Which Do You Need?

The difference between a landing page and a full website, when to use each, and what a high-converting landing page should include.

Key takeaways
  • A website serves many purposes; a landing page serves one
  • Use landing pages for ads, campaigns and specific offers
  • Remove navigation and distractions so the only choice is to act
  • Most businesses need both

"Do I need a website or just a landing page?" is a common question, and the answer is usually "both, for different jobs". Understanding the difference helps you spend your marketing budget where it works.

The difference in one sentence

A website is your whole shop: multiple pages for different visitors and purposes. A landing page is a single stand built for one audience, one offer and one action.

When you need a website

  • You want to be found on Google for a range of searches
  • You offer several services or serve several audiences
  • Customers research you before buying
  • You need pages like about, pricing, portfolio and blog
  • You want a long-term asset

For nearly every established business, a website is the foundation.

When you need a landing page

  • You're running Google, Facebook or Instagram ads
  • You're launching a product, event or promotion
  • You're promoting a lead magnet or webinar
  • You want to test an offer quickly
  • You're a new business validating an idea

Sending ad traffic to your homepage wastes money. A homepage offers ten paths; a landing page offers one.

Anatomy of a high-converting landing page

  1. Headline that matches the ad or email that brought them
  2. Subheadline adding the key benefit
  3. Hero image or video showing the outcome
  4. Call to action above the fold
  5. Benefits: three to five, focused on outcomes
  6. Proof: testimonials, ratings, logos, numbers
  7. How it works: simple steps
  8. Objection handling: FAQ, guarantee
  9. Urgency, if genuine: limited places, deadline
  10. Final call to action

No main menu. No links elsewhere. The only way forward is the action you want.

Message match

The words in your ad should appear on the landing page. If the ad says "Free solar quote in Barrie", the page should say exactly that. Mismatch causes visitors to bounce.

One page, one goal

Decide the single action: book a call, request a quote, buy, download. Remove anything that doesn't support it.

A one-page website is a third thing

A one-page website presents a whole business on a single scrolling page with a menu that jumps to sections. It suits new or simple businesses. The trade-off is SEO: one page can rank for only a few terms. See what pages should a small business website have?

How they work together

  • Website builds organic visibility and credibility
  • Landing pages convert paid and campaign traffic
  • Both share branding, proof and tracking

Typical setup: a main site plus a landing page for each campaign or service you advertise.

Measuring a landing page

  • Conversion rate: actions ÷ visitors
  • Cost per lead: ad spend ÷ leads
  • Bounce rate and scroll depth

Test one change at a time: headline, image, form length, button text. See how to get more leads from your website.

Should landing pages be indexed?

Campaign-specific pages are often set to noindex so they don't compete with your main pages. Evergreen ones targeting a keyword can be indexed.

Frequently asked questions

Can I start with just a landing page?

Yes, to validate an idea or run a first campaign. Plan to build a full site as you grow.

How long should a landing page be?

As long as it takes to persuade. Simple, low-cost offers need less; considered purchases need more.

Do I need a different landing page for each ad?

Ideally one per offer or audience, so the message matches precisely.
