SEO for a New Website: What to Do in the First 90 Days

A week-by-week SEO plan for a new website's first 90 days: setup, indexing, on-page basics, local signals, content and early links.

Key takeaways
  • Get the technical foundations and tracking right in week one
  • Submit your sitemap and make sure Google can index every key page
  • Publish a small amount of genuinely useful content rather than a lot of thin pages
  • Early reviews, listings and a few good links build trust fastest

A new website starts with no history, no links and no trust. Google doesn't know it exists. The first three months are about fixing that: getting found, getting understood and earning the first signs of credibility.

Here's a realistic plan.

Week 1: foundations

  • Set up Google Search Console and verify your domain
  • Set up Bing Webmaster Tools (it also feeds several AI assistants)
  • Install analytics (GA4 or a privacy-friendly alternative)
  • Submit your XML sitemap in both
  • Check indexing: use URL Inspection on your homepage and request indexing
  • Confirm HTTPS and that all versions of your domain redirect to one
  • Run PageSpeed Insights and fix anything in the red

Use our technical SEO checklist to cover everything.

Week 2: on-page basics

  • Unique title tag and meta description on every page (how to write them)
  • One clear H1 per page
  • Service pages for each thing you sell
  • Contact details in the footer
  • Schema markup: Organization or LocalBusiness, Service, FAQ (guide)
  • Internal links between related pages

Week 3: local presence

  • Create or claim your Google Business Profile and complete every field (checklist)
  • Claim Bing Places and Apple Business Connect
  • Add your business to key directories for your industry and region
  • Make sure name, address and phone match everywhere

Week 4: first reviews

Ask five to ten recent customers for a Google review. A new site with a handful of genuine reviews looks far more established than one with none.

Weeks 5 to 8: content

Publish four to six genuinely useful pieces:

  • Answers to your customers' most common questions
  • A pricing or cost guide
  • A "how it works" explainer
  • A comparison or buying guide
  • A case study or two

Quality matters more than quantity. See how to write a blog post that ranks and keyword research for small businesses.

Weeks 9 to 12: early links and refinement

Earn your first links

  • Suppliers and partners
  • Industry associations and accreditation bodies
  • Local chamber of commerce
  • Clients who list their suppliers
  • A local news story or guest article

A few relevant links from real organisations are worth more than hundreds from low-quality directories.

Review the data

In Search Console, look at:

  • Which pages are indexed
  • Which queries you're starting to appear for
  • Pages with impressions but low clicks (improve titles)
  • Any crawl errors

Our Search Console guide shows where.

What results to expect

TimeframeTypical progress
Weeks 1 to 2Site indexed, ranks for your brand name
Month 1 to 2Appears for long-tail and local searches
Month 3First organic enquiries, growing impressions
Month 6+Competing for main service keywords

New sites take time to build trust. Steady effort wins. See how long does SEO take?

What not to do

  • Buy links or use link schemes
  • Publish dozens of thin pages to "look bigger"
  • Copy content from other sites
  • Change URLs repeatedly
  • Expect page-one rankings in week two

Replacing an old site?

If your new site replaces an existing one, the priority is redirects. Without them you'll lose rankings the old site earned. See how to redesign without losing SEO.

Don't forget AI search

State clearly who you are, what you do and where; add FAQs; allow AI crawlers. See how to get mentioned in AI search.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take Google to index a new site?

Usually a few days to a couple of weeks after you submit a sitemap.

Should I run ads while SEO builds?

Often, yes. Ads bring traffic immediately while organic visibility grows. See SEO vs Google Ads.

Do I need a blog from day one?

You need useful content. A handful of strong guides is enough to start.
