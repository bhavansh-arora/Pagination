How to Position Your Coaching Niche on Your Website

Key takeaways
  • A clear niche makes marketing easier and fees higher
  • Define it with three parts: who, problem and outcome
  • Put your positioning statement in the first line of your homepage
  • You can niche your marketing without turning away other good clients

"I help people live their best life" is true of every coach and persuasive to no one. A niche isn't a cage; it's a signpost. It tells the right person, in a second, "this is for you".

Why niching works

  • Recognition. People respond to messages that describe their exact situation.
  • Referrals. "She's the coach for new managers in tech" is easy to remember and pass on.
  • Search. "Career coach for nurses" is far easier to rank for than "life coach".
  • Pricing. Specialists charge more than generalists.
  • Content. You always know what to write about.

The three-part positioning formula

I help [who] to [outcome] without/despite [problem].

  • "I help first-time founders lead growing teams without burning out."
  • "I help women navigating fertility treatment feel supported and in control."
  • "I help tech professionals move into work that feels like flow."

Write ten versions. Read each aloud. The right one feels slightly scary because it's specific.

Four ways to niche

DimensionExamples
WhoLawyers, new mothers, expats, creatives
ProblemBurnout, imposter feelings, divorce, redundancy
OutcomePromotion, career change, confidence, boundaries
MethodSomatic work, strengths-based, a named framework

The strongest niches combine two: a who and a problem.

Put it on your website

The hero

Your positioning belongs in your headline or the line directly beneath it. Don't make visitors hunt for it.

The "is this you?" section

Three or four bullet points describing your ideal client's situation in their own words.

Your programmes

Name and describe them in terms of the niche outcome.

Your testimonials

Choose stories from clients who fit the niche. People look for someone like themselves.

Your content

Articles, lead magnets and social posts all speak to the same person.

"But I don't want to turn clients away"

You won't. Niching is about marketing focus, not a rule about who you can work with. People outside your niche will still find you through referrals and often say "I know I'm not exactly your usual client, but...". What changes is that your ideal clients finally recognise you.

How to find your niche

  1. Look at your best clients. Who did you love working with and get great results for? What did they have in common?
  2. Look at your own story. Many coaches serve a past version of themselves.
  3. Check demand. Are people searching for it and paying for it?
  4. Test. Change your headline and LinkedIn bio for 90 days and see who shows up.

Signs your positioning is working

  • Discovery calls start with "I read your site and it felt like you were describing me"
  • Referrals arrive pre-sold
  • You stop competing on price
  • Writing content gets easier

Frequently asked questions

Can I have more than one niche?

You can serve two closely related audiences, each with its own page. More than that and the homepage message blurs.

What if I pick the wrong niche?

You adjust. Updating a headline and a few pages is quick. Staying vague costs more than choosing imperfectly.

Do I need to niche from day one?

No. Many coaches discover their niche after their first 20 clients. Start reasonably specific and narrow as patterns emerge.
