Key takeaways
  • The best coaching sites lead with the client's problem, not the coach's biography
  • Each example uses one clear call to action, usually a free call
  • Distinct colour, typography and photography stop a site looking like a template
  • You can borrow structure from any of them without copying the style

Looking at good examples is the fastest way to work out what you want from your own site. These eleven are all sites we designed and built at Code Bunny. For each, we've pulled out the one idea worth borrowing.

1. High Fifteen: executive coaching with attitude

High Fifteen helps senior leaders build inclusive, high-performing cultures. The site is bold and confident, with a headline that names the stakes ("Cultures that perform under pressure") and a founder photo that's impossible to ignore.

Borrow this: a point of view. The site says what it's against as well as what it's for.

2. Take Root Life Coaching: calm and grounded

Whole-person life coaching presented with natural imagery and a gentle palette. The "First Step call" is on every screen.

Borrow this: give your discovery call a name that fits your brand.

3. Co-Allies: sensitive topics handled with care

Coaching for fertility journeys, life transitions and conscious leadership. The design is soft and human, and the copy never rushes the reader.

Borrow this: match your tone to how your client feels when they arrive.

4. Live in Flow Coaching: a tight niche

Career-transition coaching for tech professionals. The headline ("Feeling stuck? Find your flow.") and every section speak to that one audience.

Borrow this: name your niche in the first line.

5. Polish: coaching meets AI leadership

Executive coaching, AI leadership programmes and keynote speaking for leaders in New Zealand and Australia. Dramatic photography and crisp type signal a premium offer.

Borrow this: separate pages for separate audiences (coaching, programmes, speaking).

6. Lasting Change Wellness: a named method

The whole site hangs on the S.H.I.N.E. method, turning an abstract service into a clear, step-by-step journey.

Borrow this: name your framework and build your pages around it.

7. Sharon Tabak: credibility through association

A certified Jay Shetty life coach specialising in trauma, identity and big transitions. The certification is shown early, and the headline promises a specific transformation.

Borrow this: if you have a recognisable credential, put it near the top.

8. Arielle Innou Coaching: warmth and clarity

Life, relationship and corporate coaching plus retreats. A friendly portrait and a three-word promise ("Clear, confident, in control") do the heavy lifting.

Borrow this: a short promise people can repeat.

9. George Dupont Leadership: authority branding

A former pro hockey captain sells a leadership system to sports franchises and C-suites. Dark, cinematic design and a trademarked method position him as the obvious expert.

Borrow this: let your background shape the visual language.

10. Insight Transformational Coaching: clear tiers

Three coaching levels for women ready to move from wishing to action, each clearly explained.

Borrow this: offer good, better and best so prospects choose which, not whether.

11. Lauren Powers: a personal brand that performs

A ten-time bodybuilding champion, TV host and author. The site is loud, bold and unmistakably hers.

Borrow this: don't sand off your personality to look "professional".

What all eleven have in common

  1. A headline about the client. Not one opens with a job title.
  2. One call to action. A free call, on every screen.
  3. A real person, front and centre.
  4. A distinct look. None of them could be mistaken for a template.
  5. Speed. Hand-coded pages that load quickly on a phone.

How to use these examples

Pick two or three you're drawn to and note what you like: the layout, the tone, the way packages are shown. Include those notes in your website brief. It gives a designer a far clearer target than "something modern".
