Key takeaways
  • The best photography sites open with one unforgettable image and a clear location
  • Each has its own visual identity that matches the photographer's style
  • All make it effortless to enquire from any page
  • Fast loading is designed in, not bolted on

Templates have made wedding photography sites look increasingly alike. These six, all built by Code Bunny, show how much room there is to be distinctive while staying fast and easy to book.

1. Dan Reynolds Photography — Chicago & Peoria, Illinois

A husband-and-wife photo and film team ranked among the top photographers in Illinois. The homepage opens with a golden-hour frame and the line "Moments you'll see again", then moves through portfolio, films and investment.

What works: photo and film presented as one experience; a built-in editor and blog so the team can publish their own stories; location in the first line for local SEO.

After launch, Dan told us: "I think the site looks great. I got a lot of compliments on it."

2. Mark Arista Films — Oahu, Hawaii

A wedding videographer whose site feels like the opening titles of a film: a dark palette, film-strip details and a single centred title.

What works: the design language is the medium. You know it's a filmmaker's site before you read a word.

3. Captured by Katherine — Brisbane, Australia

Candid wedding, family and formal photography since 2009. Warm, romantic and relaxed, with an availability check couples can use in seconds.

What works: the "Check your date" hook turns browsing into an enquiry.

4. Jack Albert Weddings — Ottawa Valley, Canada

Warm, cinematic wedding films across Renfrew County, Ottawa and Kingston. The headline "The day, heard again" sells what film offers that photos can't: sound.

What works: a headline that makes the case for video in four words.

Catholic and Christian wedding photography and film. The site leads with reverence and a clear statement of who it's for.

What works: a tight niche. Couples who want a photographer who understands a Catholic ceremony know instantly they've found one.

What they have in common

  1. One hero image or clip. No twelve-slide carousels.
  2. Location in the first line.
  3. A personality. Colour, type and voice match the work.
  4. An enquiry button on every screen.
  5. Speed. Hand-coded, optimised images, lazy loading.
  6. Text for Google. Beautiful galleries supported by real copy.
