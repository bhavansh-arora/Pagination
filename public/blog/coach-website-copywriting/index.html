Coaching Website Copywriting: Headlines and Words That Convert

How to write coaching website copy that connects: headline formulas, section-by-section guidance, words to avoid and examples from real coaching sites.

Key takeaways
  • Write to one person in the words they'd use themselves
  • Your headline should name their problem or their goal
  • Cut jargon like "empower" and "unlock your potential"
  • Every section should end by pointing to the next step

Design gets attention. Words get the booking. Most coaching websites lose people in the first sentence by talking about the coach when the visitor is thinking about themselves.

Here's how to write copy that sounds like you and speaks to them.

Rule one: write to one person

Picture a real client, ideally your favourite one. Write as if you're sending them a message. Use "you" twice as often as "I".

Rule two: use their words

Go through discovery call notes, emails and testimonials. Collect the exact phrases clients use: "I feel like I'm treading water", "I know I'm capable of more". Those phrases are your copy. They'll outperform anything you invent.

Headline formulas that work

Problem then promise "Feeling stuck? Find your flow."

The outcome "Clear, confident and in control again."

The transformation "Transform life's hardest moments into your greatest comeback."

The enemy "Cultures that perform under pressure. Without burnout, blame or busy nonsense."

The identity "For women who are done wishing and ready to act."

Notice what none of them say: "Welcome to my website" or "Certified Professional Coach".

Section by section

Subheadline

One sentence: who you help and how. "One-to-one coaching for tech professionals ready for a career that fits."

The problem section

Describe their situation so accurately they feel understood. Three short lines beat one long paragraph.

The solution section

Introduce your coaching as the bridge. Focus on what changes for them.

How it works

Three steps. Short verbs: Book. Plan. Grow.

About snippet

Two or three sentences on why you, with a link to your about page.

Call to action

Specific and low-pressure: "Book your free 30-minute call". Add a reassurance: "No pressure. Just a conversation."

Words to cut

Instead ofTry
Empower you to unlock your potentialHelp you [specific outcome]
Holistic, transformative journeyA three-month programme
I am passionate about(just say what you do)
Solutions(name the actual thing)
Leverage, synergy, optimiseUse, together, improve

If a phrase could appear on any coach's site, it isn't doing any work on yours.

Make it scannable

Most visitors skim first and read second.

  • Short paragraphs (one to three lines)
  • A heading every few paragraphs
  • Bullet points for lists
  • Bold for the key phrase in a section, sparingly

Calls to action that get clicked

  • Use the same wording everywhere so it becomes familiar
  • Start with a verb: Book, Start, Get
  • Say what happens: "Book a free 30-minute call"
  • Place one after every major section

The read-aloud test

Read every page out loud. Anywhere you stumble, sound stiff or wouldn't say it to a client's face, rewrite. This catches most problems.

Should you use AI to write your copy?

AI tools are useful for outlining and tidying, but they default to the same generic phrases every other coach's site uses. Use them to speed up a draft built from your own client language, then edit until it sounds like you.

Frequently asked questions

How long should my homepage be?

Long enough to answer the visitor's main questions and build trust; usually 500 to 900 words broken into clear sections.

Should I write in first person?

Yes, for a solo coach. "I" and "you" feel personal. Use "we" only if you're genuinely a team.
