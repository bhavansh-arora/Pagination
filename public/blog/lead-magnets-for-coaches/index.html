20 Lead Magnet Ideas for Coaches (That People Actually Want)

Twenty lead magnet ideas for life, career, executive and wellness coaches, plus how to choose one, where to put it and what to send afterwards.

Key takeaways
  • A lead magnet should solve one small, specific problem quickly
  • Quizzes and checklists usually outperform long ebooks
  • Promote it in your hero, mid-page and footer
  • Follow up with a short email series that leads to a discovery call

Most people who visit your coaching website aren't ready to book a call today. A lead magnet gives them a useful first step and gives you permission to keep in touch until they are.

The best ones share three traits: they're specific, they're quick to consume, and they give a real result.

Quick-win formats

  1. Checklist. "The 10-point Sunday reset for a calmer week."
  2. One-page worksheet. "Find your top five values in ten minutes."
  3. Script or template. "Three ways to say no without feeling guilty."
  4. Cheat sheet. "20 questions to ask yourself before changing careers."
  5. Journal prompts. "Seven prompts for the week after a breakup."

Interactive formats

  1. Quiz. "What's your leadership style?" Quizzes convert well because people love learning about themselves.
  2. Self-assessment scorecard. "Rate your work-life balance across eight areas."
  3. Wheel of life. A classic coaching tool, made interactive or printable.
  4. Calculator. "What is your burnout costing you?"

Deeper formats

  1. Short email course. Five days, one small action a day.
  2. Mini video training. 15 minutes on one problem.
  3. Guided audio. A meditation, visualisation or pep talk.
  4. Workbook. A longer guided exercise around your method.
  5. Webinar or masterclass replay.
  6. First chapter of your book.

Niche-specific ideas

  1. Career coaches: a CV or LinkedIn headline template, or a "should I stay or go?" decision guide.
  2. Executive coaches: a 90-day plan for new leaders, or a one-to-one meeting template.
  3. Wellness coaches: a seven-day energy tracker or simple meal planner.
  4. Relationship coaches: conversation starters for couples.
  5. Business coaches: a pricing calculator or weekly planning template.

How to choose

Ask: what's the first small problem my ideal client wants solved, the one that sits just before the bigger problem I'm paid to solve? Your lead magnet should fix that small thing and reveal the bigger one.

A career coach's checklist on "signs it's time to leave your job" naturally leads to a call about what to do next.

Where to put it on your website

  • In the hero as a secondary button under "Book a call"
  • A dedicated section halfway down the homepage
  • At the end of every blog post
  • In the footer
  • A dedicated landing page you can link to from social

Keep the form to first name and email. Every extra field lowers sign-ups.

What happens after they sign up

The download is the start, not the goal. Set up a short welcome sequence:

  1. Immediately: deliver the resource and say who you are.
  2. Day 2: a quick tip that builds on it.
  3. Day 4: a client story.
  4. Day 6: common mistakes people make with this problem.
  5. Day 8: an invitation to book a discovery call.

Then add them to your regular newsletter. For the full journey, see the discovery call funnel.

Make it look good

A scruffy PDF undermines a premium service. Use your brand fonts and colours, keep layouts clean and add your photo and booking link on the last page.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Too long. A 40-page ebook rarely gets read.
  • Too broad. "A guide to a better life."
  • No follow-up.
  • Asking for a phone number on the form.
  • Hiding it on one page.

Frequently asked questions

Do lead magnets still work?

Yes, when they're genuinely useful. People are more selective about giving their email, so quality matters more than ever.

What tool do I need?

An email platform such as MailerLite, Kit (ConvertKit), Flodesk or Mailchimp. They provide the form, delivery and automated emails.

How many lead magnets should I have?

Start with one strong one. Add more later for different audiences or blog topics.
