Should You Show Prices on Your Wedding Photography Website?

The case for and against showing wedding photography prices online, what to include on an investment page, and wording that filters without scaring couples off.

Key takeaways
  • Showing a starting price saves time for you and the couple
  • You don't have to publish full packages; "collections begin at" works well
  • Present price alongside what's included and why it's worth it
  • Follow pricing with proof and an easy way to enquire

Few questions divide wedding photographers like this one. One camp says publishing prices scares couples away before they've fallen for your work. The other says hiding them wastes everyone's time. After building sites for photographers and filmmakers across six countries, here's where we land.

The case for showing prices

  • Couples expect it. Most are comparing several photographers and will skip those who make them ask.
  • It filters enquiries. You stop spending evenings replying to couples whose budget is a third of your rate.
  • It builds trust. Openness reads as confidence.
  • It anchors value. Seeing a number next to what's included frames the conversation.

The case against

  • Bespoke quoting. If every wedding is priced individually, a single number may mislead.
  • Competitors can see it. True, though they can find out anyway.
  • Sticker shock without context. A price with no explanation of value can put people off.

The middle path most photographers should take

Show a starting price and explain what shapes the final figure.

"Wedding collections begin at $3,800. Most couples invest between $4,500 and $6,000 for full-day coverage with a second photographer."

This filters effectively without boxing you in, and invites the enquiry rather than replacing it.

What to include on an investment page

  1. A short intro about your approach and what the experience includes
  2. Starting price or two to three collections
  3. What's included: hours, second shooter, engagement session, gallery, albums, turnaround time
  4. Add-ons: extra hours, albums, film, rehearsal dinner
  5. How booking works: retainer, contract, payment schedule
  6. Travel: where you're based and how travel fees work
  7. Testimonials from couples who felt it was worth every penny
  8. FAQs
  9. A call to action: "Check your date"

Presenting collections

If you list full packages, three is the magic number. Name them, lead with the experience and put the most popular in the middle.

Wording that reassures

  • Call it "Investment" or "Pricing"; both are fine. Couples search for "pricing".
  • Explain the why: "You're not only booking a day of photography. You're booking 14 months of planning support, 40 hours of editing and images your grandchildren will see."
  • Offer payment plans if you have them.
  • Invite conversation: "Every wedding is different. Tell me about yours and I'll put together a custom quote."

If you truly can't show prices

Say so and give a reason, plus a response time: "Because every celebration is unique, I create a custom proposal for each couple. Send me your date and I'll reply within 24 hours." Silence is the real conversion killer.

A pricing guide PDF

Some photographers offer a detailed guide in exchange for an email address. It works if the site still shows a starting price. If the guide is the only way to see any number, many couples won't bother.

Frequently asked questions

Will I get fewer enquiries if I show prices?

Possibly fewer, but better. Photographers usually find the quality and booking rate improve.

Should I show prices on Instagram too?

A "starting at" line in your highlights or link page is helpful and saves repetitive DMs.

How often should I raise my prices?

Review them at least once a year, or whenever you're booking more than about 70% of enquiries.
