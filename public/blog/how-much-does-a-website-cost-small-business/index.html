How Much Does a Website Cost for a Small Business in 2026?

Real price ranges for small business websites in 2026, what drives the cost up or down, and how to avoid paying for things you don't need.

Key takeaways
  • Most small business websites cost between $1,500 and $10,000 to build professionally
  • Page count matters less than features like booking, payments and custom design
  • Ongoing costs (domain, hosting, maintenance) usually run $10 to $150 a month
  • A fixed quote and a preview before you pay removes most of the risk

"How much does a website cost?" is the first question almost every business owner asks us, and the honest answer is a range. A website is closer to a renovation than a product on a shelf: the price depends on what you're building, who builds it and how much of the thinking is done for you.

This guide gives you real numbers, explains what actually moves the price, and shows you how to budget without getting burned.

The short answer

Here are the typical ranges we see across the market in 2026 for a small business website:

RouteTypical upfront costBest for
DIY builder (Wix, Squarespace)$0 to $500, plus your timeBrand-new businesses testing an idea
Freelancer using a template$500 to $2,500Simple brochure sites on a tight budget
Custom design by a small studio$2,500 to $10,000Established businesses that need leads
Agency build with strategy and content$10,000 to $50,000+Larger companies with complex needs

Most established small businesses (a coaching practice, a salon, a solar installer, a wedding photographer) land in the third row. That's the range where you get custom design, proper SEO foundations and someone thinking about conversion, without paying for a room full of account managers.

What actually drives the cost

Page count is the thing people fixate on, but it's rarely the biggest factor. These are.

1. Custom design vs a template

A template gives you someone else's layout with your logo dropped in. Custom design starts from your customers: what they need to see, in what order, to trust you and get in touch. Custom takes more hours, which is why it costs more, but it's also why it tends to convert better.

2. Functionality

A five-page site that only displays information is simple. The cost rises when the site has to do things:

  • Online booking or appointment requests
  • Payments, deposits or a full online store
  • Calculators, quizzes or quote builders
  • Member areas, courses or client portals
  • Integrations with your CRM, calendar or email platform

Each of these is a mini software project. A solar savings calculator, for example, can take longer to build than the rest of the site combined.

3. Content

Someone has to write the words and source the images. If you supply polished copy and professional photos, the build is faster. If your designer is also writing your copy, structuring your offers and directing photography, expect to pay for that time. It's usually worth it: content is what persuades.

4. SEO depth

Every decent build should include the basics: fast pages, clean structure, title tags, a sitemap. Deeper work costs extra: keyword research, location pages, schema markup, and migrating an old site without losing rankings. If you rely on Google for leads, read our technical SEO checklist before you sign anything.

5. Who you hire

A freelancer has low overheads. An agency has project managers, strategists and an office. A small founder-led studio sits in between: senior work without the layers. Rates also vary a lot by country, which is why a studio like ours can deliver agency-level work for clients in the US, UK and Australia at a lower price than a local agency.

The ongoing costs people forget

The build is a one-off. These are not:

  • Domain name: $10 to $20 a year for most .com names.
  • Hosting: $5 to $50 a month for a typical small business site. Builders like Wix and Squarespace bundle this into a $16 to $40 monthly plan.
  • Maintenance: $0 to $150 a month depending on the platform. WordPress sites need regular plugin and security updates. Hand-coded static sites need very little.
  • Email: around $6 to $12 per user per month for Google Workspace or Microsoft 365.
  • Tools: booking software, email marketing and CRM subscriptions, if you use them.

A fair all-in running cost for most small businesses is $20 to $100 a month. If you're being quoted $300 a month for "hosting" on a five-page site, ask exactly what's included. We cover this in detail in our website maintenance guide.

Three sample budgets

To make this concrete, here's how three common projects typically price out.

A one-page site for a new coach. A single long page with an about section, services, testimonials and a booking link. Expect $800 to $2,500 depending on design polish and whether copy is included.

A lead-generation site for a local service business. Eight to fifteen pages: home, about, a page per service, a few location pages, reviews, contact and a quote form. Expect $3,000 to $8,000 with proper SEO setup.

A booking or e-commerce site. A salon with online booking and deposits, or a store with 50 products and Stripe checkout. Expect $5,000 to $15,000 depending on the catalog and integrations.

How to avoid overpaying

  1. Get a fixed quote, in writing. Hourly billing puts all the risk on you. A fixed price for a defined scope puts it where it belongs.
  2. Ask what you'll own. You should get the domain, the code, the design files and admin access. If the answer is vague, walk away. More on this in do you really own your website?
  3. Check live work, not mockups. Anyone can make a pretty picture. Click through real sites they've launched and test them on your phone.
  4. Separate must-haves from nice-to-haves. Launch with what you need to win customers, then add features once the site is paying for itself.
  5. See something before you pay. A studio confident in its work will show you a direction first. That's why we offer a free website preview: we design your homepage on a live link before you commit to anything.

Is a cheap website a false economy?

Sometimes. A $300 website that brings in no enquiries costs more than a $5,000 website that brings in two new clients a month. The right question isn't "what's the cheapest site I can get?" but "what will this site be worth to my business?"

If one new client is worth $2,000 to you, a site that converts even one extra visitor a month pays for itself quickly. That's the lens to use.

Frequently asked questions

Can I build a website for free?

Yes. Builders like Wix and Google Sites have free plans, but they show ads, use a subdomain and limit features. They're fine for testing an idea, not for a business that wants to look established.

Why do quotes for the same website vary so much?

Because the scope behind them differs. One quote may be a template with your logo; another includes strategy, copywriting, custom design and SEO. Ask each provider to list exactly what's included and compare like for like.

Should I pay monthly or upfront?

Upfront builds usually cost less over three years and leave you owning the site. "Website as a service" monthly plans lower the barrier but often mean you're renting, and lose the site if you stop paying. Check the contract.
