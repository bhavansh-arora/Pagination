Key takeaways
  • Complete every field; incomplete profiles are outranked by complete ones
  • The primary category is the most important single choice
  • Fresh photos, posts and review replies signal an active business
  • Never stuff keywords into your business name

Your Google Business Profile is often the first thing a customer sees: before your website, before your shopfront. It's free, it takes an hour to do well, and most of your competitors have done it badly. Work through this checklist and you'll be ahead of most of them.

Setup

  • [ ] Claim or create your profile at google.com/business
  • [ ] Verify it (video, phone or postcard)
  • [ ] Add a second owner so you're never locked out

Core information

  • [ ] Business name: exactly as on your signage. No added keywords or locations.
  • [ ] Primary category: the most specific match ("Hair salon", not "Beauty")
  • [ ] Secondary categories: up to nine, only if they truly apply
  • [ ] Address or service area: hide your address if customers don't visit
  • [ ] Phone number: a local number that's answered
  • [ ] Website: your homepage, or the most relevant page for that location
  • [ ] Hours: including holidays and special hours
  • [ ] Appointment or booking link

Description

  • [ ] 750 characters; put the essentials in the first 250
  • [ ] What you do, who for and where
  • [ ] What makes you different
  • [ ] Natural language; no keyword lists

Services and products

  • [ ] Every service listed with a short description
  • [ ] Prices or "from" prices where possible
  • [ ] Products with photos if you sell them
  • [ ] Menu link for restaurants

Attributes

  • [ ] Accessibility, payment options, ownership attributes, amenities
  • [ ] Anything customers filter by: "wheelchair accessible", "online appointments"

Photos and video

  • [ ] Logo and cover photo
  • [ ] Exterior shots so people can find you
  • [ ] Interior shots
  • [ ] Team photos
  • [ ] Work examples: before and afters, finished jobs, dishes
  • [ ] Short videos
  • [ ] New photos added at least monthly

Businesses with plenty of recent, real photos get more clicks, calls and direction requests.

Reviews

  • [ ] Create your short review link
  • [ ] Ask every satisfied customer
  • [ ] Reply to every review, good or bad
  • [ ] Never offer rewards for reviews
  • [ ] Report fake reviews through the profile

Posts

  • [ ] One post every week or two: offers, news, events, new work
  • [ ] A photo and a call-to-action button on each

Questions and answers

  • [ ] Add your own common questions and answer them
  • [ ] Monitor for new questions

Messaging

  • [ ] Turn on only if you can reply quickly
  • [ ] Set a welcome message

Connect it to your website

  • [ ] Name, address and phone on your site match the profile exactly
  • [ ] Local business schema on your site (how)
  • [ ] Embed the Google map on your contact page
  • [ ] Link to your profile from your site and ask for reviews there

Track performance

  • [ ] Check the Performance tab monthly: searches, views, calls, direction requests
  • [ ] Add tracking parameters to your website link so visits show in analytics

What to avoid

MistakeWhy it hurts
Keywords in business nameRisk of suspension
Virtual office addressesAgainst guidelines
Duplicate profilesSplits reviews and confuses Google
Ignoring bad reviewsLooks careless to customers
Wrong hoursFrustrated customers, poor reviews

Keep it alive

A profile isn't a set-and-forget job. Ten minutes a week keeps it fresh. For the wider picture, see our local SEO guide.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to see results?

Changes can show within days, but meaningful ranking gains usually take a few weeks of consistent activity.

Can I list more than one location?

Yes, one profile per genuine, staffed location.

What should I do if my profile is suspended?

Review Google's guidelines, fix any violations (often the business name or address) and submit a reinstatement request with evidence.
