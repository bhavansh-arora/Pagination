How to Build a Life Coach Website That Books Discovery Calls

A step-by-step guide to building a life coach website that builds trust, explains your offer clearly and turns visitors into booked discovery calls.

Key takeaways
  • Your website's one job is to get the right person onto a discovery call
  • Lead with the client's problem and the change you help them make, not your certifications
  • Show your face, your story and real client results
  • Put a booking button in the header, the hero and at the end of every page

People don't hire a life coach the way they buy a product. They hire someone they trust with the parts of their life that aren't working. Your website has to earn that trust quickly, then make the next step (a free call) feel safe and easy.

We've built coaching sites for life, executive, wellness, career and relationship coaches in five countries. This is what the ones that book calls have in common.

Start with one goal

Almost every successful coaching site is built around a single action: book a free discovery call. Not "learn more", not "follow me on Instagram". One call to action, repeated consistently.

Before you design anything, decide what that call is named (First Step call, clarity call, chemistry session), how long it is, and how people book it.

The homepage, section by section

1. A headline about them

Weak: "Certified Life Coach and NLP Practitioner." Strong: "Feeling stuck? Find your footing and choose your path."

Your visitor is thinking about their own problem. Meet them there. Follow the headline with one sentence on who you help and how, and a booking button.

2. Your face

Coaching is personal. A warm, professional photo of you in the hero section does more for trust than any stock image of a sunrise.

3. The problem, in their words

Three or four lines that describe how your ideal client feels right now. When someone reads this and thinks "that's me", they keep scrolling.

4. The change you offer

What will be different after working with you? Be concrete: "set boundaries without guilt", "make the career move you've been postponing for two years".

5. How it works

Three simple steps: book a free call, choose a programme, start. Removing uncertainty about the process removes a major barrier.

6. Proof

Testimonials with full names and photos where clients allow it. Results are more persuasive than praise: "I left the job I'd hated for six years" beats "She's a wonderful coach." More in how to use testimonials on a coaching website.

7. About you, briefly

A short version of your story with a link to the full about page. Credentials belong here, as supporting evidence.

8. The call to action, again

End with a clear invitation and a booking button.

The other pages you need

  • About: your story, told in a way that shows you understand the client's.
  • Work with me: your programmes and packages. See how to write a coaching packages page.
  • Testimonials: a fuller collection of client stories.
  • Book a call: an embedded calendar, not a form that leads to email tennis.
  • Blog or resources: optional, but powerful for SEO and trust.

Make booking effortless

Embed a scheduler (Calendly, TidyCal, Acuity or Google Calendar's booking pages) directly on your site. Every extra step between "I'm interested" and "I'm booked" loses people. Ask only what you need: name, email and one question about what they'd like help with.

Mistakes we see on coaching websites

  • Leading with qualifications. Clients care about their outcome first, your training second.
  • Trying to help everyone. "I help people live their best life" speaks to nobody. See positioning your coaching niche.
  • No prices or even a hint. Mystery reads as expensive or evasive.
  • Walls of text. Short paragraphs, clear headings, generous space.
  • A hidden booking link. If it takes more than one click to find, it's hidden.
  • Slow, template-heavy pages. They look like every other coach's site.

Don't forget Google

Clients search for coaches: "career coach Vancouver", "life coach for women", "executive coach Auckland". A site with clear service pages, your location and helpful articles can bring in enquiries every month without ads. Start with SEO for coaches.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a website if I get clients from Instagram?

Yes. Social platforms are rented space, and serious prospects will look you up. A website is where they decide whether to book.

Should I put prices on my coaching website?

At least a starting price or range. It filters out poor fits and makes the discovery call more productive.

How many pages does a coaching website need?

Five is plenty to start: home, about, work with me, testimonials and booking. Add a blog when you're ready to invest in SEO.
