Website Copywriting for Small Businesses: Words That Win Customers

A practical guide to writing website copy for a small business: headlines, service pages, about pages, calls to action and editing tips that lift conversions.

Key takeaways
  • Write about the customer's problem and outcome before you write about yourself
  • Clear beats clever; use the words your customers use
  • Break text into short sections with meaningful headings
  • Every page should lead to one next step

Visitors don't read websites the way they read books. They scan, looking for a reason to stay or go. Good web copy makes the right things jump out: what you do, why it matters to them, why they should trust you and what to do next.

You don't need to be a writer. You need to be clear.

Start with the customer

Before writing, answer:

  • Who is this page for?
  • What problem brought them here?
  • What do they want to happen?
  • What might stop them choosing you?

Then write to one person, using "you".

Headlines

Your headline is the most-read line on the page. Make it specific.

VagueClear
Welcome to our websiteEmergency plumbing in Leeds, 24 hours a day
Quality you can trustKitchens designed, built and fitted in six weeks
Solutions for your businessBookkeeping for freelancers, from £49 a month

A good test: could this headline appear on a competitor's site? If yes, sharpen it.

Benefits before features

A feature is what something is. A benefit is what it does for the customer.

  • Feature: "24-panel 9.6 kW system"
  • Benefit: "Cut your electricity bill by up to 70%"

Lead with the benefit, support with the feature.

Use their words

Listen to how customers describe their needs in emails, reviews and calls. Use those phrases. If they say "leaky tap", don't write "faucet malfunction".

Structure for scanning

  • Short paragraphs: one to three sentences
  • Headings that summarise each section
  • Bullets for lists
  • Bold for the key phrase
  • Front-load: put the important words at the start of sentences and headings

Page by page

Homepage

Headline, who you help, services overview, proof, how it works, call to action. See homepage design best practices.

Service pages

  • Who it's for and the problem
  • What you do and what's included
  • How it works
  • Price guidance
  • Proof
  • FAQs
  • Call to action

About

Why you do this, who's behind the business and why customers can trust you. Tell it as a story with a point.

Contact

Reassure: how quickly you reply and what happens next.

Be specific

Specifics persuade. Generalities don't.

  • "Many happy customers" → "186 five-star Google reviews"
  • "Fast turnaround" → "Most sites live in two weeks"
  • "Experienced team" → "17 years installing solar in Southern California"

Cut the filler

Delete:

  • "We are passionate about"
  • "State-of-the-art"
  • "One-stop shop"
  • "Tailored solutions"
  • "Going the extra mile"

If a sentence could be removed without losing meaning, remove it.

Write like you talk

Read your copy aloud. If it sounds stiff, rewrite it the way you'd say it to a customer across a table.

Calls to action

Tell people what to do, and what they'll get. "Get my free quote" beats "Submit". More in call-to-action examples.

Writing for search

  • Include the phrases people search in titles, headings and opening paragraphs
  • Answer real questions
  • Don't stuff keywords; write naturally

See keyword research for small businesses.

Proof in words

Let customers do the talking. A short testimonial beside a claim makes it believable.

Editing checklist

  • [ ] Is the main point clear in five seconds?
  • [ ] Are there more "you"s than "we"s?
  • [ ] Is every claim specific or backed up?
  • [ ] Are paragraphs short?
  • [ ] Is there one clear next step?
  • [ ] Have I read it aloud?
  • [ ] Is it free of typos?

Using AI as a helper

AI tools can outline, suggest headlines and tidy grammar. Left alone, they produce generic copy that sounds like everyone else. Feed them your real customer language and edit hard.

Frequently asked questions

How much text should a page have?

Enough to answer the visitor's questions. Service pages usually need 400 to 900 words; homepages can be similar, broken into clear sections.

Should I hire a copywriter?

If writing isn't your strength, yes. Good copy pays for itself in conversions. Many web designers, including us, include copy support.

How often should I update copy?

Whenever your services, prices or proof change, and review everything at least yearly.
