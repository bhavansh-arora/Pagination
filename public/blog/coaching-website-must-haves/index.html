15 Must-Haves for a Coaching Website in 2026

The fifteen elements every coaching website needs to build trust and book clients, from a clear niche statement to embedded booking and real testimonials.

Key takeaways
  • Clarity about who you help beats clever design
  • Booking should be one click from anywhere on the site
  • Real photos and named testimonials build trust faster than anything else
  • A fast, mobile-friendly site is non-negotiable because most visitors arrive from social on their phone

Use this as a checklist against your current site. Score yourself honestly: most coaching websites we review are missing at least five.

1. A clear niche statement

Within five seconds a visitor should know who you help and with what. "Career transition coaching for tech professionals" tells the right person they're in the right place.

2. A client-focused headline

Speak to the feeling or goal your client has today. Save your title and credentials for the about section. For formulas and examples, see coaching website copywriting.

3. A professional photo of you

Not a selfie, not a stock image. A warm, well-lit portrait where you're looking at the camera.

4. One primary call to action

"Book a free call" in the header, the hero and the footer. Same wording each time.

5. Embedded online booking

A live calendar on your site. No "email me to arrange a time".

6. A signature method or framework

Naming your process (a four-step method, a framework with an acronym) makes your coaching tangible and memorable. Lasting Change Wellness, for example, builds its whole site around the S.H.I.N.E. method.

7. Clear programmes or packages

What's included, how long it lasts, who it's for. See how to write a coaching packages page.

8. Testimonials with names and results

Specific outcomes from real people. Video is even better.

9. Credentials and affiliations

ICF, EMCC, certifications, relevant career history. Shown as logos or a short list, supporting your story rather than leading it.

10. An about page that connects

Your story, framed around why you understand your clients. See how to write a coach about page.

11. A lead magnet

Most visitors aren't ready to book today. A useful free resource in exchange for an email keeps the conversation going. Ideas in 20 lead magnets for coaches.

12. Mobile-first design

Most coaching traffic comes from Instagram and LinkedIn links opened on a phone. Design for the small screen first.

13. Fast loading

A slow site loses people before they've read your headline. Aim for under two seconds on mobile.

14. SEO foundations

A page per service, your location if you coach locally, proper title tags and a few genuinely helpful articles. Start with SEO for coaches.

15. A privacy policy and clear contact details

You're collecting names and emails. A privacy policy is a legal requirement in most places, and visible contact details signal you're a real business.

Nice-to-haves once the basics are solid

  • Media and speaking page with logos of podcasts and publications you've appeared in
  • A quiz or self-assessment that qualifies leads and gives instant value
  • A client portal or resource library
  • Retreat or event pages with their own booking flows
  • Corporate or B2B page if you also sell to organisations

Frequently asked questions

What's the most important page on a coaching website?

The homepage, because it decides whether anyone sees the rest. After that, your booking page.

Do I need a blog?

Not on day one. It becomes valuable when you want steady traffic from Google and something to share on social.

Can I build a coaching website myself?

Yes, with a builder like Squarespace. The trade-off is time and a template look. If you'd like to compare, we'll design a free preview of a custom homepage for you.
