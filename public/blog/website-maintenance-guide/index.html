Website Maintenance: What It Is, What It Costs and What You Can Skip

What website maintenance actually involves, what it should cost for a small business, and which tasks you can do yourself in 30 minutes a month.

Key takeaways
  • Maintenance covers security, backups, updates, performance and content
  • Costs range from almost nothing for static sites to $50 to $300 a month for complex ones
  • WordPress needs the most upkeep because of plugins
  • A 30-minute monthly check catches most problems early

A website isn't finished at launch. Like a shop, it needs someone to sweep the floor, change the window display and make sure the lock works. The good news: for most small business sites, that's not much work, and you shouldn't be paying a fortune for it.

What maintenance actually covers

Security and updates

Any site built on software (WordPress, plugins, e-commerce platforms) needs regular updates to patch security holes. Out-of-date plugins are the most common way small business websites get hacked.

Backups

A complete copy of your site, taken automatically and stored somewhere other than the web server, so a mistake or attack can be undone in minutes.

Uptime and performance

Checking the site is online, loads quickly and that nothing has broken after an update.

Content

Keeping information accurate: hours, prices, team members, services, recent work. An out-of-date site quietly erodes trust.

Technical health

Fixing broken links, renewing the domain and SSL certificate, and watching Search Console for errors.

How much maintenance does your site need?

It depends heavily on how the site was built.

Site typeMaintenance needTypical monthly cost
Hand-coded static siteVery low$0 to $30
Wix / SquarespaceLow (platform handles it)Included in plan
WordPress with pluginsMedium to high$50 to $150
E-commerce storeHigh$100 to $300+
Custom web appHighQuoted individually

This is one reason we build many client sites as fast static sites: there's no database to hack and no plugins to update, so upkeep is close to zero.

What a care plan should include

If you pay someone monthly, you should be getting:

  • Software, theme and plugin updates, tested after each one
  • Daily or weekly off-site backups
  • Uptime and security monitoring
  • A set amount of time for small content changes
  • A short monthly report
  • A guaranteed response time when something breaks

If your "maintenance" fee is really just hosting with a markup, ask for the list above.

The 30-minute monthly check you can do yourself

  1. Open the site on your phone. Does everything look right?
  2. Submit your own contact form. Did the email arrive?
  3. Check Search Console for new errors. Our Search Console guide shows where to look.
  4. Run PageSpeed Insights on your homepage.
  5. Read your homepage as if you were a customer. Is anything out of date?
  6. Confirm a recent backup exists.
  7. Check your domain's renewal date.

Put it in your calendar for the first Monday of each month.

Signs your site is being neglected

  • Copyright year is out of date
  • "Latest news" is from three years ago
  • A browser warning says "Not secure"
  • The site is noticeably slower than it used to be
  • Forms stopped sending emails and nobody noticed
  • You find spammy links or pages you didn't create

Any of the last three needs attention today.

Should you maintain it yourself or pay someone?

Do it yourself if you're on a builder or a static site and comfortable editing text and images.

Pay someone if you're on WordPress or run a store, if the site generates real revenue, or if you simply don't want to think about it. An hour of your time is usually worth more than the monthly fee.

When maintenance costs more than it's worth

If you're spending hundreds a month patching an old, slow site, compare that with the cost of a rebuild on a lower-maintenance platform. It often pays for itself within a year. See 12 signs you need a new website.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a maintenance plan for a simple site?

Not always. A static or builder-based brochure site mainly needs occasional content updates and a renewed domain.

What happens if I don't update WordPress?

Sooner or later a known vulnerability gets exploited. Hacked sites can be flagged by Google and removed from search results until cleaned.

How often should I update my content?

Whenever something changes, and review everything at least twice a year. Adding fresh work, reviews or articles regularly also helps SEO.
