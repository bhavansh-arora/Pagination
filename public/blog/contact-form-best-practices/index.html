Contact Forms That Convert: 15 Best Practices

Fifteen contact form best practices for small business websites: fewer fields, clearer labels, mobile-friendly design, spam protection and better follow-up.

Key takeaways
  • Every extra field reduces completions; ask only what you need
  • Label the button with what the visitor gets
  • Reassure about privacy and response time next to the form
  • Test your own form regularly, because broken forms lose leads silently

The contact form is where interest becomes a lead. It's also where a surprising number of websites lose people: too many fields, vague buttons, forms that don't work on phones, or submissions that vanish into a spam folder.

These fifteen practices fix most of it.

1. Ask for less

Start with the minimum: name, email (or phone) and message. Add fields only if you'll genuinely use the answer to respond better.

2. Make optional fields optional

Mark them clearly. Don't force a phone number if email will do.

3. Use a single column

Easier to scan and far better on mobile.

4. Put labels above fields

Visible labels beat placeholder-only text, which disappears when someone starts typing and causes accessibility problems.

5. Use the right input types

Email fields bring up the email keyboard; phone fields bring up the number pad; dates get a date picker. Small things that make mobile completion much easier.

6. Enable autofill

Let browsers fill in name, email and phone. Fewer keystrokes, more submissions.

7. Write a specific button

"Send message", "Get my free quote", "Request a callback". Never just "Submit". See call-to-action examples.

8. Set expectations

Next to the form: "We reply within one business day." People are more willing to enquire when they know what happens next.

9. Reassure about privacy

"We'll never share your details." Link to your privacy policy.

10. Use multi-step for longer forms

If you need more information (quotes, applications), split it into steps with a progress indicator. Start with easy questions.

11. Show clear errors

Tell people exactly what's wrong, next to the field, as they go. Don't clear the form if there's a mistake.

12. Confirm success

After submission, show a clear thank-you message or page explaining next steps. Send a confirmation email too.

13. Stop spam without annoying humans

  • A hidden "honeypot" field bots fill in and humans don't see
  • Invisible verification services
  • Avoid image puzzles where possible

14. Offer alternatives

Beside the form, list phone, email and WhatsApp. Some people will never fill in a form. See adding WhatsApp to your website.

15. Test it regularly

Submit your own form monthly. Check the email arrives, and not in spam. A broken form can go unnoticed for weeks while leads disappear.

Where to put forms

  • A dedicated contact page
  • At the end of service pages
  • In the footer or a final section on the homepage
  • On landing pages, above the fold

Don't make interested visitors hunt for it.

Route submissions properly

  • Send to an inbox that's monitored
  • Add to your CRM automatically
  • Notify by SMS or app for urgent enquiries
  • Record the page the enquiry came from

Follow up fast

Speed of reply is one of the biggest factors in winning the business. Aim for minutes during working hours, and the same day at the latest.

Track conversions

Record form submissions as conversions in analytics so you know which pages and traffic sources generate leads. See how to get more leads from your website.

Accessibility

Labels, keyboard access, visible focus and clear errors make forms usable for everyone. See web accessibility basics.

Frequently asked questions

How many fields is too many?

For a first enquiry, more than five or six starts to hurt. Ask the rest later.

Should I require a phone number?

Only if a call is essential to your process. Otherwise make it optional.

Is a form better than showing my email address?

Offer both. Forms give structure; some people prefer to write directly.
