Wellness Coach Website Guide: Design, Pages and Booking

How to build a wellness or health coach website that feels calm and credible, explains your method and turns visitors into clients.

Key takeaways
  • Wellness clients need to feel safe and understood before they'll book
  • A named method and clear programme structure make an intangible service concrete
  • Be careful with health claims; promise support, not cures
  • Calm design still needs a strong, visible call to action

Wellness coaching covers a lot of ground: health, nutrition, stress, sleep, hormones, burnout, habits. What unites the clients is that they've often tried things before and feel discouraged. Your website's job is to make them feel understood, show them a believable path and make starting easy.

Set the tone

Wellness sites work best when they feel like a deep breath:

  • Colour: soft, natural palettes with one confident accent
  • Photography: real, warm, natural light; you, not stock models with salads
  • Space: generous margins and short paragraphs
  • Language: kind, clear and free of guilt

Calm doesn't mean vague. The booking button should still be the most obvious thing on the page.

Say who you help

"I help people feel better" won't land. Specific does:

  • "Coaching for women navigating perimenopause"
  • "Burnout recovery for healthcare professionals"
  • "Helping impact-driven women quiet self-doubt"

The narrower the statement, the more the right person feels seen. See positioning your coaching niche.

Make your method visible

Clients want to know there's a plan. Name your approach and show the steps. Lasting Change Wellness does this with the S.H.I.N.E. method: a simple acronym that turns an abstract service into a journey people can picture.

Pages to include

  • Home: who you help, the change you offer, proof and a call to action
  • About: your own wellness story and credentials
  • Programmes: clear options with what's included
  • Success stories
  • Resources or blog: recipes, guides, articles
  • Book a call

Optional: a quiz ("What's your stress type?"), a free challenge, an online course area.

Be careful with claims

Health is regulated, and trust is fragile. Protect both:

  • Don't promise to diagnose, treat or cure
  • Use phrases like "support", "guide" and "work towards"
  • Add a short disclaimer that coaching isn't a substitute for medical care
  • Be clear about your qualifications and scope
  • Only use testimonials that reflect typical, honest experiences

Credentials that matter

Display relevant ones: NBHWC board certification, health coaching diplomas, nutrition qualifications, a clinical background if you have one. A former psychologist turned wellness coach, for example, should say so prominently.

Lead magnets that fit wellness

  • A seven-day reset plan
  • An energy or symptom tracker
  • A simple meal-planning template
  • A guided breathing audio
  • A self-assessment quiz

Booking and intake

Offer a free introductory call with an embedded calendar. For wellness, a short intake question helps: "What would you most like to feel different three months from now?" Keep anything medical for a secure form after they've become a client.

Selling programmes online

If you sell a group programme or course, your site can take payment directly through Stripe or connect to a course platform. Make the path short: programme page, pay, welcome email with next steps.

SEO for wellness coaches

People search for help with specific struggles: "burnout coach", "health coach for PCOS", "stress management coaching". Build a page for each area you specialise in and write articles answering the questions clients ask. See SEO for coaches.

Frequently asked questions

Should I show prices on a wellness coaching site?

A starting price helps set expectations. Many wellness coaches share full pricing for programmes and discuss bespoke options on a call.

Can I sell supplements or products on my site?

Yes, with an online shop, but be careful about claims and check local regulations.

Do I need a blog?

It helps with SEO and gives you content for social. Recipes, practical guides and myth-busting articles tend to do well.
