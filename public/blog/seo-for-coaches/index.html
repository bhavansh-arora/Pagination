SEO for Coaches: How to Get Coaching Clients from Google

A practical SEO guide for coaches: the keywords clients actually search, the pages you need, local SEO, blogging and how to appear in AI search results.

Key takeaways
  • Clients search by niche and location, like "career coach Toronto", so build a page for each service
  • A Google Business Profile helps even if you coach online
  • Helpful articles answering client questions bring steady traffic
  • Clear, well-structured pages also help you appear in ChatGPT and Google AI answers

Most coaches rely on referrals and social media. Both are valuable, and both stop when you do. Search is different: a page that ranks keeps bringing in prospects month after month while you're busy coaching.

You don't need to become an SEO expert. You need to do a handful of things well.

How people search for coaches

Searches fall into three groups:

  • Service + location: "life coach Denver", "executive coach London"
  • Service + niche: "career coach for tech workers", "coach for women in leadership", "divorce coach online"
  • Problem-based: "how to change careers at 40", "feeling stuck in life what to do"

The first two are people ready to hire. The third are people earlier in the journey who may hire later.

Step 1: one page per service

If you offer life coaching, career coaching and corporate workshops, each needs its own page. One page can't rank for three different things.

On each page include:

  • The service and audience in the page title and main heading
  • Who it's for and the problems it solves
  • How it works
  • Testimonials specific to that service
  • FAQs
  • A booking call to action

Read title tags and meta descriptions to get the basics right.

Step 2: claim your location

Even if you work on Zoom, many clients prefer someone "local". Mention your city and region on your homepage, contact page and footer, and set up a Google Business Profile. You can hide your home address and list a service area. Our Google Business Profile checklist walks through it.

Then ask happy clients for Google reviews. They influence both rankings and trust.

Step 3: write what your clients are Googling

List the ten questions you hear most on discovery calls. Each is an article:

  • "How do I know if I need a career coach?"
  • "What happens in a life coaching session?"
  • "How much does executive coaching cost?"
  • "Life coach vs therapist: what's the difference?"

Answer each clearly in 800 to 1,500 words, with a call to action at the end. One good article a month is enough. See how to write a blog post that ranks.

Step 4: get the technical basics right

  • Speed: fast on mobile
  • Mobile-friendly: obviously
  • HTTPS: the padlock
  • Clear structure: one H1 per page, logical headings
  • Schema markup: tells Google you're a professional service with reviews and FAQs

Step 5: earn a few good links

Links from other sites tell Google you're trusted. Good sources for coaches:

  • Your certifying body's directory (ICF, EMCC)
  • Podcast guest appearances
  • Guest articles for industry publications
  • Local business associations
  • Collaborations with therapists, HR consultants and other coaches

Getting found in AI search

People now ask ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's AI Overviews for recommendations: "who's a good career coach for engineers in Vancouver?" These tools pull from clear, specific, well-structured web pages.

To be included: state plainly who you help, where and how; use question-style headings with direct answers; keep your details consistent across your site, LinkedIn and directories. We cover this in depth in how to get mentioned in AI search.

How long will it take?

Expect brand searches (your name) to work within weeks, niche and local terms within three to six months, and competitive terms longer. SEO compounds: the work you do this quarter keeps paying next year. More in how long does SEO take?

Frequently asked questions

Can I rank if I only coach online?

Yes. Target niche terms ("online career coach for nurses") and still claim your home city for local searches.

Is SEO worth it for a solo coach?

If one new client is worth $1,500 or more, even one extra enquiry a month from Google is a strong return.

Do I need to blog every week?

No. One genuinely helpful article a month beats four thin ones.
