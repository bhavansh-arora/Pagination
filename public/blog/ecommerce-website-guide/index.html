Building an E-commerce Website: Shopify vs Custom, and What You Need

How to build an e-commerce website for a small business: choosing between Shopify and custom, essential pages, product pages that sell, checkout and SEO.

Key takeaways
  • Shopify is the quickest route for most small stores; custom suits unusual needs or tight brand control
  • Product pages do the selling, so invest in photos, descriptions and reviews
  • A short, trustworthy checkout prevents abandoned carts
  • Plan for shipping, tax, returns and SEO before launch

Selling online has never been more accessible, and the choices have never been more confusing. Shopify, WooCommerce, Squarespace, custom builds. The right answer depends on what you sell, how many products you have and how much control you need.

Step 1: choose a platform

Choose Shopify if

  • You have many products or variants
  • You want inventory, shipping and tax handled out of the box
  • Non-technical staff will run the store

Choose custom if

  • You sell a small number of products or services
  • You need an unusual purchase flow (configurators, bookings with payment, subscriptions with custom logic)
  • Speed and distinctive design are a competitive advantage
  • You want to avoid monthly platform and app fees

A custom storefront with Stripe Checkout can be remarkably simple and fast for a focused catalog.

Step 2: essential pages

  • Homepage with bestsellers and clear categories
  • Category pages with filters
  • Product pages
  • Cart and checkout
  • About and brand story
  • Shipping and returns
  • Contact and FAQ
  • Privacy policy and terms

Step 3: product pages that sell

The product page is your salesperson.

  • Photos: several angles, in use, with zoom. Consistent style.
  • Title: clear and descriptive
  • Price and any savings
  • Description: benefits first, then specifications
  • Variants: size, colour, clearly selectable
  • Stock and delivery estimate
  • Reviews with photos
  • Trust: returns policy, secure payment, guarantees
  • A prominent "Add to cart" button
  • Related products

Write unique descriptions. Copying manufacturer text hurts SEO.

Step 4: a frictionless checkout

  • Guest checkout, no forced account
  • As few steps and fields as possible
  • Express payment: Apple Pay, Google Pay, Shop Pay, PayPal
  • Clear shipping costs early; surprise fees are the top cause of abandonment
  • Trust badges and security reassurance
  • Order summary always visible

See accepting payments on your website.

Step 5: shipping, tax and returns

Decide before launch:

  • Flat rate, free over a threshold, or calculated shipping
  • Which regions you ship to
  • How sales tax or VAT is handled
  • Your returns policy and who pays for returns

Publish all of it clearly.

Step 6: SEO for stores

  • Keyword-led category and product titles
  • Unique descriptions
  • Clean URLs
  • Product schema with price, availability and reviews (guide)
  • Fast pages and optimised images (image SEO)
  • Helpful content: buying guides, comparisons

Step 7: recover abandoned carts

  • Automated emails an hour and a day after abandonment
  • A small incentive on the second or third email
  • Clear, direct link back to the cart

Step 8: build trust

  • Reviews and ratings
  • Real contact details
  • Clear policies
  • Secure checkout
  • Professional design

After launch

  • Track conversion rate, average order value and abandonment
  • Collect reviews with post-purchase emails
  • Build an email list from day one
  • Test product pages and checkout continuously

Selling services or bookings

Not all e-commerce is physical products. Courses, programmes, deposits and appointments can all be sold online with the right flow. See online booking for small businesses.

Frequently asked questions

How much does an e-commerce website cost?

A Shopify store with a customised theme might cost $2,000 to $8,000 to set up. Custom stores typically start around $5,000 and rise with complexity.

Can I start small and grow?

Yes. Launch with your best products and expand. Choose a platform that can scale with you.

Do I need a blog on my store?

Helpful content brings organic traffic and builds trust. Buying guides are especially effective.
