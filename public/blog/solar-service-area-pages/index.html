Service Area Pages for Solar Installers (Without Thin Content)

How solar companies can build location pages that rank in every town they serve, with a content template that avoids duplicate, thin pages.

Key takeaways
  • One strong page per priority area beats dozens of near-duplicates
  • Each page needs genuinely local content: projects, reviews, conditions and incentives
  • Link area pages to service pages and case studies
  • Start with the five to ten areas that matter most

If you install solar across twenty towns but your website only mentions your head office, you're competing for one town's searches. Service area pages fix that, when they're done properly. Done lazily (the same paragraph with the town name swapped) they can drag your whole site down.

Why they work

Google tries to show local results for searches like "solar installers Galway". A page that's clearly about solar in Galway, with evidence you actually work there, is a strong match.

What makes a page "thin"

  • Identical text across pages with only the place name changed
  • No local detail
  • No unique images
  • No reason for a human to read it

Google's guidelines call these doorway pages. Avoid them.

The template for a strong area page

1. Headline and intro

"Solar Panel Installation in [Town]" plus two or three sentences on your experience there.

2. Local proof

  • Number of systems installed in the area
  • Two or three local projects with photos, system size and savings
  • Reviews from customers in that town

3. Local conditions

  • Typical sunlight hours and expected generation
  • Common roof types and housing stock
  • Local electricity provider and export arrangements
  • Planning or permit notes specific to the council or county

4. Incentives

National schemes plus anything regional. Link to your full incentives guide.

5. Services available

Short summaries linking to residential, commercial, battery and EV pages.

6. FAQs

Three or four questions specific to the area.

7. Call to action

A quote form with the town pre-filled.

Aim for 500 to 900 words that couldn't be pasted onto another town's page.

How many pages?

Start with five to ten: your base and the largest towns or counties you serve. Add more as you complete projects there and have something real to say. Quality first.

URL and linking structure

  • /areas/ index page listing every location
  • /areas/galway/ for each
  • Link from each service page to the relevant area pages and back
  • Link case studies to their area page

Clear structure helps both visitors and search engines. More in our internal linking guide.

Titles and meta

  • Title: "Solar Panel Installers in Galway | [Company]"
  • Meta description: mention the area, a proof point and the quote offer

See title tags and meta descriptions.

Schema

Add LocalBusiness schema with areaServed listing your locations, plus FAQ schema on each area page. See our schema markup guide.

Keep them alive

Every time you complete an install in an area, add the photo and a line to that page. Fresh, real content keeps pages ranking and makes them more persuasive.

Area pages and your Google Business Profile

Your profile's service areas should match the areas on your site. Consistency reinforces relevance. See the Google Business Profile checklist.

Frequently asked questions

Can I create pages for towns where I haven't installed yet?

Yes, if you genuinely serve them and can write useful local content. Be honest about experience.

Will many similar pages hurt my site?

Near-duplicates can. Unique, useful pages won't.

Do I need a physical office in each area?

No. Service area pages are designed for businesses that travel to customers.
