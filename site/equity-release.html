How it works

Your home, your choice.

The most common form of equity release is a lifetime mortgage: a loan secured on your home that’s usually repaid when the last borrower dies or moves into long-term care.

  • Stay in your home for life and keep 100% ownership
  • Tax-free lump sum, drawdown facility, or both
  • Optional monthly interest payments to limit roll-up
  • No-negative-equity guarantee on plans meeting Equity Release Council standards
  • Protect part of your property’s value as an inheritance

People use it to help children onto the ladder, clear an interest-only mortgage, adapt their home, or simply enjoy retirement more.

Things to consider

Good advice means the full picture.

Compound interest

If you don’t make payments, interest rolls up and the amount owed grows over time.

Inheritance

Equity release reduces the value of your estate. We encourage you to involve your family in the conversation.

Benefits

Receiving a lump sum could affect your entitlement to means-tested state benefits.

Alternatives

Downsizing, a retirement interest-only mortgage or family help may suit you better. We’ll look at all of them.

Early repayment

Repaying early can carry significant charges, although many plans now include downsizing protection.

Legal advice

You’ll need an independent solicitor, and that safeguard is built into the process.

A lifetime mortgage is a loan secured against your home. It could accumulate interest and reduce the value of your estate. Check that this mortgage will meet your needs if you want to move or sell your home, or you want your family to inherit it. If you are in any doubt, seek independent advice.
Questions

Equity release FAQs

Will I still own my home?

Yes. With a lifetime mortgage you keep full ownership of your home and can live there for the rest of your life.

Can I leave an inheritance?

Many plans let you ring-fence a percentage of your property’s value as a guaranteed inheritance.

Can I move house later?

Most modern plans are portable to a new property, as long as it meets the lender’s criteria.

What does your advice cost?

Our specialist equity release advice fee is typically £995, and we’ll confirm it at your first appointment, which is free.
